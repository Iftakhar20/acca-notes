E1 The Scope of Corporation Tax

Key Definitions

TermDefinition
Period of AccountAny period for which a company draws up accounts (can be up to 18 months for a new company). NOT necessarily equal to 12 months.
Accounting Period (AP)The period for which CT is charged. Maximum 12 months. If accounts > 12 months, split into two APs: first 12 months + remainder.
Financial Year (FY)Runs 1 April to 31 March. FY2025 = 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026. Tax rates are set per FY.

When Does an Accounting Period Start?

  • When a company comes within the charge to CT (e.g., starts trading)
  • Immediately after the end of the previous AP
  • When it acquires a source of income after a dormant period

When Does an Accounting Period End?

  • 12 months after it started
  • At the end of the company's period of account (e.g., year-end)
  • When the company starts/stops trading
  • When the company goes into liquidation
⚠️ Exam Trap If a company prepares accounts for 15 months (say 1 Jan 2025 to 31 Mar 2026), this creates two APs: AP1 = 1 Jan 2025 to 31 Dec 2025 (12 months) and AP2 = 1 Jan 2026 to 31 Mar 2026 (3 months). Profits must be time-apportioned between the two APs.

Residence

A company is UK resident if:

  • Incorporated in the UK (automatic residency), or
  • Its central management and control (board decisions) is exercised in the UK (common law test)

UK-resident companies pay CT on worldwide profits. Non-resident companies only pay CT on UK-source trading profits through a UK permanent establishment.

✅ Excluded from TX syllabus Investment companies, close companies, reorganisations, personal service companies — you will NOT be tested on these.

E2a Tax-Adjusted Trading Profit

Start with accounting profit, then make tax adjustments. The rules are broadly the same as for a sole trader, but with some differences for companies.

Adjustments to Accounting Profit

Add Back (Disallow):

  • Depreciation and amortisation (replaced by capital allowances)
  • Capital expenditure incorrectly charged to P&L
  • Entertaining customers (UK or overseas — both disallowed)
  • Fines and penalties
  • Political donations
  • Excessive salary to a shareholder-director (if not arm's length)
  • Non-trade losses

Deduct (Allow):

  • Capital allowances (added as a separate deduction)
  • Pre-trading expenditure (incurred within 7 years before trade starts — treated as incurred on day 1 of trading)
  • Employee entertaining — fully deductible
  • Staff costs, rent, rates, insurance — all allowable
  • Legal fees relating to revenue matters (e.g., debt collection, renewing short leases)
⚠️ Common Mistakes Legal fees for acquiring capital assets = disallowed. Legal fees for renewing a short lease = allowed. Entertaining staff = allowed. Entertaining customers = always disallowed.

E2b Capital Allowances for Companies

Companies claim capital allowances instead of accounting depreciation. The rules mirror income tax, with one important addition: the Full Expensing (100% FYA) on main pool assets and 50% FYA on special rate pool assets for new assets only.

ℹ️ Full Expensing (FE) — Key Rule Full expensing (100% FYA) replaced the super-deduction from April 2023. When an asset on which FE was claimed is sold, a 100% balancing charge applies (i.e., proceeds are added back in full — there is no pool balance to net off against).

Short Accounting Periods

AIA limit and WDA percentages are time-apportioned for APs shorter than 12 months. For example, for a 9-month AP:

  • AIA limit = £1,000,000 × 9/12 = £750,000
  • WDA main pool = 18% × 9/12 = 13.5%

E2c Property Business Profits

Companies compute property business profits the same way as income tax: rental income less allowable expenses (mortgage interest is fully deductible for companies — there is NO finance cost restriction that applies to individuals).

  • Accruals basis applies
  • All UK property income pooled together
  • Capital allowances available on fixtures in commercial property
  • Furnished holiday lettings rules do NOT apply for CT (they applied for IT only and were abolished from April 2025 anyway)

Property Business Loss

A property business loss is carried forward and set against future property income only. It cannot be surrendered as group relief or offset against general profits.

⚠️ Exam Key Point For companies, loan interest on property borrowing = fully deductible (loan relationships). For individuals, restricted to 20% basic rate relief. Examiner often tests this difference.

E2d Loan Relationship Rules

Companies do not simply deduct interest paid and credit interest received through trading profit. Instead, the loan relationship rules apply:

TypeTreatmentIncluded in TTP as
Interest paid on a trading loan (e.g., overdraft for the business)Trading loan relationship — debitDeducted from trading profit
Interest paid on a non-trading loan (e.g., to buy investments)Non-trading loan relationship — debitDeducted from TTP as a separate item
Interest received on cash deposits, intercompany loans, bondsNon-trading loan relationship — creditAdded to TTP as a separate income item
✅ Simple Rule for TX In most TX exam questions, the examiner will tell you whether interest is "trading" or not. If it's bank interest received — always add to TTP. If it's bank loan interest on the trade — deduct in trading profit. Interest on investment loans — deduct separately.

E2e Qualifying Charitable Donations

  • Cash donations by a company to UK-registered charities = qualifying charitable donations (QCDs)
  • Deducted from Taxable Total Profits (after trading profit, property income, gains, loan relationships are all added)
  • Cannot create or increase a loss — if TTP is insufficient, the excess is simply lost
  • No grossing up required (unlike Gift Aid for individuals)
⚠️ Note QCDs are deducted after TTP is arrived at — not within trading profit. Loss relief claims are set off before QCDs. QCDs cannot be carried back or forward.

E2g Trading Losses — Companies

Option 1: Carry Forward

  • Carried forward indefinitely against total profits (post April 2017 losses) — company can choose how much to relieve
  • Pre-April 2017 losses: only against future trading profits of the same trade
  • Exam tip: assume losses are post-April 2017 unless stated otherwise (and the syllabus excludes the £5m restriction)

Option 2: Claim Against Current Period Total Profits

  • Current year claim: set against total profits of the AP in which the loss arose
  • Must be claimed within 2 years of the end of the loss-making AP
  • Relief is for the full loss — cannot claim part (but QCDs may then be wasted)

Option 3: Carry Back Against Previous Period

  • After a current year claim, can carry back up to 12 months before the start of the loss AP
  • Set against total profits of the earlier AP

Option 4: Group Relief

Losses can be surrendered to group members (see Section E5).

OptionAgainst What?TimeframeFlexibility?
Carry forwardFuture total profitsIndefiniteYes — choose amount
Current yearCurrent period total profitsSame APNo — all or nothing
Carry backPrior period total profits12 months backNo — all or nothing
Group reliefGroup member's total profitsSame APYes — choose amount

Factors Influencing Choice of Relief

  • Speed of relief (carry back = fastest cash repayment)
  • Marginal rate: if carry back offsets 25% CT vs carry forward at 19%, prefer carry back
  • Impact on QCDs: current year/carry back claim = QCDs may be wasted
  • Cash flow needs of the company

E3a Chargeable Gains for Companies

Companies do not pay CGT — chargeable gains are included in TTP and charged to corporation tax at the same rate as other profits.

Basic Gain Computation

Proceeds (or market value)X
Less: Incidental costs of disposal(X)
Less: Cost of acquisition(X)
Less: Enhancement expenditure(X)
Less: Indexation allowance(X)
Chargeable gainX
ℹ️ Key Differences — Company Gains vs Individual CGT
  • No annual exempt amount for companies
  • Indexation allowance available (not available to individuals after Dec 2017)
  • No entrepreneurs' relief / BADR
  • Gains taxed at CT rates (19% or 25%), not CGT rates
  • No 30-day reporting requirement

Capital Losses

  • Capital losses can only be set against chargeable gains — NOT against trading profits
  • Current year losses are automatically set against gains of the same period
  • Excess losses carried forward against future chargeable gains
  • Cannot be carried back

E3b Indexation Allowance

Indexation allowance compensates companies for the effect of inflation on the cost of assets. It uses the Retail Prices Index (RPI).

Indexation Allowance = Cost × Indexation Factor

The indexation factor is given in the exam question — you do not need to calculate it yourself. It is frozen at December 2017 values for disposals from January 2018 onwards (but still applies up to December 2017).

✅ Key Rules
  • The indexation allowance can reduce a gain to nil but CANNOT create or increase a loss
  • Available on cost and enhancement expenditure (not on disposal costs)
  • For disposals from January 2018: indexation factor is frozen at December 2017
  • The exam question will give you the factor — apply it to cost
📝 Example — Gain with Indexation

Petra Ltd sold a commercial property in December 2025:

  • Sale proceeds: £850,000
  • Original cost (March 2010): £400,000
  • Enhancement (June 2015): £50,000
  • Indexation factor on original cost (March 2010 → December 2017): 0.261
  • Indexation factor on enhancement (June 2015 → December 2017): 0.062
Proceeds850,000
Less: Cost(400,000)
Less: Enhancement(50,000)
Less: Indexation on cost (£400,000 × 0.261)(104,400)
Less: Indexation on enhancement (£50,000 × 0.062)(3,100)
Chargeable gain292,500

E3c Share Disposals by Companies

Companies use a share pool (also called the s.104 pool) to track share costs, with separate matching rules that differ from individuals.

Matching Order for Companies

  1. Same day acquisitions (shares bought on same day as disposal)
  2. Acquisitions within the following 9 days (FIFO basis)
  3. The share pool (all remaining shares — average cost)
⚠️ Difference from Individuals Individuals match with: same day → previous 30 days (LIFO) → share pool. Companies use 9 days forward matching (not 30). Companies do NOT have a bed-and-breakfasting 30-day rule.

The Share Pool

The share pool is a running account of number of shares and their total cost. Each new purchase adds to both. Each disposal reduces both proportionately. Indexation is calculated on the indexed cost column up to December 2017 (then frozen).

Bonus Issues & Rights Issues

  • Bonus issue: Additional free shares. No cost — just increase number of shares in pool. The cost per share falls.
  • Rights issue: Additional shares bought at preferential price. Add both shares and cost to the pool. Run indexation up to date of rights issue first.
  • Takeovers/reorganisations: If shares are exchanged for shares + cash (or only shares), apportion original cost over new holding based on market values. No gain on a share-for-share exchange unless cash received.

E3d Rollover Relief — Companies

Rollover relief defers a chargeable gain when the sale proceeds from a qualifying business asset are reinvested in another qualifying asset.

Qualifying Assets

  • Land and buildings (used in the trade)
  • Fixed plant and machinery (not moveable)
  • Goodwill (for unincorporated businesses — NOT for companies from 2002)
  • Ships, aircraft, hovercraft, satellites

Conditions

  • New asset must be acquired within 1 year before or 3 years after the disposal
  • Both old and new assets must be qualifying business assets
  • Company must use both assets in the trade

Amount of Relief

Gain deferred = lower of: gain OR (proceeds reinvested − original cost)

If proceeds are not fully reinvested, the gain deferred is reduced. The part of proceeds NOT reinvested is immediately chargeable.

📝 Example — Partial Rollover

Crescent Ltd sells a factory for £600,000, making a gain of £180,000. It buys a new factory for £550,000 within 3 years.

Proceeds600,000
Amount not reinvested (£600,000 − £550,000)50,000
Immediately chargeable (lower of £50,000 and £180,000)50,000
Gain deferred (rolled into cost of new factory)130,000
New factory cost for CT purposes: £550,000 − £130,000 =420,000

Marginal Relief Formula

MR = (Upper Limit − Augmented Profits) × 3/200 × TTP / Augmented Profits
  • Augmented profits = TTP + exempt dividends from non-group companies
  • In most exam questions, augmented profits = TTP (if no exempt dividends)
  • Upper limit = £250,000; Lower limit = £50,000
⚠️ Associated Companies — Limits Divided The £50,000 and £250,000 limits are divided by the number of associated companies + 1. Two associated companies = divide limits by 3. The examiner may tell you "Apex Ltd has 2 associated companies" — you MUST divide limits accordingly.

What counts as associated? A company is associated if one controls the other, or both are under common control. Dormant companies are excluded.
📝 Example — CT Liability with Marginal Relief

Nova Ltd has TTP of £180,000 for the year ended 31 March 2026. It has one associated company.

Lower limit = £50,000 / 2 = £25,000 | Upper limit = £250,000 / 2 = £125,000

Step 2: Determine rate
TTP = £180,000 > £125,000 → Main rate applies → 25% (no marginal relief needed)

Quarterly Instalments (Large Companies)

A company is large if its augmented profits exceed £1,500,000 (divided by number of associated companies + 1). Large companies must pay CT in quarterly instalments based on estimated profits.

ℹ️ TX Exam Tip The examiner rarely tests the detailed mechanics of quarterly instalments — just know the threshold (£1,500,000) and the general timing. Most questions test the 9 months + 1 day deadline.

Interest on Late/Early Payment

  • Interest on underpaid CT: 8.5% per annum
  • Interest on overpaid CT (repayment supplement): 3.5% per annum

E5 Groups of Companies

75% Group — Group Relief

A 75% group exists when a parent company owns (directly or indirectly) at least 75% of the ordinary share capital of another company AND is entitled to at least 75% of distributable profits and net assets on winding up.

What can be surrendered?

  • Trading losses (current period only — NOT carry-forward losses for group relief)
  • Non-trade loan relationship deficits
  • Excess qualifying charitable donations
  • Excess property business losses
  • Capital allowances in excess of income

Key Rules

  • Losses can be surrendered to any group member for the overlapping period of membership
  • The surrendering company gets no benefit — claimant pays nothing (or a payment up to tax saved at 25% rate)
  • Group relief is not available for losses carried forward (post-2017 carry-forward losses)
  • Claimant company deducts group relief after its own losses
📝 Example — Group Relief

Apex Ltd (parent) owns 80% of Base Ltd. Year ended 31 March 2026:

  • Apex Ltd: TTP = £400,000
  • Base Ltd: Trading loss = £80,000

Base Ltd surrenders £80,000 loss to Apex Ltd:

Apex Ltd TTP400,000
Less: Group relief from Base Ltd(80,000)
Taxable TTP320,000
CT @ 25%80,000
CT liability80,000

Base Ltd: has no CT liability (loss used). Limits for associated companies: £50,000 / 2 = £25,000 lower; £250,000 / 2 = £125,000 upper. TTP of £320,000 > £125,000 → main rate 25%.

75% Chargeable Gains Group

The same 75% test applies, PLUS the parent must have an effective economic interest of more than 50% in the subsidiary (i.e., 75% × 75% = 56.25% minimum if going through intermediate companies).

Reliefs available in a gains group:

  • No gain/no loss transfer: Assets can be transferred between group members with no CT charge on the gain (gain deferred until asset leaves the group)
  • Gains and losses matching: Capital gains can be offset against capital losses of group members by election
⚠️ Degrouping Charge If a company leaves the group within 6 years of receiving an asset via a no gain/no loss transfer, a degrouping charge applies — the deferred gain is triggered. This is an excluded topic in TX — just know the basic rule exists.

✅ Last-Minute Exam Tips
  • Always show your TTP proforma — even if some figures are zero. Marks are given for structure.
  • Remember to divide limits by associated companies + 1 before determining the rate.
  • If augmented profits ≠ TTP (due to exempt dividends), use augmented profits for limit comparison but TTP for rate calculation.
  • Indexation allowance cannot create a loss — state this if indexation exceeds unindexed gain.
  • QCDs cannot be carried forward or back — if TTP is insufficient, excess is lost.
  • Capital losses are ring-fenced — only against capital gains, not trading profit.
  • Group relief: surrendering company's losses reduce claimant's TTP — no payment required (unless agreed commercially up to tax saving value).
