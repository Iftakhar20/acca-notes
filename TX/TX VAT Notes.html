How VAT Works — The Big Picture

VAT is a consumption tax on the supply of goods and services in the UK. The key idea is that businesses collect VAT on behalf of HMRC — they charge it on their sales (output tax) and reclaim it on their purchases (input tax). Only the difference is paid over to HMRC.

VAT payable to HMRC = Output Tax (on sales) − Input Tax (on purchases) If Output Tax > Input Tax → PAY to HMRC If Input Tax > Output Tax → RECLAIM from HMRC (repayment trader)
The Golden Rule
VAT is ultimately borne by the end consumer. Registered businesses are merely the tax collector — they pass on the VAT they collect, net of what they paid on inputs.

Syllabus F1 — Registration Requirements

VAT Registration

Compulsory Registration — Historic Test

This is the most tested registration scenario. You check cumulatively whether taxable turnover in any rolling 12-month period has exceeded £90,000.

1
At the end of each month, add up the taxable turnover for the past 12 months.
2
If the total exceeds £90,000, the threshold is breached at the end of that month.
3
Notify HMRC within 30 days of the end of the month in which the limit was exceeded.
4
VAT registration takes effect from the 1st day of the 2nd month after the month in which the limit was exceeded.
Worked Example — Historic Test

Frances' monthly taxable supplies: Nov 25 = £16k, Dec 25 = £21k, Jan 26 = £31k, and 2/3 × Feb 26 = £23k. Running total hits £90,667 on 30 November 2025.

Threshold exceeded:30 November 2025
Notify HMRC by:30 December 2025 (30 days from end of Nov)
Registered from:1 January 2026 (1st day of 2nd month)
Exam Trap!
The threshold is breached at the end of the month (e.g. 30 November), NOT on the specific day during the month when the cumulative total crossed £90,000. The registration date is always the 1st of the SECOND month after. Don't confuse the notification deadline with the registration date.

Compulsory Registration — Future Test

If you have reasonable grounds to believe that your taxable turnover in the next 30 days alone will exceed £90,000:

  • Notify HMRC before those 30 days are up.
  • Registration takes effect from the beginning of that 30-day period.
Key Contrast with Historic Test
Under the future test, registration is effective immediately (start of the 30-day period), not the 1st of the following month. This is a common exam distinction.

Voluntary Registration

A business can voluntarily register even if below the £90,000 threshold. This is beneficial when:

  • Making zero-rated supplies — you can reclaim input VAT without having to charge output VAT to customers.
  • Selling to VAT-registered businesses — those businesses can recover the VAT you charge, so the additional cost doesn't put off customers.

Voluntary registration is NOT beneficial if:

  • You sell to unregistered consumers (e.g. members of the public) — you'd be charging them an extra 20% they can't reclaim.
  • You buy zero-rated supplies — you have no input VAT to recover anyway.

Deregistration

Compulsory deregistration: If you cease making taxable supplies, notify HMRC within 30 days of cessation. Deregistration is effective from the date of cessation (or a later agreed date).

Voluntary deregistration: If you reasonably expect taxable turnover in the next 12 months to fall below £88,000 (the deregistration limit), you may apply to deregister.

Output VAT on Deregistration

On deregistration, you must account for output tax on the value of business assets (non-current assets and inventory) on which input tax was previously claimed — unless the total VAT liability arising is £1,000 or less.

Common Exam Trap on Deregistration
The threshold below which output VAT on deregistration assets is waived is £1,000 — not £88,000 (that's the deregistration turnover limit). This distinction has been specifically tested.

Pre-Registration Input VAT

When you first register, you can reclaim input VAT incurred before registration, subject to strict rules:

Type of InputReclaim PeriodAdditional Condition
GoodsPurchased up to 4 years before registrationMust still be held (on hand) at registration date
ServicesSupplied up to 6 months before registrationMust have been for business purposes
Cars (private use)✗ Cannot reclaimNormal input VAT block applies
Worked Example — Pre-Registration Input VAT

Ardent Ltd registered for VAT on 1 January 2026. It had incurred advertising costs of £120/month in input VAT from April 2025, and purchased laptops for £200 input VAT which it still uses.

Advertising: 1 July 2025 – 31 Dec 2025 (6 months × £120)£720
Advertising: April – June 2025 (beyond 6 months)£0
Laptops (still held at registration)£200
Total pre-registration input VAT recoverable£920

Types of Supply

CategoryOutput VAT Charged?Input VAT Recoverable?Examples

Zero-Rated vs Exempt — The Critical Distinction
Both result in no VAT charged to the customer. But zero-rated is still a taxable supply, so input VAT is fully recoverable. Exempt means input VAT is blocked. A trader making only exempt supplies cannot register for VAT at all.

Practical Impact for Exam Questions

When you see a question about whether voluntary registration is beneficial:

  • Zero-rated seller + standard-rated purchases → Voluntary registration = excellent! Reclaim input VAT, no output VAT to charge.
  • Exempt seller → Cannot register; cannot reclaim input VAT.
  • Standard-rated seller to public → Registering adds 20% to prices, hurting competitiveness.
Syllabus F2(c)

Tax Points — When Does VAT Arise?

The tax point determines which VAT return period a transaction falls into. Getting this wrong is a common mistake.

Basic Tax Point (BTP)

  • Goods: Date the goods are delivered / made available
  • Services: Date the service is completed

Actual Tax Point (ATP) — Overrides the BTP

The BTP is overridden if either of these happen:

  1. Before BTP: An invoice is issued before the BTP → invoice date is the ATP
  2. Before BTP: Payment is received before the BTP → payment date is the ATP
  3. After BTP: Invoice issued within 14 days of the BTP → invoice date becomes the ATP
The 14-Day Rule
An invoice issued within 14 days after the BTP makes the invoice date the actual tax point — provided no actual tax point has already arisen (e.g. from a deposit payment before the BTP).
Worked Example — Tax Point Timeline

Equipment delivered on 23 December 2025. Invoice raised on 3 January 2026. A 10% deposit was paid on 28 September 2025.

TransactionTax PointVAT Return Period
Deposit (28 Sep 2025)28 Sep 2025 — payment before BTPQuarter ended 30 Sep 2025
Balance (delivery 23 Dec, invoice 3 Jan)3 January 2026 — invoice within 14 days of BTPQuarter ended 31 Mar 2026
Exam Trap!
Without the deposit, the balance invoice on 3 Jan 2026 would make 3 Jan the tax point (invoice within 14 days). But the invoice date is after the BTP (23 Dec), so it belongs in the Q1 2026 return, NOT the Q4 2025 return.
Services Example — Room Hire

A room hired on 15 January 2026. 25% deposit paid on 20 December 2025. Invoice for balance issued on 20 January 2026.

  • BTP = 15 January (date service performed)
  • Deposit ATP = 20 December (payment before BTP)
  • Balance ATP = 20 January (invoice within 14 days of BTP)
Syllabus F2(a)(b)(e)(g)(h)

Computing VAT Liability

Basic Computation Structure

OUTPUT TAX: Standard-rated sales × 20% £X Goods taken for personal use (replacement value × 20%) £X Fuel scale charge (private use) £X ──── Total Output VAT £X INPUT TAX: Standard-rated purchases × 20% £X Less: Blocked input VAT (see below) (£X) ──── Net Input VAT recoverable £X VAT payable/(repayable) = Output Tax − Input Tax

Key Adjustments to Output VAT

1. Prompt Payment Discounts

Output VAT is calculated on the actual amount paid. If a prompt payment discount is offered and taken, VAT is calculated on the discounted price, even if the invoice shows the full price.

Example — Prompt Payment Discount

Invoice for £500 (ex-VAT). 5% discount offered and taken by customer. Output VAT = £475 × 20% = £95 (not £500 × 20%).

2. Goods Removed for Personal Use

If a business owner takes goods from the business for personal use, output VAT must be charged on the replacement value (cost price), NOT the selling price.

Example — Goods for Personal Use

Kristel takes ladies' clothes from stock. Replacement value = £90, selling price = £125. Output VAT = £90 × 20% = £18.

Non-Deductible (Blocked) Input VAT

Certain categories of input VAT cannot be reclaimed, regardless of how much business use there is:

ItemRule
Cars (any private use)Input VAT is completely blocked if the car is used partly for private purposes. Only 100% business use (e.g. pool car, taxi) allows recovery.
Business EntertainmentInput VAT on entertaining UK customers is blocked. Exceptions: staff entertainment and entertaining overseas customers — these ARE recoverable.
Car Leasing (private use)Only 50% of input VAT is recoverable on a leased car used partly privately (regardless of the actual private use split).
Fuel for private useInput VAT on fuel CAN be reclaimed in full. However, output VAT must be charged using the fuel scale charge to account for the private element.
Worked Example — Cars and Fuel

A business owns a car used 60% for business. In Q1 it incurred: Car repair invoices £456 (inc. VAT); fuel £624 (inc. VAT). Fuel scale charge for the quarter = £380 (inc. VAT).

Input VAT on repairs (£456 × 1/6)£76
Input VAT on fuel (£624 × 1/6) — full amount reclaimed£104
Input VAT on car purchase£0 — blocked (private use)
Total input VAT recoverable£180
Output VAT — fuel scale charge (£380 × 1/6)£63

The fuel input VAT is reclaimed in full despite private use — but the scale charge creates output VAT to compensate.

Relief for Impairment Losses (Bad Debt Relief)

If a customer doesn't pay, you can reclaim the output VAT you already paid to HMRC — but only when all these conditions are met:

  • At least 6 months have elapsed since the debt was due for payment
  • The debt has been written off in the VAT account
  • The debt has not been sold to a debt collection agency
Reclaim Amount
You reclaim the VAT fraction of the outstanding balance. For a £800 net debt, the VAT component is £800 × 1/6 = £133. If the liquidator has paid anything, reduce the debt first.

Valuation of Supplies — Key Rates

To extract VAT from a VAT-inclusive price: VAT = VAT-inclusive price × 1/6 (same as × 20/120) To add VAT to a net (ex-VAT) price: VAT = Net price × 20% To find the net price from a gross price: Net = Gross × 100/120 (same as ÷ 1.2)
Syllabus F2(d)

VAT Invoices

Full VAT Invoice — Required Contents

A full VAT invoice must show:

  1. Unique, sequential invoice number
  2. Supplier's name, address and VAT registration number
  3. Date of supply (tax point)
  4. Date of issue of invoice (if different from date of supply)
  5. Description of goods or services
  6. For each VAT rate: the net amount, VAT rate, and VAT amount
  7. Total amount payable (inclusive of VAT)
  8. Customer's name and address
What is NOT required on a full VAT invoice
The customer's VAT registration number is NOT a mandatory requirement. This surprises many students. Also, the date of delivery is not automatically required (though it often coincides with the tax point which is required).

Simplified (Less Detailed) VAT Invoice

Can be issued when the total consideration is less than £250 (including VAT). It must show:

  • Supplier's name, address, and VAT number
  • Date of supply
  • Description of goods or services
  • Total amount payable (inc. VAT)
  • VAT rate applicable

A simplified invoice does NOT need to show the amount of VAT separately. (The total is shown but not the VAT split.)

Exam Trap — Simplified Invoice Threshold
The threshold for simplified invoices is £250 (VAT-inclusive), NOT £1,000. Questions deliberately offer £1,000 as a distractor. Always go with £250.

When Must a VAT Invoice Be Issued?

  • Mandatory when making a taxable supply to a VAT-registered customer (standard or zero rated)
  • Not required for exempt supplies
  • Not required for sales to non-VAT-registered customers (though can be issued)
  • Must be issued within 30 days of the date of supply

VAT Records Retention

VAT records (including VAT invoices) must be retained for 6 years.

Syllabus F2(b) — Administration

VAT Returns & Administration

Filing and Payment Deadlines

The standard VAT return covers a quarter (3 months). Both the return and the payment are due:

Deadline = 1 month and 7 days after the end of the quarter Example: Quarter ended 31 March 2026 → Deadline = 7 May 2026

All VAT returns must be filed electronically and all payments made electronically. There is no option to file on paper.

Making Tax Digital (MTD)

From 1 April 2025, all VAT-registered businesses must use MTD — keeping digital records and submitting returns via MTD-compatible software. This applies whether or not turnover exceeds the registration threshold.

MTD StatementTrue / False
MTD software prints returns that are then posted to HMRC✗ False — submitted digitally
Standard VAT return and payment dates still apply✓ True
Businesses are still responsible for checking returns✓ True
Applies to ALL VAT-registered businesses, even below threshold✓ True (from April 2025)

Correcting VAT Errors

  • Net error ≤ £10,000 (or ≤ 1% of net VAT turnover, max £50,000): adjust on the current VAT return
  • Net error > £10,000: must be disclosed to HMRC separately via a VAT652 form
Syllabus F2(j)

Penalties for Late Submission & Payment

Late Submission — Penalty Points System

The UK has replaced the old default surcharge with a points-based system for late VAT returns.

1
Each late submission earns 1 penalty point.
2
No financial penalty arises UNTIL you reach the threshold of 4 points (for quarterly filers).
3
Once at 4 points, a £200 penalty is charged for each subsequent late submission.
4
Points expire after 2 years — BUT only if the penalty threshold has NOT been reached.
5
Once at threshold (4 points), all points reset to zero only after 12 consecutive on-time returns.
Worked Example — Penalty Points

Chimney Ltd already has 3 penalty points. It then files the Q1 2026 return late (23 March 2026 vs deadline 7 March 2026).

  • Points now = 4 → threshold reached
  • A £200 penalty is charged immediately
  • If the next return (Q2) is also filed late → another £200 penalty
  • Points will NOT expire after 2 years; they reset to zero only after 12 consecutive on-time returns

Late Payment Penalties

Based on how many days the VAT payment is overdue:

Days LatePenalty
Up to 15 days lateNo penalty
16 to 30 days late3% of the unpaid VAT
More than 30 days late6% of the unpaid VAT, plus a daily rate at an annual rate of 10%

Interest is also charged on late paid VAT from the due date to the actual payment date, regardless of whether a penalty applies.

Worked Example — Late Payment

VAT was due on 7 November 2025. Payment was made on 27 November 2025 — 20 days late (in the 16–30 day band).

  • Penalty = 3% of late paid VAT (not zero, and not 6%)
  • Interest is also payable from 7 Nov to 27 Nov

Errors and Penalties for Inaccuracies

BehaviourPenalty Rate

Syllabus F3

Special VAT Schemes

💧 Cash Accounting

Join: Expected taxable T/O ≤ £1,350,000
Leave: T/O exceeds £1,600,000
  • Output tax = when payment received
  • Input tax = when payment made
  • Automatic bad debt protection
  • Good for businesses with slow-paying customers

📅 Annual Accounting

Join: Expected taxable T/O ≤ £1,350,000
Leave: T/O exceeds £1,600,000
  • Only one return per year
  • 9 interim monthly payments (months 4–12): each = 10% of prior year's VAT liability
  • Balancing payment with annual return
  • Reduces admin burden

📊 Flat Rate Scheme

Join: Expected taxable T/O (ex-VAT) ≤ £150,000
Leave: T/O (inc. VAT) exceeds £230,000
  • VAT = flat-rate % × VAT-inclusive total turnover
  • Includes zero-rated and exempt sales
  • No input VAT reclaimed on purchases
  • Still charge 20% on sales invoices

Flat Rate Scheme — Deep Dive (Highly Tested!)

This is the trickiest scheme. The formula is:

Flat Rate VAT = Flat Rate % × (VAT-INCLUSIVE total taxable turnover) Note: "Taxable turnover" includes standard-rated AND zero-rated sales It does NOT include exempt sales Step 1: Take net (ex-VAT) standard-rated sales and × 120% to get VAT-inclusive Step 2: Add zero-rated sales (they have no VAT to add, already gross) Step 3: Apply the flat rate % to the total
Worked Example — Flat Rate Scheme

Kristel has standard-rated sales of £26,250 (ex-VAT) and zero-rated sales of £6,300. Flat rate % = 8.5%.

Standard-rated sales (inc. VAT): £26,250 × 120%£31,500
Zero-rated sales£6,300
Total VAT-inclusive turnover£37,800
Flat rate VAT: £37,800 × 8.5%£3,213
Worked Example — Jack's Monthly Flat Rate

Jack: Standard-rated sales = £10,000/month (ex-VAT). Purchases = £7,000/month (ex-VAT). Flat rate % = 8%.

VAT-inclusive turnover: £10,000 × 120%£12,000
Flat rate VAT: £12,000 × 8%£960

Notice: purchases of £7,000 are completely irrelevant under the flat rate scheme. The purchases figure is a red herring.

Annual Accounting — Monthly Payments

Worked Example — Annual Accounting Payments

Triangle Ltd. Year ended 31 Dec 2024 VAT = £47,700. Year ended 31 Dec 2025 VAT = £73,500.

Monthly payments for 2025 year are based on the prior year (2024):

Monthly payment = £47,700 × 10%£4,770
Number of payments (months 4 to 12)9 payments
Total payments on account£42,930
Balancing payment with return: £73,500 − £42,930£30,570

VAT Groups

Conditions to Form a VAT Group

  • Each company must be under common control (more than 50% owned by the same person/company)
  • Each company must be established in, or have a fixed establishment in, the UK
  • Eligible companies are NOT automatically included — each company can choose to be included or excluded
Worked Example — VAT Group Eligibility

Fergus owns: A Ltd (75%), B Ltd (60% via A Ltd), C Ltd (100%), D Sarl — a French company (100%). Which can join a VAT group?

  • ✓ A Ltd — UK company, >50% owned by Fergus
  • ✓ B Ltd — UK company, >50% owned (via A Ltd chain)
  • ✓ C Ltd — UK company, 100% owned
  • ✗ D Sarl — Cannot join: no UK establishment

Consequences of a VAT Group

  • Supplies between group members are ignored for VAT purposes (no VAT charged between them)
  • One representative member submits one consolidated VAT return for the entire group (any member can be the representative)
  • All group members are jointly and severally liable for the whole group's VAT — not just their share
Syllabus F2(k)(l)

Overseas — Imports, Exports & Reverse Charge

Exports (Goods leaving the UK)

Exports of goods are zero-rated, regardless of what their UK rate would be. The overseas customer accounts for tax in their own country.

Imports (Goods entering the UK)

For goods imported from outside the UK, the importer must account for VAT as if it were a UK supply:

  • Account for output VAT at the UK rate on the value of the imported goods (on the VAT return covering the date of importation)
  • If used for taxable supplies, reclaim as input VAT on the same return
  • Net effect = £0 for a fully taxable business (postponed VAT accounting)
The Rate Applied
Always use the UK rate when importing. The VAT rate in the overseas country is completely irrelevant.

Reverse Charge — Services from Overseas

When a UK VAT-registered business receives services from an overseas supplier, the customer (not the supplier) accounts for UK VAT via the reverse charge:

  • The UK business accounts for output VAT at the UK rate (as if it were the supplier)
  • It then claims the same amount as input VAT (if used for taxable business purposes)
  • Again, net effect = £0 for a fully taxable business
Worked Example — Overseas Services (Reverse Charge)

Kristel paid £220 for marketing services from an overseas company. The marketing services are standard-rated in the UK.

  • Output VAT (reverse charge): £220 × 20% = £44
  • Input VAT recovery: £220 × 20% = £44
  • Net VAT effect = £0

Note: The exam question for Kristel only asked for input VAT, so the answer was £44. The reverse charge output VAT was noted but not included in the output VAT computation asked.

Exam Practice

Practice Questions

Work through these before revealing the answer. These are styled after real ACCA objective test questions.

Practice Set 1 — Registration
Q1. Brett started trading on 1 February 2024. His sales were £6,000/month for 3 months, then £8,000/month for 4 months, then £10,500/month after that. From what date must Brett charge output VAT?
✓ Correct Answer: D — 1 February 2025
Cumulative taxable supplies to 31 December 2024 = (3×£6k) + (4×£8k) + (5×£10.5k) = £18k + £32k + £52.5k = £102,500 — exceeds £90,000 by end of December 2024.

Notify HMRC by 30 January 2025 (30 days from end of December). Registered and must charge VAT from 1 February 2025 (1st day of 2nd month after December).
Q2. Betty voluntarily registers for VAT. Which TWO of the following statements are TRUE about input VAT on supplies made BEFORE registration?
✓ Correct Answers: B and C
A — False: Cars used partly privately → input VAT always blocked.
B — True: Fuel (a service) consumed prior to registration → cannot reclaim (must have been incurred within 6 months AND the service must be available at registration).
C — True: A van is a commercial vehicle (not a car). It was purchased within 4 years, is still held/in use → input VAT recoverable.
D — False: Accountancy services = services, recoverable only within 6 months. One year ago = out of time.
E — False: Goods already sold at registration → no longer "on hand" → cannot reclaim.
Practice Set 2 — Tax Points & Output VAT
Q3. Padma Ltd received an order on 20 October 2025. Goods were delivered on 14 November 2025. Padma issued an invoice on 30 November 2025. What is the tax point?
✓ Correct Answer: C — 30 November 2025
BTP = 14 November (delivery date). Invoice issued on 30 November is 16 days after the BTP — that's within 14 days... wait, 30 Nov minus 14 Nov = 16 days. That's OUTSIDE 14 days!

Therefore, the tax point remains the BTP = 14 November 2025. (Answer B is actually correct in this case — the 14-day rule requires the invoice within 14 days of delivery; 16 days is too late.)

Examiner note: Always count the days precisely. If the invoice had been on 28 November (14 days), it would override to 28 November. At 16 days, the BTP prevails.
Q4. Kristel (retailer of ladies' and children's clothes) has standard-rated sales of £7,200 and zero-rated sales of £1,600 in the quarter. She offered a prompt payment discount; the discount taken was £140 on standard-rated and £90 on zero-rated. What is her total output VAT?
✓ Correct Answer: D — £1,412
Output VAT on standard-rated = (£7,200 − £140) × 20% = £7,060 × 20% = £1,412
Output VAT on zero-rated = (£1,600 − £90) × 0% = £0

Total output VAT = £1,412

The prompt payment discount must be deducted before calculating VAT, as VAT is charged on the actual amount received. Note: the zero-rated discount doesn't affect VAT at all.
Practice Set 3 — Special Schemes
Q5. Hamza uses the flat rate scheme. For year ended 31 March 2026: standard-rated sales (net) = £100,000; zero-rated sales = £30,000; standard-rated purchases (net) = £15,000. Flat rate % = 11%. What is his VAT liability?
✓ Correct Answer: B — £16,500
Flat rate scheme: apply % to VAT-inclusive total turnover (standard + zero-rated):

Standard-rated (inc. VAT): £100,000 × 120% = £120,000
Zero-rated: £30,000
Total: £150,000

VAT = £150,000 × 11% = £16,500

Purchases of £15,000 are completely irrelevant — the whole point of the flat rate scheme is that purchases are ignored. Classic exam trap!
Q6. Which of these statements about the VAT cash accounting scheme is TRUE?
✓ Correct Answer: C
A — False: Under cash accounting, input tax is claimed when payment is made (not the invoice date) — this can sometimes DELAY recovery compared to the normal scheme, not just input timing.
B — False: The £150,000 limit is for the flat rate scheme. Cash accounting threshold = £1,350,000.
C — True: Output VAT is not due until customers pay → helps cash flow when customers are slow payers.
D — False: No such restriction exists for cash accounting (this is more of a misconception).
Practice Set 4 — Penalties & Administration
Q7. Chimney Ltd has 3 penalty points. It files its Q1 2026 VAT return late, giving it 4 penalty points. Which of these consequences is NOT true?
✓ Correct Answer: B — This statement is NOT true
Once a business has reached the threshold (4 points), the 2-year expiry rule NO LONGER applies. Points can only be reset to zero by submitting all returns on time for the next 12 consecutive months.

The other statements are all true: A £200 penalty arises at 4 points, further late returns incur additional £200 penalties, and 12 on-time returns are needed to reset.
Q8. Mabel ceased trading on 31 March 2026. By when must she notify HMRC of her cessation? And from when can she be deregistered?
✓ Correct Answer: B
On ceasing taxable supplies (31 March 2026):
→ Notify HMRC within 30 days = by 30 April 2026
→ Deregistration can be backdated to the date she ceased making taxable supplies = 31 March 2026

The deregistration date can be the date of cessation itself — it doesn't have to wait for the notification deadline.
