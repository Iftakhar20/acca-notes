Section 01

Employment Income — the Basics

Employment income is assessable under the receipts basis: income is taxed in the tax year it is received (or when the employee becomes entitled to it, if earlier for directors).

Structure of Employment Income

-- Employment Income Computation (Tax Year 2025/26) -- Salary / wages / bonuses X Add: Taxable benefits in kind X Add: Taxable reimbursed expenses X Less: Allowable deductions (e.g. pension, expenses) (X) ─── Employment Income X

Receipts Basis — Key Timing Rules

Income TypeWhen TaxableExample
Regular salaryDate received (usually pay date)Salary paid 28 Feb 2026 → 2025/26
BonusDate received OR date entitled — whichever is earlierBonus approved Dec 2025, paid Apr 2026 → 2025/26 (if entitled Dec 2025)
Director's remunerationEarliest of: credited to account, received, or when entitlement determinedBoard minutes Dec 2025 = taxed 2025/26
📝
Exam Tip: A bonus paid on 6 April 2025 in respect of the year ending 31 December 2024 is taxable in 2025/26 (date received). A bonus paid 3 April 2026 for year ending 31 December 2025 is also taxable in 2025/26.

Allowable Deductions from Employment Income

DeductibleNot Deductible
Employee contributions to occupational pension schemeInterest on mortgage for main residence
Expenses incurred wholly, exclusively, and necessarily in performing dutiesInterest on loan to buy shares in a quoted company
Professional subscriptions (approved bodies)Cost of commuting between home and office
Business travel expensesClothing (unless a specific uniform)
Home working allowance £6/week (HMRC approved)General training not required for current job
Section 02

Exempt Benefits (Zero Taxable Value)

These benefits are provided by an employer but create no taxable benefit for the employee and carry no Class 1A NIC liability for the employer.

🚗 Workplace Transport

EXEMPT
Employer-provided car parking at or near the workplace.
Works bus services / minibuses.

📱 Mobile Phone

EXEMPT
One mobile phone per employee (including private calls). A second phone is a taxable benefit.

🍽️ Staff Canteen

EXEMPT
Meals provided free or subsidised on the employer's premises, available to all staff.

🏫 Workplace Nursery

EXEMPT
Employer-provided or contracted childcare on the employer's premises (or in a facility the employer manages). Not merely a voucher.

🏋️ In-House Gym

EXEMPT
Use of the employer's own sporting / recreational facilities. But an external gym membership paid by the employer is TAXABLE.

💼 Employer Pension Contributions

EXEMPT
Employer contributions to registered pension schemes are exempt from tax and Class 1A NIC.

🎁 Trivial Benefits

EXEMPT
Gifts to employees costing ≤ £50 per occasion, not cash/cash-voucher, not performance-related. (Directors: max £300/year aggregate.)

⚡ Electric Car Charging

EXEMPT
Electricity provided by the employer to charge an electric company car. There is no additional fuel benefit charge.

⚠️
Common Exam Trap: A company gym on the employer's premises used only by employees = exempt. A membership to an external health club = taxable. In the Dill question (Kaplan Q95), the company gym is £0 benefit; the external health club of £990 is fully taxable.

Annual Party / Staff Event

Annual parties (e.g. Christmas party) are exempt if the cost per head does not exceed £150 per year in total across all qualifying events. If cost exceeds £150 per head, the full amount (not just the excess) becomes taxable.

📝
A summer event costing £200 per head = fully taxable (exceeds £150). The event costing £120 per head = exempt.
Section 03

Company Car Benefit — Full Calculation Method

Key FA2025 Figures — Company Car
Base CO₂: 55 g/km
Base %: 17% at 55 g/km
Diesel surcharge: +4% (unless RDE2)
Each 5 g/km above 55: +1%
Electric (0 g/km): 3%
Max %: 37%
1
Find the List Price — always use the manufacturer's list price, never the price actually paid by the employer. Include any accessories added at or after delivery.
2
Determine the Appropriate Percentage — based on fuel type and CO₂ emissions (see table below).
3
Calculate the Gross Benefit — List Price × Appropriate % × months available/12.
4
Deduct Employee Contributions — any amount the employee pays the employer for private use of the car reduces the benefit.

Appropriate Percentage Table (FA2025 — Tax Year 2025/26)

Fuel Type / CO₂Appropriate %Notes
Electric (0 g/km)3%No electricity fuel benefit
Hybrid: ≥130 mile electric range3%CO₂ between 1–50 g/km
Hybrid: 70–129 mile electric range6%CO₂ between 1–50 g/km
Hybrid: 40–69 mile electric range9%CO₂ between 1–50 g/km
Hybrid: 30–39 mile electric range13%CO₂ between 1–50 g/km
Hybrid: <30 mile electric range15%CO₂ between 1–50 g/km
Petrol / RDE2 Diesel: 51–54 g/km16%
Petrol / RDE2 Diesel: 55 g/km17%Base level
Petrol: 56–59 g/km18%+1% per 5 g/km above 55
Petrol: 60–64 g/km19%
Petrol: 100 g/km26%17 + (100–55)/5 = 17+9
Petrol: 155 g/km37%Capped at 37%
Non-RDE2 Diesel: add 4% to above+4%Rounds CO₂ down to nearest 5
🔑
CO₂ Rounding Rule: CO₂ emissions are rounded down to the nearest 5 g/km before applying the formula. E.g., 152 g/km → rounded to 150 g/km.

Formula Summary

-- Appropriate % Calculation (Petrol, CO₂ > 55 g/km) -- % = 17 + [(CO₂ rounded down to nearest 5) – 55] ÷ 5 -- If Non-RDE2 Diesel: add 4% -- -- Car Benefit -- Car Benefit = List Price × Appropriate % × (months available ÷ 12) Less: Employee's contribution to private use (X) ─── Taxable Car Benefit X
🚗 Worked Example 1 — Petrol Car (Thiago / Kaplan Q27)

Facts: New diesel car (meets RDE2), list price £28,000, CO₂ = 152 g/km, available from 6 May 2025 (11 months).

-- Step 1: CO₂ rounded down -- 152 g/km → 150 g/km -- Step 2: Appropriate % -- Base (RDE2 diesel, so no 4% surcharge) = 17% Plus: (150 – 55) ÷ 5 = 95 ÷ 5 = 19% ──── Appropriate % 36% -- Step 3: Car Benefit -- £28,000 × 36% × 11/12 = £9,240
✓ Answer
Taxable car benefit = £9,240
⚡ Worked Example 2 — Electric Car (Enzo / Kaplan Q28)

Facts: Electric car, zero CO₂, list price £45,000 (employer paid £42,000 — irrelevant). Used 60% business / 40% private — also irrelevant; proportion of private use does NOT affect the benefit.

-- Appropriate % for electric car -- CO₂ = 0 → Appropriate % = 3% -- Car Benefit (full year) -- £45,000 × 3% = £1,350 -- Charging electricity provided -- Electric car charging at office = £0 (exempt)
✓ Answer
Taxable car benefit = £1,350. No fuel benefit charge.
🔋 Worked Example 3 — Hybrid Car (Jason / Kaplan Q98)

Facts: Hybrid car, list price £62,500, CO₂ between 1–50 g/km, electric range < 30 miles. Available from 15 March 2025 (whole of 2025/26 = full year).

-- Hybrid: CO₂ 1–50 g/km, range < 30 miles -- Appropriate % = 15% -- Car Benefit -- £62,500 × 15% = £9,375
✓ Answer
Taxable car benefit = £9,375
🚗 Worked Example 4 — Non-RDE2 Diesel (Poppy / Kaplan Q99)

Facts: Diesel car (does NOT meet RDE2), list price £21,800, CO₂ = 70 g/km. Provided 1 August 2025 to 5 April 2026 (8 months).

-- Step 1: CO₂ rounded down -- 70 g/km → 70 g/km (already a multiple of 5) -- Step 2: Base % (treated as petrol first) -- 17 + (70 – 55) ÷ 5 = 17 + 3 = 20% -- Add diesel surcharge (NOT RDE2) -- 20 + 4 = 24% -- Step 3: Car Benefit -- £21,800 × 24% × 8/12 = £3,488
✓ Answer
Taxable car benefit = £3,488
Section 04

Car Fuel Benefit

FA2025 Fuel Benefit Base Figure
Fuel benefit base: £28,200
Formula: £28,200 × Appropriate %
-- Car Fuel Benefit (only if employer pays for ANY private fuel) -- Fuel Benefit = £28,200 × Appropriate % × (months available ÷ 12) Less: Employee contribution for private fuel (Only if pays FULL cost of ALL private fuel) ─── Taxable Fuel Benefit X
⚠️
All-or-Nothing Rule: If the employee makes a partial payment for private fuel, the fuel benefit is not reduced — it only reduces to nil if the employee reimburses the full cost of all private fuel. This makes it almost always beneficial to include the full fuel benefit rather than a partial reimbursement.
⛽ Worked Example — Fuel Benefit (FA2025)

Facts: Emma has a petrol company car, list price £30,000, CO₂ = 105 g/km, available all year. Employer also pays for all private fuel.

-- Appropriate % -- CO₂ rounded: 105 g/km → 105 g/km % = 17 + (105 – 55) ÷ 5 = 17 + 10 = 27% -- Car Benefit -- £30,000 × 27% = £8,100 -- Fuel Benefit -- £28,200 × 27% = £7,614 -- Total car-related benefit -- £8,100 + £7,614 = £15,714

Company Van & Van Fuel Benefit

FA2025 Van Benefit Figures (Given in Exam!)
Van benefit: £4,020
Van fuel benefit: £769
Zero-emission van: £0

Unlike company cars, van benefits are flat-rate charges. The list price, CO₂ emissions, and % of private use are all irrelevant — except if private use is insignificant, in which case there is no benefit at all.

🚐 Worked Example — Van (Olayide / Kaplan Q29)

Facts: Van (list price £30,000, CO₂ 158 g/km) provided full year, 40% private use. Employer pays private fuel.

-- Van Benefit (list price and CO2 irrelevant) -- Van benefit (flat rate) = £4,020 Van fuel benefit (flat rate) = £769 ──── Total taxable benefit = £4,789
✓ Answer
Total van benefit = £4,789
Section 06

Living Accommodation Benefit

Where an employer provides living accommodation to an employee, there are potentially three separate benefits:

1️⃣ Basic Benefit (Annual Value)

Always charged. The annual value (rateable value / rental value) of the property.

2️⃣ Additional Benefit (Expensive Accommodation)

Only if original cost + improvements made before start of tax year exceeds £75,000.

3️⃣ Running Costs

If the employer also pays bills (heating, council tax, etc.), these are added as a separate benefit at cost.

Additional Benefit — Expensive Accommodation

-- Only applies if cost > £75,000 -- -- Case A: Purchased < 6 years before first provided -- Base = Cost of property + cost of improvements before start of tax year -- Case B: Purchased ≥ 6 years before first provided -- Base = Market Value when first made available to employee (improvements before this date are already reflected in MV) -- Additional Benefit Calculation -- (Base – £75,000) × Official Rate of Interest (3.75% for 2025/26) = Additional Benefit -- Total Living Accommodation Benefit -- Annual Value X Additional benefit (if > £75,000) X Running costs paid by employer X Furniture benefit (20% of cost) X ─── Sub-total X Less: Employee's contribution (X) ─── Total Taxable Benefit X × months available ÷ 12 (if not full year)

Furniture in Employer-Provided Accommodation

If the employer also provides furniture, an additional benefit arises:
Furniture Benefit = 20% × Cost of furniture

This is a separate benefit — not included within the accommodation benefit calculation.

Job-Related Accommodation — Exempt!

Accommodation is exempt from all three benefit charges if it qualifies as job-related, e.g.:

  • Necessary for the proper performance of duties (e.g. caretaker, minister)
  • Customary for that type of employment and enables better performance of duties
  • Provided for security reasons (e.g. police officer, government minister)
🏠 Worked Example — Amanda's Accommodation (Kaplan Q123)

Facts: Employer purchased property on 10 February 2023 for £174,000. Improvements of £8,000 were made. Annual value £6,300. Furniture provided by employer at cost £12,400. Accommodation provided 1 November 2025 to 5 April 2026 (5 months). Official interest rate = 3.75%.

Property was purchased less than 6 years before first provided to Amanda → use cost method.

-- Annual Value -- Annual value = 6,300 -- Additional Benefit -- Cost 174,000 Add: Improvements +8,000 ─────── 182,000 Less: £75,000 limit (75,000) ─────── Excess 107,000 × ORI 3.75% = 4,013 -- Furniture Benefit -- £12,400 × 20% = 2,480 ─────── Sub-total = 12,793 ─────── -- Time apportion (5 months) -- £12,793 × 5/12 = £5,330
✓ Answer
Total taxable living accommodation benefit = £5,330
📝
Examiner's Warning: The most common mistakes are: (1) Using MV instead of cost when property was purchased <6 years ago; (2) Forgetting the furniture benefit; (3) Forgetting to time-apportion when accommodation was not available for the full year; (4) Not deducting employee contributions.
Section 07

Beneficial Loan Benefit

If an employer provides a loan to an employee at below the official rate of interest (ORI), the difference is a taxable benefit. ORI for 2025/26 = 3.75%.

💡
No benefit arises if the total outstanding loans to an employee do not exceed £10,000 at any point in the tax year (de minimis rule).

Two Calculation Methods

Method 1: Average Method

Formula:

Average loan = (Opening balance + Closing balance) ÷ 2

Benefit = Average loan × ORI × months/12

Less: interest actually paid by employee


Default method in the exam unless told otherwise or employee requests exact method.

Method 2: Exact Method

Formula:

Calculate interest on the actual loan balance day-by-day (or monthly)

Can only be used if HMRC or the employee requests it.


The employee (not the employer) can elect to use this method if it gives a lower benefit.

-- Average Method (most common in exam) -- Balance at start of tax year (or when loan taken) A Balance at end of tax year (or when loan repaid) B ─── Average loan = (A + B) ÷ 2 Benefit = Average loan × ORI (3.75%) × months/12 Less: Interest actually paid by employee (X) ─── Taxable Loan Benefit X
💰 Worked Example — Beneficial Loan (Woojin / Kaplan Q30)

Facts: Loan of £100,000 taken 6 April 2025. Employee repaid £40,000 on 6 August 2025. Balance at 5 April 2026 = £60,000. Employee pays 1% interest.

-- Average Method -- Balance at start (6 April 2025) = 100,000 Balance at end (5 April 2026) = 60,000 ─────── 160,000 Average loan (÷2) = 80,000 -- Gross Benefit -- £80,000 × 3.75% = 3,000 -- Less interest paid by employee -- Apr–Aug (4 months): £100,000 × 1% × 4/12= (333) Aug–Apr (8 months): £60,000 × 1% × 8/12 = (400) ───── Net Taxable Loan Benefit = £2,267
✓ Answer
Taxable beneficial loan benefit = £2,267 (Answer B in Kaplan)
Section 08

Other Benefits — Assets, Medical, Mileage

Use of an Asset Provided to Employee

When an employer provides an asset (e.g. a television, bicycle, laptop) for the employee's private use, the annual benefit is:

Annual Benefit = 20% × Market Value of the asset when first provided to the employee

The benefit runs each year the asset is available for private use.

Transfer / Gift of an Asset to the Employee

When the employer later gives (gifts) the asset to the employee, the benefit is the higher of:

(i) Market Value at date of transfer (ii) Market Value when first made available for private use Less: Annual benefits already taxed in prior years (20% × MV per year) ───────────────── Net amount ───────────────── Taxable gift benefit = Higher of (i) or (ii)
📸 Worked Example — Camera Gift (Max / Kaplan Q31)

Facts: Star Ltd provided Max with a camera on 6 April 2024. Market value at that date = £2,000. On 6 April 2025, Star Ltd gifted the camera to Max. Market value on gift date = £1,400.

-- Use of asset benefit (2024/25) -- £2,000 × 20% = 400 (taxed in 2024/25) -- Gift benefit (2025/26): Higher of -- (i) MV at date of gift = 1,400 (ii) MV when first provided = 2,000 Less: already taxed (£2,000 × 20%) = (400) ───── 1,600 ───── Higher of £1,400 and £1,600 = £1,600
✓ Answer D in Kaplan
Taxable gift benefit in 2025/26 = £1,600
📺 Worked Example — Television Provided & Gifted (Kim / Kaplan Q94)

Facts: SharpSuit plc provided Kim with a television costing £2,000 on 6 April 2024 for private use. On 6 April 2025, it was given to Kim. Market value on gift date = £1,400 (typical question format).

-- 2024/25: Use of asset benefit -- £2,000 × 20% = 400 -- 2025/26: Gift benefit -- (i) MV at gift date = 1,400 (ii) MV when first provided = 2,000 Less: taxed 2024/25 = (400) Net = 1,600 Higher of (i) and (ii) = £1,600
✓ Answer
Taxable benefit 2025/26 = £1,600

Approved Mileage Allowance Payments (AMAP)

FA2025 AMAP Rates
First 10,000 miles: 45p/mile
Over 10,000 miles: 25p/mile

When an employee uses their own car for business travel:

  • Employer pays less than AMAP → difference is a deduction (allowable expense) from employment income.
  • Employer pays more than AMAP → excess is a taxable benefit.
  • Commuting (home to office) is not business travel — no AMAP relief.
🚗 Worked Example — AMAP (John Beach / Kaplan Q96)

Facts: John drove 5,960 business miles (excluding commuting — 4,270 miles to customers + 510 miles to other offices, totalling 4,780 allowable). Wait — re-reading: total paid was for 5,960 miles at 60p. Allowable AMAP: (4,270+510) = 4,780 × 45p (all under 10,000).

-- Mileage received -- 5,960 miles × 60p = 3,576 -- AMAP entitlement (4,780 miles, all under 10,000) -- (4,270 + 510) miles × 45p = (2,151) ────── Taxable excess mileage benefit = £1,425
✓ Answer
Taxable mileage benefit = £1,425
Note: travel between home and office is ordinary commuting — excluded from allowable business miles.
Section 09

National Insurance Contributions — Class 1A

Class 1A NIC is paid by the employer only (not the employee) on the taxable value of most non-cash benefits in kind.

Rate: 15% for 2025/26 (applied to the taxable benefit value).

Due date: 22 July following the end of the tax year (if paying electronically), or 19 July if paying by cheque.

What Attracts Class 1A NIC?

✅ Class 1A NIC Applies
  • Company car benefit
  • Car fuel benefit
  • Van benefit and van fuel benefit
  • Private medical insurance
  • Living accommodation
  • Beneficial loan benefit
  • Non-cash assets provided/gifted
  • External gym / sports club membership
  • Annual events over £150/head
❌ Class 1A NIC Does NOT Apply
  • Exempt benefits (one mobile, staff canteen, in-house gym etc.)
  • Cash payments → Class 1 NIC instead
  • Cash vouchers → Class 1 NIC instead
  • Employer pension contributions → exempt
  • Car parking at/near workplace → exempt
  • Workplace nursery → exempt
  • Ordinary commuting mileage → Class 1 if cash paid
💷 Worked Example — Class 1A NIC (Alonso / Kaplan Q60)

Facts: Red Dolphin Ltd provided: (1) Cash vouchers £3,000; (2) Gift of a new television £2,000.

-- Cash vouchers -- Class 1 NIC (not Class 1A) — excluded = £0 Class 1A -- Television (non-cash asset) -- £2,000 × 15% = £300 -- Due date (electronic) -- = 22 July 2026
✓ Answer
Class 1A NIC = £300, due 22 July 2026

Practice Questions & Full Answers

❓ Practice Question 1 — Car & Fuel Benefit

Foo is provided with a petrol car on 1 October 2025. The car has a list price of £24,000 and CO₂ emissions of 90 g/km. The employer also provides fuel for all private journeys. The car is available for the rest of 2025/26 (6 months). Calculate the car benefit and fuel benefit.

▶ Click to reveal answer
✓ Answer
-- Appropriate % (Petrol) -- CO₂: 90 g/km → 90 g/km % = 17 + (90 – 55) ÷ 5 = 17 + 7 = 24% -- Car Benefit (6 months) -- £24,000 × 24% × 6/12 = £2,880 -- Fuel Benefit (6 months) -- £28,200 × 24% × 6/12 = £3,384

Car benefit = £2,880 | Fuel benefit = £3,384

❓ Practice Question 2 — Living Accommodation

Hannah's employer (Lomly Ltd) provides her with accommodation from 1 January 2026. The property was purchased by Lomly Ltd 3 years ago for £250,000. Annual value = £16,000. No improvements. Official rate 3.75%. Calculate the accommodation benefit for 2025/26.

▶ Click to reveal answer
✓ Answer
-- Property purchased < 6 years ago → use cost -- Annual Value = 16,000 Additional Benefit: Cost 250,000 Less: £75,000 (75,000) Excess 175,000 × 3.75% = 6,563 ────── Sub-total = 22,563 ────── -- Time apportion: 3 months (Jan–Apr) -- £22,563 × 3/12 = £5,641

Taxable accommodation benefit = £5,641

❓ Practice Question 3 — Television Benefit

On 6 April 2024, an employer provided an employee with a 65-inch television costing £1,800 for personal use. On 6 April 2025, the employer gave the television to the employee as a gift. Its market value was £1,100 on 6 April 2025. Calculate the taxable benefit in 2025/26.

▶ Click to reveal answer
✓ Answer
-- 2024/25: Use of asset benefit (already taxed) -- £1,800 × 20% = £360 -- 2025/26: Gift benefit — higher of: -- (i) MV at gift date = 1,100 (ii) MV when first provided = 1,800 Less: taxed in 2024/25 = (360) ───── 1,440 Higher of £1,100 and £1,440 = £1,440

Taxable benefit 2025/26 = £1,440

❓ Practice Question 4 — Beneficial Loan

On 1 August 2025, Subsequent plc provided Jason with an interest-free loan of £140,000 to help him purchase a main residence. No repayments are made before 5 April 2026. Calculate the taxable loan benefit for 2025/26 (8 months).

▶ Click to reveal answer
✓ Answer
-- Average Method -- Balance at start (1 Aug 2025) = 140,000 Balance at end (5 Apr 2026) = 140,000 Average = 140,000 -- Benefit (8 months, interest-free so no deduction) -- £140,000 × 3.75% × 8/12 = £3,500

Taxable loan benefit = £3,500

❓ Practice Question 5 — Mixed Benefits Summary (Comprehensive)

Tom is employed by Alpha Ltd on a salary of £55,000 for 2025/26. He receives the following:

  • Diesel company car (RDE2), list price £32,000, CO₂ = 120 g/km, available full year
  • Employer pays all fuel including private
  • One mobile phone worth £600
  • Employer contributes £2,000 to Tom's occupational pension
  • External gym membership £1,200 paid by Alpha Ltd
  • Staff canteen meals (open to all staff) costing employer £800

Calculate Tom's total employment income.

▶ Click to reveal answer
✓ Answer
-- Car benefit % (RDE2 diesel, CO₂ 120 g/km) -- 120 g/km → 120 g/km % = 17 + (120–55)÷5 = 17 + 13 = 30% -- RDE2: NO 4% surcharge -- -- Car Benefit -- £32,000 × 30% = 9,600 -- Fuel Benefit -- £28,200 × 30% = 8,460 -- Exempt items -- 1 mobile phone = 0 (exempt) Employer pension contribution = 0 (exempt) Staff canteen (open to all staff) = 0 (exempt) -- Taxable items -- External gym membership = 1,200 (taxable) -- Employment Income -- Salary = 55,000 Car benefit = 9,600 Fuel benefit = 8,460 Gym membership = 1,200 ────── Total Employment Income = £74,260

Total employment income = £74,260

Class 1A NIC (employer) = (£9,600 + £8,460 + £1,200) × 15% = £19,260 × 15% = £2,889, due 22 July 2026.
