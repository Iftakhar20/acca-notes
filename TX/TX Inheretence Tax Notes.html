1The Big Picture — What IHT Is About

Inheritance Tax is a tax on the transfer of wealth. The UK charges IHT when someone gives away assets — either during their lifetime or when they die.

The key logic is: the government wants a cut every time wealth passes from one person to another outside of exempt categories. IHT is therefore charged on the donor (the person giving), not the recipient.

🎁 Lifetime Giving

Some gifts are immediately taxable (CLTs). Others are tentatively tax-free (PETs) but become chargeable if the donor dies within 7 years.

💀 Death Estate

Everything owned at death is valued, exemptions/deductions applied, and IHT charged at 40% on the excess over the nil rate band.

⚡ Syllabus Scope (FA2025)

The June 2026 syllabus excludes: trusts, business property relief, agricultural property relief, domicile/long-term residence rules, related property, and grossing-up. Everything else below is examinable.

2Transfer of Value & Types of Gift

What is the "Transfer of Value"?

The transfer of value is NOT simply the market value of what you give away. It is the loss to the donor's estate — the diminution in value. This is especially important for shares.

🔑 Principle

Transfer of value = Value of estate BEFORE the gift − Value of estate AFTER the gift

For cash or normal assets, loss to estate = market value of the gift. For shares in a private company, because a majority shareholding commands a higher price per share, the loss to the donor's estate is more than the market value of the shares given away.

🧠 Memory Hook

Person → Person = PET. Person → Trust = CLT.

Chronological Order Rule

Always process gifts in date order — oldest first. You apply the nil rate band to the earliest gift. Later gifts only get whatever NRB remains.

3Lifetime Exemptions

These reduce the transfer of value to arrive at the gross chargeable transfer (GCT). Apply them in the order given below.

⚠️ Exam Trap — Annual Exemption

AEs cannot be deducted from the death estate. They only apply to lifetime gifts. Many students incorrectly apply AEs to the estate calculation.

⚠️ Exam Trap — AE is Applied Even to PETs That Never Become Chargeable

If someone makes a PET in 2023/24 and survives 7 years, the AE for 2023/24 is still used up. This means less AE available for the next gift in the same year.

4Lifetime Tax on CLTs

When someone gives into a trust (a CLT), there is an immediate IHT charge. The rate depends on who pays the tax.

Donee (Trust) Pays Tax

Tax = 20% × taxable amount.
The gross chargeable transfer = value of gift (after exemptions).

Donor Pays Tax

Tax must be grossed up because paying the tax is itself a further gift. Tax = 25% × taxable amount (= 20/80 × taxable).
Gross chargeable transfer = gift + tax paid.

⚠️ Critical Point — PETs Do NOT Reduce NRB at Lifetime Stage

When calculating lifetime tax on a CLT, only prior CLTs eat into the available NRB. Prior PETs are completely ignored at this stage (they are only relevant on death).

5Death & the Seven-Year Rule

When a person dies, HMRC looks back 7 years to review all lifetime gifts. The outcome depends on what type of gift each one was.

Gift TypeIf made > 7 years before deathIf made ≤ 7 years before death
PET Falls away — no tax charge at all.
But AE used at the time is still used up.		 Becomes fully chargeable. Tax at death rates (40%) less taper relief if 3–7 years.
CLT Falls out of the 7-year window — no additional death charge.
But still reduces NRB for gifts made after it (while still within 7 years of those later gifts).		 Re-assessed at death rates. If death tax > lifetime tax already paid → pay the difference (top-up). Never a refund.

Key Principle — NRB Accumulation at Death

When computing death tax on each gift, the NRB available is reduced by all gross chargeable transfers (both CLTs and PETs that have become chargeable) in the 7 years before that particular gift.

  • List all gifts in date order, applying exemptions to arrive at the gross chargeable amount for each.

  • For each gift within 7 years of death, find the NRB at the date of death and deduct GCTs in the 7 years before that gift.

  • Apply 40% to the taxable amount. Apply taper relief if gift was 3–7 years before death.

  • For CLTs: deduct lifetime tax paid. For PETs: no lifetime tax was paid, so no deduction.

  • Then compute the death estate (see Section 7).

🔑 Important: NRB is at date of DEATH, not date of gift

For all death calculations (on both lifetime gifts and the death estate), always use the NRB at the date of death — currently £325,000. The NRB at the time the gift was made is only relevant for lifetime tax on CLTs.

Worked Example — PET Becoming Chargeable on Death

Tony makes a PET of £500,000 on 10 July 2023. A CLT of £120,000 (net of AEs) was made on 14 April 2017. Tony dies in 2025.

💀 Tony — Death Tax on PET (10 July 2023)
Transfer of value (PET)500,000
NRB at death 2025/26325,000
Less: GCTs in 7 years before this gift (10.6.2016–10.6.2023)
CLT on 14.4.2017 (within that window)(120,000)
NRB available(205,000)
Taxable amount295,000
IHT payable (£295,000 × 40%)£118,000
The earlier PET (Feb 2017) fell more than 7 years before death — it is completely ignored. The CLT on 14.4.2017 is only relevant because it is within 7 years of the PET on 10.7.2023 — it eats into the NRB available for that PET.

6Taper Relief

If the donor survives more than 3 years after making a PET or CLT, taper relief reduces the IHT payable on death. It does NOT reduce the chargeable amount — only the tax.

Years Between Gift and Death% Reduction of TaxEffective Tax Rate on Excess
0 – 3 years0%40%
More than 3 but less than 4 years20%32%
More than 4 but less than 5 years40%24%
More than 5 but less than 6 years60%16%
More than 6 but less than 7 years80%8%
🧠 Memory Hook — Taper
0 / 20 / 40 / 60 / 80 = reduction at each year milestone after year 3.
Think: "Every year after 3, I get 20% more relief."
⚠️ Critical Point — Taper Only Applies if Tax Actually Arises

Taper only matters if the gift exceeds the available NRB. If the gift falls entirely within the NRB, there is no tax and therefore no taper to apply. Wasting time on taper for NRB-covered gifts is a common timing mistake.

⚠️ Taper Reduces Tax — NOT the Chargeable Amount

This is frequently misunderstood. The full chargeable amount still goes into the NRB accumulation for subsequent gifts. Only the tax payable is reduced by taper.

⚡ For CLTs at Death — Credit for Lifetime Tax

After applying taper, deduct lifetime IHT already paid. The result must not be negative (no refunds). If the death tax (after taper) is less than lifetime tax paid, the additional tax payable is simply nil.

8Nil Rate Band — Transfer Between Spouses

When a spouse/civil partner dies, any unused percentage of their NRB can be transferred to the surviving spouse. This can potentially double the surviving spouse's NRB.

How It Works — Step by Step

  • Calculate the NRB actually used by the first spouse on death — this includes specific legacies (not to spouse) and PETs/CLTs within 7 years.

  • Calculate the unused percentage = (NRB − amount used) ÷ NRB at first death × 100%.

  • Apply that percentage to the NRB at the date of the second spouse's death.

  • Add this to the surviving spouse's own NRB.

🔑 Use the Percentage, Not the Amount

It is the unused percentage that is transferred, not the pound amount. This means if the NRB has changed between the two deaths, the surviving spouse benefits from the higher current NRB.

Worked Example

Nadia died when the NRB was £255,000. Half her estate (£275,400 / 2 = £137,700) was used to pay IHT (the other half went to her husband, exempt). Her husband Tareq dies in 2025/26 when NRB is £325,000.

👫 Transferable NRB Calculation
NRB at Nadia's death255,000
NRB used on Nadia's death (£275,400 × 50%)(137,700)
Unused NRB on Nadia's death117,300
Unused % = £117,300 / £255,00046%
Tareq's own NRB in 2025/26325,000
Transferred NRB (£325,000 × 46%)149,500
Total NRB available to Tareq£474,500
ℹ️ Note

If the first spouse left their entire estate to the surviving spouse (fully exempt), then 100% of the NRB is unused and can be transferred in full. This is the most common scenario in exams.

9Residence Nil Rate Band (RNRB)

The RNRB is an additional nil rate band of £175,000 available on death when:

  • The estate includes a residential property (or has included one at some point — i.e. the deceased has at some point lived in it as their home).

  • The property is left to direct descendants — children, grandchildren, step-children, or their spouses.

ScenarioRNRB Available?Notes
House left to children Yes Standard application. RNRB = £175,000.
House left to siblings/friends No Siblings are not direct descendants.
No main residence in estate at death Transferred only Own RNRB cannot be used but unused % can still be transferred to surviving spouse.
RNRB restricted by property value Partially If net value of residence < £175,000, RNRB is capped at the net value.
Spouse left entire estate to surviving spouse Transferred 100% 100% of RNRB transfers even if no property at that death. The surviving spouse's total RNRB = £350,000.
⚡ Exam Note — RNRB Not Reduced by Lifetime Gifts

Unlike the main NRB, the RNRB is not reduced by lifetime gifts made within 7 years of death. It always starts at the full £175,000 (subject to the property value cap).

Transfer of Unused RNRB from Spouse

Works exactly like the transferable NRB — the unused percentage is applied to the RNRB at the date of the surviving spouse's death. If the first spouse had 100% unused RNRB, the survivor gets both their own RNRB (£175,000) and the transferred RNRB (£175,000) = £350,000 total.

10IHT Planning Techniques

The exam regularly asks you to explain or evaluate basic IHT planning. Here are the key techniques you must know.

1. Make Lifetime Gifts (PETs)

Gifts made more than 7 years before death are fully exempt. Even if the donor dies within 7 years, taper relief reduces the tax after 3 years. Lock in asset value — future growth escapes IHT.

2. Skip a Generation

Leaving assets directly to grandchildren rather than children avoids a second charge to IHT when children pass those assets on. Tax is only levied once before grandchildren inherit.

3. Use Exemptions Efficiently

Annual exemptions (£3,000/year) and small gifts (£250/person) reduce the estate over time. Regular gifts from income can be made without limit if conditions are met.

4. Spouse Exemption

Gifts between spouses are fully exempt. Combined NRBs of £650,000 (plus up to £350,000 RNRB = £1 million potential) can pass to children tax-free through proper planning.

Calculating IHT Savings from Lifetime Giving

A common Section C question asks to compare: (a) keeping the asset until death vs (b) giving it away in lifetime.

📋 IHT Saving Calculation Framework
IHT if asset kept in estate at death (40% × asset value)£X
Less: Death tax on lifetime gift (after taper relief)(£X)
Less: Lifetime IHT paid on CLT (if applicable)(£X)
Net IHT saving from making the lifetime gift£X
Remember: the value of the gift is fixed at the time it was made — future growth in the asset's value is outside the estate and saves IHT.
✅ Exam Tip — Three Planning Points to Know

1. Even without 7-year survival, taper relief reduces IHT after 3 years.
2. The value is frozen at date of gift — future appreciation escapes IHT.
3. Skipping a generation prevents double taxation of the same assets.

11Payment of IHT — Administration Rules

This area regularly appears in OT questions. Learn these rules cold — they are easy marks if you know them.

SituationWho PaysDue Date
Lifetime CLT — Tax on making the gift Primarily the Donor (unless the trust agrees to pay) Later of: (i) 6 months after end of month of transfer, or (ii) 30 April after end of tax year of transfer
Death tax on a PET The Donee (recipient of the gift) 6 months after end of month of death
Death tax on a CLT (top-up) The Trustees of the trust (the donee) 6 months after end of month of death

The "Due Date" Rule for CLT Lifetime Tax — Worked Out

Gift made on 15 January 2026 (tax year 2025/26):

Rule (i): 6 months after end of month

End of January 2026 = 31 January 2026
+ 6 months = 31 July 2026

Rule (ii): 30 April after year end

Tax year 2025/26 ends 5 April 2026
30 April after that = 30 April 2027

ℹ️ Due Date

Take the later of the two = 30 April 2027. This is why gifts between 6 October and 5 April use rule (ii) as the later date.

🔑 Simple Version of the "Later Of" Rule

If the gift is made between 6 April and 30 September → rule (i) (6 months from month end) is typically later.
If the gift is made between 1 October and 5 April → rule (ii) (30 April after tax year end) is typically later.

12Diminution in Value — Share Gifts

When an individual gifts shares in a private company, the transfer of value may be greater than the market value of the shares given. This is because majority shareholdings command a premium price per share. By giving shares away, the donor moves from a majority position to a minority position, so each remaining share is worth less.

Formula

Formula

Transfer of value = (Shares before gift × price before) − (Shares after gift × price after)

Worked Example

Dominic owns 7,500 shares (75%) in Halder Ltd at £20/share. He gifts 3,000 shares. After the gift his remaining 4,500 shares (45%) are worth only £8/share.

📊 Diminution in Value — Dominic
Value of estate before transfer (7,500 × £20)150,000
Less: Value of estate after transfer (4,500 × £8)(36,000)
Transfer of value (loss to estate)£114,000
Note: The market value of the shares gifted was only 3,000 × £8 = £24,000. But the IHT transfer of value is £114,000 because of the loss in value of the remaining shares.
⚠️ Exam Trap

Students often use the market value of the shares given away as the transfer of value. This is wrong. Always use the diminution in the donor's estate.

14Common Exam Traps & Examiner Tips

🚨 Trap 1 — Using NRB at Gift Date for Death Calculations

For all death calculations (death tax on gifts AND the death estate), always use the NRB at date of death. The NRB at the time the gift was made is only used for CLT lifetime tax.

🚨 Trap 2 — Including PETs in Lifetime NRB Calculations

When calculating lifetime tax on a CLT, PETs in the prior 7 years do NOT reduce the NRB. Only prior CLTs matter at the lifetime stage.

🚨 Trap 3 — Deducting AEs from the Death Estate

Annual exemptions only apply to lifetime gifts. Never deduct the £3,000 AE from the death estate.

🚨 Trap 4 — Taper Reduces Tax, Not the Chargeable Amount

Taper relief reduces the IHT payable on death. It does not reduce the value of the gift for NRB accumulation purposes. The full GCT still eats into the NRB for subsequent gifts.

🚨 Trap 5 — Wasting Time on Taper When No Tax Arises

If a gift is fully within the NRB, there is no IHT on it — taper is irrelevant. Examiner reports repeatedly note that students apply taper relief unnecessarily, wasting exam time.

🚨 Trap 6 — Who Pays IHT Confusion

Death tax on PETs → donee. Death tax on CLTs → trustees. Death estate → personal representatives. Mix these up and you drop marks on easy administration questions.

🚨 Trap 7 — Non-Enforceable Debts in the Estate

Promises to pay (not legally binding), debts already paid, and IHT itself are not deductible from the death estate. Only legally enforceable debts outstanding at date of death are deductible.

⭐ Examiner's Favourite: Check if Gift is Within 7 Years of Death First

Before doing any calculation on a lifetime gift, always check: is it within 7 years of death? If not, for a PET → ignore. For a CLT → no death charge, but it still affects the NRB available for later gifts that ARE within 7 years.

✅ Efficient Exam Technique

Read the scenario first and mark each gift: (1) > 7 years from death? (2) PET or CLT? This takes 30 seconds and prevents you spending time on calculations that aren't needed.
