Overview of CGT Reliefs

There are two types of tax-saving tools in CGT. Understanding which category a relief belongs to is the first step to applying it correctly in the exam.

CategoryWhat it doesEffectReliefs in scope
Deferral reliefsPostpone the gain — it becomes payable laterDeferRollover relief, Gift holdover relief
Reduction reliefsReduce the rate of tax on the gainReduce rateBADR (14%), Investors' Relief (14%)
ExemptionsEliminate the gain entirelyExemptPRR (full or partial), AEA £3,000, exempt assets
⚠️ Exam Trap — Order Matters!

Always apply reliefs in this order:

  1. Exemptions first (PRR, AEA)
  2. Then check deferral reliefs (rollover, holdover)
  3. Then check rate-reduction reliefs (BADR, IR)

You cannot apply BADR to a gain that has already been held over. And once a gain is deferred, you pay the tax later — usually at standard rates (not BADR) unless conditions are re-met on the replacement asset disposal.

Rollover Relief

Think of it like this: You sell a business asset and make a big gain. Instead of paying CGT now, you "roll" the gain into the cost of the new asset. Tax is deferred until you eventually sell the new asset.

Conditions — All 5 Must Be Met

1
Both assets must qualify

The old AND new asset must both be qualifying assets (see table below).

2
Used in trade

Both assets must be used for trading purposes (not investment).

3
Timing window

New asset must be acquired: 1 year before OR 3 years after the date of disposal of the old asset.

4
Full reinvestment for full relief

ALL of the proceeds must be reinvested for full rollover. Partial reinvestment = partial deferral.

5
Same business

The assets must be used in the same trade (individuals) or by the same company or group.

Qualifying Assets for Rollover Relief

AssetQualifies?Notes
Land and buildings✔ YesMost common exam asset
Fixed plant & machinery✔ YesFixed only — must not be movable
Goodwill✔ YesBut only for sole traders/partnerships — NOT for companies
Movable plant & machinery✘ NoClassic exam trap — movable P&M does NOT qualify
Shares✘ NoShares never qualify for rollover relief
Inventory / stock✘ NoNot a fixed asset
⚠️ Exam Trap — Movable vs Fixed Plant!

The exam frequently lists movable plant and machinery as an answer option to catch you out. Always check — only fixed plant qualifies.

How Rollover Relief Works — The Mechanics

SituationEffect on gainEffect on new asset cost
Full reinvestment (proceeds fully spent)Entire gain deferredCost reduced by full gain deferred
Partial reinvestment (some proceeds not spent)Gain chargeable = lower of: (a) unspent proceeds or (b) total gainCost reduced by gain deferred (= total gain − gain chargeable now)
No reinvestmentFull gain chargeable immediatelyNo adjustment — claim not possible
📝 Example 1 — Full Rollover Relief

Jasmine sells her factory (a qualifying asset) for £270,000 on 1 March 2025. The factory cost £231,000. She buys a replacement warehouse for £290,000 (fully reinvested, acquired within 3 years).

Gain on Factory
Proceeds£270,000
Less: Cost(£231,000)
Chargeable gain before rollover£39,000
Less: Rollover relief (full — all proceeds reinvested)(£39,000)
Gain chargeable NOW£NIL
Base Cost of Replacement Warehouse
Actual cost paid£290,000
Less: Gain rolled over(£39,000)
Base cost for future sale£251,000
Result: No CGT now. When Jasmine eventually sells the warehouse, she will have a higher gain due to the reduced base cost — the tax is deferred, not cancelled.
📝 Example 2 — Partial Rollover Relief (Proceeds Not Fully Reinvested)

Tariq sells his office premises for £184,000 making a gain of £38,600. He buys a replacement for £143,000. He does NOT fully reinvest all the proceeds.

Partial Rollover Calculation
Total chargeable gain£38,600
Proceeds NOT reinvested (£184,000 − £143,000)£41,000
Gain chargeable NOW = lower of gain (£38,600) OR unspent proceeds (£41,000)£38,600
Gain deferred (rollover)£NIL
Key insight: Since the unspent proceeds (£41,000) exceed the total gain (£38,600), the entire gain is immediately chargeable and no rollover is possible! The formula always uses the lower of the two amounts as the immediately chargeable portion.
📝 Example 3 — Partial Rollover (Some Relief Available)

Sara sells her factory for £300,000 making a gain of £90,000. She reinvests £240,000 in a new qualifying asset.

Partial Rollover
Total gain£90,000
Proceeds NOT reinvested (£300,000 − £240,000)£60,000
Gain chargeable NOW = lower of: £90,000 or £60,000£60,000
Gain deferred (£90,000 − £60,000)£30,000
Adjusted Base Cost of New Asset
Actual cost£240,000
Less: Gain deferred(£30,000)
Base cost for future CGT computation£210,000

Rollover Relief — Time Limit to Claim

⏰ Deadline

4 years from the end of the later of:

  • The accounting period in which the old asset was sold, OR
  • The accounting period in which the new asset was acquired

Gift Holdover Relief

Think of it like this: You give away a business asset. There are no actual proceeds, but HMRC deems the disposal at market value — creating a large paper gain. Gift holdover relief lets you "hold over" (defer) that gain. The donor pays no CGT now; the donee inherits a lower base cost and pays CGT when they eventually sell.

📌 Key Principle

Gift holdover relief does NOT eliminate the gain — it transfers the tax liability from the donor to the donee by reducing the donee's base cost.

Donee's base cost = Market value at gift date − holdover relief claimed

Qualifying Assets for Gift Holdover Relief

Asset typeQualifies?Notes
Assets used in a trade/profession/vocation (business assets)✔ YesE.g. factory, equipment used in sole trader's business
Shares/securities in unquoted trading companies✔ YesCompany must be a trading company (not investment)
Shares/securities in quoted trading companies (if donor owns ≥5%)✔ Yes (restricted)Donor must hold at least 5% of voting rights
Agricultural property✔ YesWithin scope for exam
Shares in non-trading / investment companies✘ NoCompany must be trading
A private residence (main home)✘ NoHoldover does not apply to personal use assets
⚠️ Exam Trap — "No Minimum Ownership Period"

Gift holdover relief has no minimum ownership period — unlike BADR which requires 2 years. This is frequently tested in the exam as a true/false question.

How Gift Holdover Relief Works

📝 Example 4 — Simple Gift Holdover (Full Relief)

Faisal gifts shares in his unquoted trading company to his daughter Leila. Market value = £150,000. Faisal's original cost = £40,000. Both make a joint claim for holdover relief.

Faisal (Donor)
Deemed proceeds (MV)£150,000
Less: Cost(£40,000)
Gain before relief£110,000
Less: Gift holdover relief(£110,000)
Gain chargeable now£NIL
Leila (Donee) — Adjusted Base Cost
Market value at date of gift£150,000
Less: Gain held over(£110,000)
Leila's base cost for future sale£40,000

When Leila later sells, her gain will be: Proceeds − £40,000. The deferred gain is now "locked in" to her cost.

Gift Holdover + Sale at Undervalue

When the donor sells at BELOW market value (but above cost), not all of the gain can be held over. The excess of actual proceeds over cost is immediately chargeable.

📝 Example 5 — Sale at Undervalue (Partial Holdover)

Michael gifts shares in Minnow Ltd (unquoted trading company) to his son but charges him £53,000 (below MV of £98,000). Michael's cost = £41,500.

Michael (Donor)
Deemed proceeds (MV)£98,000
Less: Cost(£41,500)
Gain before relief£56,500
Less: Gift holdover relief (W)(£45,000)
Gain chargeable now (actual proceeds − cost = £53,000 − £41,500)£11,500
Rule: The immediately chargeable gain = actual proceeds received MINUS cost. The rest can be deferred via holdover relief.

Restriction — Company with Non-Business Assets

If the shares are in a personal trading company (i.e. donor owns ≥5%) AND the company owns non-business (investment) assets, gift holdover relief is restricted:

Formula — Restricted Holdover Relief

Holdover relief = Total gain × (Chargeable Business Assets / Total Chargeable Assets)

Chargeable Business Assets = assets used in the trade. Investment assets are excluded from the numerator.

📝 Example 6 — Restricted Gift Holdover (Company with Investments)

Jerome (owns 60% of Minnow Ltd, an unquoted trading company) gifts shares. Gain before relief = £92,500. Company's chargeable business assets = £200,000. Total chargeable assets = £250,000.

Restricted Holdover
Gain × Business assets / Total assets£92,500 × £200,000 / £250,000
Gift holdover relief available£74,000
Gain immediately chargeable (£92,500 − £74,000)£18,500

Joint Claim Requirement

📌 Both Donor AND Donee Must Claim
  • Both parties must jointly elect for gift holdover relief.
  • Claim deadline: 4 years from the end of the tax year in which the gift is made.
  • E.g. Gift made in 2025/26 → claim by 5 April 2030.

Business Asset Disposal Relief (BADR)

Think of it like this: You've built a business. When you sell it, HMRC rewards you for your entrepreneurship with a lower CGT rate of just 14% — instead of 18% or 24%.

📌 BADR at a Glance — FA2025
  • Rate: 14% on qualifying gains (up from 10% in prior years)
  • Lifetime limit: £1,000,000 per individual
  • Once the £1m is used up, any further gains are taxed at standard CGT rates (18%/24%)

BADR — Type 1: Disposal of an Unincorporated Business (or part of it)

1
Trading business

The business must be a trading business — not an investment business.

2
2 years' ownership

The business must have been owned by the individual for at least 2 years prior to disposal.

3
Whole or substantial part

You must dispose of the whole business OR a substantial part (not just an individual asset).

⚠️ Exam Trap — Disposal of Single Asset vs Business

Selling a single business asset (e.g. a warehouse the business no longer needs) does NOT qualify for BADR. You need to dispose of the business or a substantial part of it. Rollover relief, however, IS available for individual business asset sales.

📝 Example 7 — BADR on Sole Trader Business Disposal

Michael sells his wholesale business. Assets disposed of:

BADR Computation — 2025/26
Goodwill£60,000
Freehold property£64,000
Storage unit (loss)(£13,000)
Net chargeable gains qualifying for BADR£111,000
CGT @ 14%£15,540
Key rule: Losses on assets within the same qualifying BADR disposal are netted against the gains. You cannot separate them out.

BADR — Type 2: Disposal of Shares in a Personal Trading Company

1
Trading company

The company must be a trading company (or holding company of a trading group).

2
At least 5% shareholding

The individual must hold at least 5% of the ordinary share capital AND at least 5% of the voting rights.

3
Employee or officer

The individual must be an employee or director of the company. Does not need to be full-time.

4
2 years

All 3 conditions above must have been met for at least 2 years before the disposal date.

🧠 Mnemonic — BADR Conditions for Shares: "TESTT"
T · E · S · T · T
  • Trading company
  • Employee or director (any role — not just full-time)
  • Shares = minimum 5%
  • Two year holding period
  • Taxed at 14% (up to £1m lifetime)
📝 Example 8 — BADR on Share Disposal (Pass vs Fail)

Four individuals each sell shares in Cinnamon Ltd, an unquoted trading company:

PersonShareholdingRolePeriod heldBADR?Reason
Innocent20%Managing Director5 years✔ YesAll conditions met: 5%+, officer, 2 years
Amin10%Investor only6 years✘ NoNever worked for the company — fails employee condition
Camilla15%Director3 months✘ NoHas not held shares for 2 years
Dimitri3%Director4 years✘ NoBelow 5% threshold

BADR — Does it Need Enough Lifetime Limit?

📌 Partial BADR is Possible

If only some of the lifetime limit remains, BADR applies to that portion at 14%. The remainder is taxed at 18% or 24%.

Example: Lifetime limit used = £600,000. Gain this year = £700,000. BADR applies to £400,000 at 14%. Remaining £300,000 taxed at 18%/24%.

BADR Claim Deadline

⏰ Deadline

Claim by 1st anniversary of 31 January following the tax year of disposal.

Example: Disposal in 2025/26 → claim by 31 January 2028.

Investors' Relief (IR)

Think of it like this: BADR rewards business owners who work in their company. Investors' Relief rewards external investors who put money into new trading companies but don't work there.

📌 Key Facts — Investors' Relief FA2025
  • Rate: 14% (same as BADR)
  • Lifetime limit: £1,000,000 (separate from BADR lifetime limit)
  • The £1m limit was reduced from £10m effective 30 October 2024 — the exam uses the new £1m limit

IR — Conditions

ConditionRequirementBADR equivalent?
Company typeUnlisted trading company (or holding company of trading group). AIM shares count as unlisted.Same
New shares onlyThe investor must have subscribed for (i.e. received newly issued shares). Buying shares from an existing shareholder does NOT count.No equivalent — BADR allows secondary purchases
Holding periodShares must have been held for at least 3 years (longer than BADR's 2 years)BADR = 2 years
Must NOT be an employee/officerThe investor must not be an employee, director, or officer of the companyOpposite of BADR!
Date of issueShares must have been issued on or after 17 March 2016N/A
⚠️ Classic Exam Trap — Employee Cannot Claim IR

Even if all other conditions are met, an employee or director of the company cannot claim IR. They should look at BADR instead. If they don't meet BADR conditions either (e.g. under 5%), they get no relief at all.

Private Residence Relief (PRR)

Think of it like this: Your main home is generally not taxed when you sell it. PRR shelters the proportion of your ownership period during which the house was your main residence.

📌 PRR Formula

PRR = Total gain × (Qualifying months / Total months of ownership)

Qualifying months = Periods of actual occupation + Deemed occupation periods

The Final 9 Months Rule

📌 Last 9 Months — Always Exempt

The final 9 months of ownership are always treated as qualifying (deemed occupation), provided the property was the individual's main residence at some point during ownership.

This applies even if the individual is not actually living there in the final 9 months — for example, if they've moved into a new home before selling the old one.

Periods of Deemed Occupation (Exempt Even Though Not Living There)

Absence reasonMax period coveredCondition
Any reason (the "3-year rule")Up to 3 years totalMust be preceded AND followed by actual occupation
Working overseas (any employer)UnlimitedMust be preceded AND followed by actual occupation
Required to work elsewhere in UKUp to 4 yearsMust be preceded AND followed by actual occupation
Final 9 months9 monthsProperty must have been main residence at some point
⚠️ Critical Rule — "Followed by Occupation"

The "any reason" and "working elsewhere" periods only count as exempt if the individual actually returns to live in the property after the absence. If the property is sold without returning, those periods are NOT deemed occupation (except the final 9 months which always applies regardless).

Lily bought her house in April 2012 and sold it in March 2026 (168 months total). Her occupation history:

Apr 2017 – Mar 201924 monthsEmployed overseas (followed by actual occ)✔24

Apr 2020 – Jun 202563 monthsEmpty — NOT followed by occupation✘ (except final 9)9 (final months)

Multiple Residences — Election

📌 Two Homes at the Same Time

If an individual owns two homes simultaneously, they can elect which one is their "main residence" for PRR purposes. The election must be made within 2 years of acquiring the second property. If no election is made, HMRC decides on the facts.

Business Use of Home

📌 Exclusive Business Use — PRR Restricted

If part of the property is used exclusively for business at all times, PRR is restricted proportionately. For example, if 1 room out of 4 is always a dedicated office, only 3/4 of the gain qualifies for PRR.

However, the final 9-month exempt period applies to the whole property only for rooms that have been used residentially at some point. If one room was always business-only, the final 9 months does NOT apply to that portion.

Letting Relief

Letting relief reduces the chargeable gain arising from periods when a property was let out, but only if the owner was also living in the property at the same time.

⚠️ Critical — Shared Occupancy Required

Letting relief is only available if the owner lives in part of the property while the rest is let. If the owner moves out entirely and rents the whole property, letting relief is NOT available.

Letting Relief — The Three-Way Cap

Letting relief is the lowest of three amounts:

#AmountNotes
1Maximum statutory limit: £40,000Hard cap — cannot exceed this
2PRR already claimedLetting relief cannot exceed the amount of PRR given
3The gain attributable to the letting periodYou can't claim relief for more gain than actually arose during letting

Masuma owns a 10-room house. She always lives in 6 rooms. She has let 4 rooms throughout her 10-year ownership. Gain before reliefs = £120,000.

Gain after PRR£48,000
Step 2 — Letting Relief (lowest of 3)

③ Gain on letting = £48,000£48,000
Letting relief = lowest → £40,000

CGT Planning & Interaction of Reliefs

Gift Holdover + BADR — A Critical Interaction

📌 The Key Conflict

If holdover relief is claimed on a gift, the gain is deferred into the donee's base cost. When the donee sells, they will not normally qualify for BADR (unless they independently meet all BADR conditions — e.g. have worked in the company for 2 years, own 5%+).

Therefore, it may be better for the donor to NOT claim holdover relief, so the donor can claim BADR on the full gain at 14%. This is a frequent exam planning question.

📝 Example 12 — Holdover vs No Holdover: Which is Better?

Bill gifts shares in High Rise Ltd (qualifying for BADR) to daughter Belle. Gain = £148,000. Bill's cost = £112,000.

Belle sells the shares shortly after for £262,000. Belle has held the shares for less than 2 years — she does NOT qualify for BADR.

Option A: Holdover claimedOption B: No holdover
Bill's CGT£3,033 (only on restricted gain, taxed at 14% BADR)£20,300 (full gain at BADR 14%)
Belle's CGT£29,360 (large gain, 24% — no BADR)£0 (tiny gain — proceeds almost = cost)
Total CGT£32,393£20,300
Best option?✘ Higher total tax✔ Saves £12,093 overall
Lesson: From a family total perspective, Option B (no holdover, Bill uses BADR) saves £12,093. However, Bill personally may prefer Option A because his own bill is lower — the best answer depends on whose perspective you're asked about.

Spouse Transfers — CGT Planning

📌 Why Transfer to a Spouse Before Selling?
  • Transfers between spouses = no gain/no loss → no CGT on transfer
  • The receiving spouse gets their own AEA (£3,000) and their own basic rate band
  • If one spouse has remaining BRB, the gain taxed at 18% instead of 24%
  • If one spouse has CGT losses, they can be used

Example: Splitting ownership 50:50 before selling a property could save significant CGT by using both partners' AEAs and lower bands.

Annual Exempt Amount (AEA) — Don't Waste It

Tax year 2025/26Amount
Annual Exempt Amount per individual£3,000
CompaniesNo AEA — companies do not get AEA
📌 Rules for AEA
  • AEA is applied after current year losses
  • AEA is applied before brought-forward losses
  • AEA cannot be carried forward — use it or lose it each year
  • Brought-forward losses are restricted so as NOT to waste AEA

Summary Comparison — All Reliefs

Rollover Relief
  • Defers gain on business asset sales
  • Must reinvest in qualifying asset within window
  • Partial reinvestment = partial relief
  • Applies to individuals & companies
  • No minimum ownership period
Gift Holdover Relief
  • Defers gain on gift of business asset
  • Joint claim by donor and donee
  • No min ownership period
  • Restricted if company has non-business assets
  • Conflict with BADR — plan carefully
BADR
  • 14% rate on qualifying gains
  • £1m lifetime limit per person
  • Must be employee/director + 5%+ shares + 2 years
  • Whole/substantial business or share disposal
  • Individuals only
Investors' Relief
  • 14% rate, separate £1m lifetime limit
  • Must subscribe for NEW shares (not buy secondhand)
  • Must NOT be employee or officer
  • Hold for 3 years (longer than BADR)
  • Unlisted trading company shares only
