A · The UK Tax System & Administration ~10% · easy marks

Self Assessment — Individuals (the dates that earn easy marks)

EventDeadline
Notify chargeability to HMRC5 Oct after the tax year
Paper return31 Oct
Online return + pay balancing payment31 Jan after tax year
1st Payment on Account (POA)31 Jan (in the tax year)
2nd Payment on Account31 July (after tax year)
Keep records (business)5 years after 31 Jan filing date
"Jan–July–Jan" = the POA rhythm. Each POA = 50% of last year's income tax + Class 4 NIC. Capital gains tax & Class 2 NIC are NOT in POAs — they're only in the 31 Jan balancing payment.
In past attempts you forgot to state the POA due dates explicitly. Always write "31 January" and "31 July" — those words are the marks.

Penalties & Interest

Late filing

  • £100 fixed (immediately)
  • >3 months: £10/day (max 90 days)
  • >6 / >12 months: 5% of tax (each)

Incorrect return — penalty = % of lost tax × behaviour

  • Careless: up to 30%
  • Deliberate: up to 70%
  • Deliberate + concealed: up to 100%

Unprompted disclosure cuts the penalty hardest.

A student files late, shrugs "it's only £100." Three months later the £10/day meter has been running and a 5% slice is coming. Penalties escalate — that's the exam point.

B · Income Tax & National Insurance biggest area

The Income Tax Computation — always the same skeleton

Pool income into three columns: Non-savings → Savings → Dividends. Deduct Personal Allowance. Tax in that order (NSI first, then savings, then dividends). Each band fills up before the next rate applies.
Income typeRates (FA2025 ref)
Non-savings & savings20% basic / 40% higher (>£50,270) / 45% additional (>£125,140)
Dividends8.75% / 33.75% / 39.35%
  • Personal Allowance £12,570, tapered £1 for every £2 of Adjusted Net Income > £100,000 → gone at £125,140.
  • Savings starting rate band £5,000 @ 0% — only useful if non-savings income is low.
  • Personal Savings Allowance: £1,000 (basic), £500 (higher), £0 (additional).
  • Dividend allowance £500 @ 0% (uses up the band though).
PA taper: "£100k is the cliff edge." The effective marginal rate in the £100k–£125,140 zone is ~60% because you lose PA and pay 40%.

Extending the basic-rate band

Gross personal pension and Gift Aid contributions extend the basic rate band (and higher-rate threshold) by the gross amount. They don't reduce income — they widen the cheap band.
Pension/Gift Aid = "stretch the band, don't shrink the income." Gross up by ×100/80.

Employment Income & Benefits

BenefitTaxable amount
Company carList price × CO₂% (cash for cars). Fuel benefit = £27,800 × same %. Reduce car only for part-year/unavailability.
Living accommodationAnnual value + (cost − £75,000) × official rate, if cost > £75,000

Your recurring trap: you applied a private-use restriction to a company/employee asset. Private-use restriction belongs to sole traders only (it adjusts the owner's own capital allowances/expenses). An employee is taxed on the full benefit regardless of private use; a company claims full capital allowances.
Exempt staples to remember: pension contributions, ≤£500 employer pension advice, mobile phone (1), £6/week home-working, trivial benefits ≤£50, workplace parking, bikes-to-work.

Trading Income — Adjustment of Profit

Start with net profit → ADD BACK disallowed expenses & non-trade items in the accounts → DEDUCT income taxed elsewhere & allowable items not in accounts (e.g. capital allowances). The result is tax-adjusted trading profit.
Add back (disallowed)Deduct / allow
Owner's drawings, private use portion, depreciation, client entertaining, most legal fees on capital items, fines, donations (non-trade), >50g/km car lease ×15%Capital allowances, trade income wrongly in non-trade lines, deductible pre-trading expenses (7 yrs)
"Depreciation is the impostor" — always add it back; capital allowances are the real tax deduction.
A sole trader buys a van used 70% for business / 30% private. The private 30% is restricted because he owns it. If instead his company owned the van, no restriction — the company gets it all and the employee is taxed on a benefit. Same van, different owner, different rule.

Capital Allowances

  • AIA £1,000,000: 100% on plant & machinery (not cars). Time-apportion the limit for short periods.
  • Main pool 18% WDA (reducing balance). Special rate pool 6% (long-life, integral features, high-CO₂ cars).
  • FYA / Full expensing 100% for companies on new main-pool plant (not cars).
  • Cars: 0g/km → 100% FYA; ≤50g/km → main pool 18%; >50g/km → special pool 6%. No AIA on any car.
  • SBA 3% straight-line on commercial building cost — exclude land.
  • Small pools ≤£1,000 → write off in full.
Two traps you've hit: (1) forgetting to time-apportion the AIA/WDA for a short or long accounting period; (2) including land in SBA qualifying cost — land never qualifies.
"Cars get no AIA, ever." And SBA is the "building, not the ground" — strip out land.

"Carry forward = same trade. Sideways (s.64) = any income."
Watch the cap: loss relief against other income is capped at the greater of £50,000 or 25% of adjusted total income.

National Insurance — who pays what

ClassWhoOn / rate (FA2025 ref)
Class 1 primaryEmployee8% on £12,570–£50,270, 2% above
Class 1 secondaryEmployer15% above £5,000 threshold; Employment Allowance £10,500

Your NIC traps, all in one place:
• Pension contributions do NOT reduce trading profit for Class 4 NIC.
• Employment Allowance applies to Class 1 secondary only — not Class 1A.
• Single-director-only companies are excluded from the Employment Allowance.
• For part-year, do NOT annualise before pro-rating — apply the actual period's thresholds correctly.
"1 = the job, 4 = the trade, 1A = the perks." Employer pays for perks (1A) and the job (secondary); the employee pays primary.

Property Income & Pensions (quick)

Property

  • Cash basis default if receipts ≤ £150,000.
  • Finance/mortgage interest → 20% tax reducer, not a deduction.
  • Replacement of domestic items relief (no initial cost).
  • Rent-a-room: £7,500 exemption.

Pensions

  • Annual allowance £60,000 (tapered for high earners), 3-yr carry forward.
  • Tax relief on contributions up to higher of £3,600 or 100% of earnings.
  • Relief via band extension (above).

C · Chargeable Gains — Individuals

Per asset: Proceeds − cost − enhancement − selling costs = gain. Pool gains, deduct losses, deduct Annual Exempt Amount £3,000, then apply CGT rate. Losses b/f used only after AEA.
Asset / reliefRate (FA2025 ref)
Normal gains — basic rate band / higher18% / 24%
Business Asset Disposal Relief (BADR)14% — lifetime limit £1m
Investors' relief14% — lifetime limit £1m
Gains "sit on top" of taxable income — fill the remaining basic-rate band at 18%, the rest at 24%. BADR gains use the band first.

Reliefs — the four to know cold

ReliefTriggerEffect
PPRSale of main homeExempt; last 9 months always count; letting relief limited
BADRSell whole/part of business, owned 2 yrs14% rate
Gift (holdover) reliefGift of business assetDefers gain — rolls into donee's base cost
Rollover reliefSell & reinvest in qualifying business assetDefers gain into new asset's cost
Think of rollover relief as a "tax snowball" — you roll the gain forward into the new asset and it grows until you finally stop reinvesting and it melts (becomes taxable).

Special rules

Chattels

  • Cost & proceeds ≤ £6,000 → exempt.
  • Proceeds > £6,000, cost ≤ £6,000 → gain capped at 5/3 × (proceeds − £6,000).
  • Wasting chattels (life ≤50 yrs) → exempt.
"6,000 is the chattel cushion, and 5/3 is the cap rope."

Shares — matching for individuals

  1. Same day
  2. Next 30 days (bed & breakfast)
  3. Section 104 pool (average cost)

Bonus/rights issues attach to the pool.

D · Inheritance Tax

Track every gift on a 7-year rolling clock. Lifetime gifts use the Nil Rate Band first (oldest first); only the excess is taxed. On death, re-test gifts in the last 7 years, then tax the estate.
TransferLifetimeOn death within 7 yrs
PET (gift to individual)No taxTaxed if death within 7 yrs
CLT (gift to trust)20% (if donor pays) on excess over NRBRe-charged at 40%, less lifetime tax paid
Death estate—40% (36% if ≥10% to charity)
  • Nil Rate Band £325,000; transferable unused % to spouse.
  • Residence NRB £175,000 — main home to direct descendants; tapered if estate > £2m.
  • Taper relief on tax (not value): 3–4 yrs 20% off, 4–5 yrs 40%, 5–6 yrs 60%, 6–7 yrs 80%.
Exemptions to fire first: £3,000 annual (1 yr carry-back), £250 small gifts, marriage (£5,000 parent / £2,500 grandparent / £1,000 other), normal expenditure out of income, and unlimited spouse & charity.
Picture a candle (the NRB) burning down over 7 years. Each gift takes a chunk. Die, and the tax-man re-lights the candle for everything in the last 7 years — but taper relief shaves the tax the longer ago the gift was.
Taper relief reduces the tax, never the gross gift value — a classic mark-loser.

E · Corporation Tax

The computation & the rate decision

Trading profit (after capital allowances) + property + non-trade interest + chargeable gains − qualifying donations = TTP. Then test the rate using augmented profits.
Augmented profitsRate (FY2025 ref)
≤ £50,000 (lower limit)Small profits rate 19%
£50,001 – £250,00025% with marginal relief (3/200)
> £250,000 (upper limit)Main rate 25%

MR = 3/200 × (£250,000 − Augmented profits) × (TTP ÷ Augmented profits)

  • Augmented profits = TTP + non-group exempt dividends.
  • Associated companies divide both limits (÷ number of associates).
  • Limits are time-apportioned for short periods; a long period is split into 12m + remainder.
Your dividend trap, nailed down: intra-group dividends are excluded from BOTH TTP and augmented profits. Non-group exempt dividends are still excluded from TTP but ARE added to augmented profits — for rate banding only. And remember associates halve the band limits.
Marginal relief lives in the "50k–250k corridor." Below it = 19%; above = 25%; inside = 25% minus the 3/200 trim.

Company Losses

LossRelief
Trading lossCurrent year vs total profits; carry back 12 months; carry forward vs total profits (with restriction over £5m)
Capital lossOnly vs current/future chargeable gains

Groups — two different tests (don't mix them!)

Group relief group (75%)

  • ≥75% direct/indirect holding throughout.
  • Surrender current-year losses (trading, excess interest, etc.) between members.

Chargeable gains group

  • Each link ≥75%, but only >50% effective interest from the top.
  • Assets transferred at no gain/no loss; can match gains & losses.
Twice-recorded trap: do NOT apply the 75% group-relief test to a chargeable-gains group. Gains group needs each link ≥75% but only >50% effective overall.
And: brought-forward losses are NOT surrenderable for group relief — only current-year losses go sideways. (You've slipped on this before.)
"75% to lend a loss, 50%-effective to share a gain."

Company self assessment

  • File return & pay tax: 9 months + 1 day after period end (small/medium companies).
  • File return: within 12 months of period end.
  • Large companies (>£1.5m profit): pay by quarterly instalments.
"Nine-and-a-day" is the small-company pay date — easy to write, easy to lose if forgotten.

F · Value Added Tax

Registration & deregistration

  • Compulsory registration — historic test: taxable supplies > £90,000 in the last 12 months → register within 30 days, effective from the start of the next month. Future test: will exceed £90,000 in the next 30 days alone → register immediately.
  • Deregister if turnover expected < £88,000.
  • Voluntary registration lets you recover input tax — good if customers are VAT-registered or you make zero-rated supplies.
"90 in, 88 out." Register over 90k, leave under 88k.

Output vs input tax

  • Standard 20%, zero-rated 0% (recover input), exempt (no recovery).
  • Irrecoverable input VAT: client entertaining and cars with any private use (the purchase). Fuel can be recovered with a scale charge.
  • Tax point = basic (delivery), overridden by invoice/payment within 14 days.
  • Bad debt relief: debt >6 months old & written off.
"Cars and canapés can't be claimed" — the two famous blocked inputs (private-use cars & entertaining).

Schemes (when each helps)

SchemeThresholdWhy use it
Cash accounting≤£1.35mAccount for VAT on payment — automatic bad-debt relief, cash-flow help
Annual accounting≤£1.35mOne return a year + instalments — less admin
Flat rate≤£150,000Pay fixed % of gross turnover — simple, no input tracking
"Cash & Annual share the 1.35m gate; Flat rate is the 150k small-trader door."

Returns & penalties

  • Return & payment: 1 month + 7 days after quarter end.
  • Late submission = points-based system; threshold reached → £200 penalties.
  • Default surcharge replaced by separate late-payment penalties + interest.
VAT lateness is now like a driving licence — you collect points for each late return, and once you hit the limit the fines start. One slip is a warning; a habit gets punished.

⏱️ Final Exam-Day Technique

  • 1.8 minutes per mark. Section C 10-markers ≈ 18 mins — set the proforma skeleton first, then fill numbers.
  • Open the tax table page before you start — never recall a rate from memory.
  • Show workings as labelled W1, W2 — method marks survive arithmetic slips.
  • State dates and rules in words (POA dates, "no gain/no loss", "exempt") — they are marks.
  • Target 75 marks of confident attempts; don't over-invest in one part.
Your personal pre-flight checklist: private-use only for owners · time-apportion CAs · land out of SBA · b/f losses can't be surrendered · taper cuts tax not value · POA dates in words.
