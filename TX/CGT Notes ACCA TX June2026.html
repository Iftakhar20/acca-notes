C1

Scope of Capital Gains Tax

CGT is charged on the gain you make when you dispose of a chargeable asset. Think of it as: you bought something, sold it for more, and now HMRC wants a slice of that profit.

Who Pays CGT?

  • UK resident individuals — on worldwide disposals
  • Non-UK residents — only on disposals of UK land/property
  • CGT applies to individuals (not companies — they pay Corporation Tax on gains)
  • The tax year runs 6 April to 5 April

What is a "Disposal"?

A disposal includes: a sale, a gift, the destruction of an asset, or receiving compensation/insurance proceeds for a lost/damaged asset.

💡 Deemed Market Value Rule
When you give away an asset (a gift), there are NO actual proceeds — but HMRC treats the disposal as if it happened at market value. This prevents people giving away assets to avoid CGT.

Chargeable vs Exempt Assets

❌ EXEMPT Assets (no CGT)
  • Your main home (usually — see PRR)
  • Cars (including business cars)
  • ISA investments
  • Gilt-edged securities (government bonds)
  • Qualifying corporate bonds (QCBs)
  • Wasting chattels (life ≤ 50 years, e.g. a boat, racehorse)
  • Non-wasting chattels bought AND sold for ≤ £6,000
  • Cash
  • Foreign currency for personal use
  • Winnings (lottery, betting)
✅ CHARGEABLE Assets
  • Land and buildings
  • Shares and securities (not gilts/QCBs)
  • Business assets
  • Antiques, jewellery, art (non-wasting chattels)
  • Shares in unquoted companies
  • Second homes / investment properties
Example — Identifying Exempt vs Chargeable

Omar has the following disposals in 2025/26:

His main home (always lived in it)EXEMPT — Private Residence Relief
His car (used privately)EXEMPT — cars always exempt
A motorboat (bought £5k, sold £12k)EXEMPT — wasting chattel (life ≤ 50 yrs)
Government bonds (gilts)EXEMPT — gilt-edged securities
Shares in Tesco plcCHARGEABLE
A buy-to-let flatCHARGEABLE — not a main home
Antique vase bought £4k, sold £5.5kEXEMPT — both under £6,000
Antique vase bought £4k, sold £7kCHARGEABLE — proceeds exceed £6,000

Basic Principles of Computing Gains & Losses

The CGT Proforma

Every single CGT computation uses this proforma. Learn it until it's automatic:

Disposal proceeds (or Market Value if a gift)£ X
Less: Incidental costs of disposal (legal fees, agent fees)(X)
Net ProceedsX
Less: Acquisition cost (original purchase price)(X)
Less: Incidental costs of acquisition(X)
Less: Enhancement expenditure (capital improvements)(X)
Chargeable Gain / (Allowable Loss)X / (X)
⚠️ Revenue vs Capital Expenditure
Only capital expenditure can be deducted. Repairs and maintenance are revenue — they are NOT deductible in a CGT computation (they are deducted from rental/trading income instead).

E.g. Replacing a warehouse roof = revenue (repair) = NOT deductible in CGT. Adding an extension = capital = deductible.

Part Disposals

When you sell part of an asset (e.g. sell half of a field), you must apportion the original cost:

Allowable Cost = Total Cost × A / (A + B)
Where: A = Proceeds from the part disposed of
B = Market value of the part RETAINED
Example — Part Disposal

Priya bought a field for £60,000. She sells a portion for £30,000. The remaining part is worth £90,000.

Proceeds£30,000
A = £30,000 (proceeds); B = £90,000 (MV retained)
Allowable cost = £60,000 × £30,000/(£30,000+£90,000)(£15,000)
Chargeable gain£15,000

Damaged / Destroyed Assets & Insurance Proceeds

✅ Asset Damaged (not destroyed)

If you spend the insurance money on restoring the asset, you can elect to deduct the proceeds from the asset's cost instead of having a disposal. This defers the gain.

🏚️ Asset Destroyed

If the asset is destroyed and you receive insurance proceeds, it is treated as a disposal. Proceeds = insurance money. BUT if you reinvest in a replacement asset → Rollover relief may defer the gain.

Capital Losses

When you sell an asset for less than it cost, you make an allowable capital loss. Here are the key rules:

  1. Current year losses are set off against current year gains in full — even if this wastes the Annual Exempt Amount (AEA). You cannot choose to skip a current year loss.
  2. If there are still net gains after current year losses, then deduct the AEA (£3,000).
  3. Only THEN set off brought forward losses — but only enough to reduce taxable gains to nil. This means brought forward losses are restricted to preserve the AEA.
  4. Any unused brought forward losses carry forward indefinitely to future years.
Example — Capital Losses

In 2025/26, Hali has a chargeable gain of £7,000 and brought forward losses of £39,300 from 2023/24.

Chargeable gain£7,000
Less: AEA(£3,000)
Taxable gain before brought forward losses£4,000
Less: Capital losses b/f (restricted — only use £4,000)(£4,000)
Taxable gains = NIL ✔£0
Loss c/f (£39,300 − £4,000)£35,300

Key point: We only used £4,000 of the loss — enough to reduce taxable gain to zero. We did NOT use the full loss, because that would waste the £3,000 AEA.

⚠️ Current Year Loss Rule (Exam Trap!)
Goma has a gain of £6,900 and a current year loss of £9,100. The current year loss is set against the gain in full:

Gain £6,900 − Current year loss £6,900 = NIL. The AEA is wasted! Loss c/f = £9,100 − £6,900 = £2,200

Spouses / Civil Partners

  • Transfers between spouses (or civil partners) are on a "no gain, no loss" basis — no CGT arises on the transfer
  • The receiving spouse takes over the original cost of the asset
  • Each spouse gets their own AEA and their own basic rate band
  • Splitting an asset before disposal can save CGT by using both AEAs and lower rates
Example — Spouse Transfer (No Gain No Loss)

Jackson bought a villa for £115,000 in May 2014. He gifted it to his wife Sophia in 2020 when it was worth £100,000. Sophia later sold it for £159,000.

Sophia's proceeds£159,000
Less: Cost (Jackson's original cost — NOT the gift value of £100,000)(£115,000)
Chargeable gain£44,000
Less: AEA(£3,000)
Less: Capital losses b/f(£4,300)
Taxable gains£36,700
CGT payable (Sophia has taxable income > BRB, so 24%)£8,808

Gains & Losses on Movable & Immovable Property

What is a "Chattel"?

A chattel is any tangible, movable property — things you can physically touch and move. Examples: paintings, furniture, jewellery, antiques, boats.

Types of Chattels

TypeDefinitionExamplesCGT Treatment
Wasting Chattel Predictable life of ≤ 50 years Boats, racehorses, caravans, machinery ALWAYS EXEMPT — no CGT
Non-wasting Chattel Predictable life of > 50 years Antiques, paintings, jewellery, porcelain Depends on proceeds and cost (see below)

The £6,000 Chattel Rules

For non-wasting chattels, the treatment depends on whether proceeds and/or cost exceed £6,000:

SituationTreatment
Both proceeds AND cost ≤ £6,000 EXEMPT — No CGT
Cost ≤ £6,000 but proceeds > £6,000 Gain is capped at: 5/3 × (Proceeds − £6,000)
Cost > £6,000 but proceeds ≤ £6,000 (a loss) Calculate loss using deemed proceeds of £6,000 (reduces allowable loss)
Both cost AND proceeds > £6,000 Normal gain/loss computation — no special rules
Example — Chattel Rules (Non-Wasting)

Scenario A: Antique painting — cost £1,000, sold for £7,200

Actual gain (£7,200 − £1,000)£6,200
Maximum gain = 5/3 × (£7,200 − £6,000) = 5/3 × £1,200£2,000
Chargeable gain (the lower of actual vs maximum)£2,000

Scenario B: Antique vase — cost £22,000, sold for £5,500 (a loss)

Deemed proceeds (restricted to £6,000 minimum)£6,000
Less: Allowable costs (£22,000 + £800 acquisition fees)(£22,800)
Allowable loss(£16,800)
Without the £6,000 floor, loss would have been £17,100 — the rule reduces the loss

Scenario C: Antique brooch — cost £3,500, sold for £4,800

EXEMPT — both proceeds AND cost below £6,000£NIL

Private Residence Relief (PRR)

When you sell your main home, any gain is usually fully exempt under PRR. This is one of the most valuable CGT reliefs available.

The PRR Formula

PRR Exempt Amount = Gain × Exempt Months / Total Months of Ownership

Periods That Count as "Occupied" (Deemed Occupation)

PeriodDeemed Occupation?Conditions
Any period of actual occupationYESAlways counts
Last 9 months of ownershipYES (always)Only if property was the main residence at some point
Working abroad for an employerYES (unlimited)Must occupy before AND after (if still own at end)
Any reasonUP TO 3 YEARSMust occupy before AND after (if still own at end)
Working elsewhere in the UKUP TO 4 YEARSMust occupy before AND after
💡 The "Last 9 Months" Rule
The last 9 months of ownership are ALWAYS exempt — regardless of whether you are actually living there. This is because people often move out before the sale completes.

Note: If part of the house was always used for business, the last 9 months rule does NOT apply to that business portion.
Example — PRR with Periods Away

Angus bought his house on 31 March 2012. He moved out on 31 March 2026 (total ownership = 168 months = 14 years).

31.3.2012 – 31.3.2017 (60 months): Actual occupationExempt: 60 months
1.4.2017 – 31.3.2019 (24 months): Working overseasExempt: 24 months (working abroad — unlimited)
1.4.2019 – 31.3.2020 (12 months): Returned — actual occupationExempt: 12 months
1.4.2020 – 31.3.2026 (72 months): Left — emptyChargeable: 63 months; Last 9 months = Exempt
Total ownership168 months
Exempt months60+24+12+9 = 105 months
PRR = Gain × 105/16862.5% exempt

Letting Relief

Available when part of the property is let out while you also live there (shared occupancy). The relief is the lowest of:

  • £40,000
  • The amount of PRR
  • The gain attributed to the letting period
Example — PRR + Letting Relief

Masuma owned her house for 10 years. She always occupied 6 of the 10 rooms. The other 4 rooms were let throughout. Gain before reliefs = £120,000.

PRR = £120,000 × 6/10 (occupied portion only)£72,000
Gain remaining after PRR£48,000
Letting relief = lowest of: £40,000 / £72,000 / £48,000£40,000
Chargeable gain = £120,000 − £72,000 − £40,000£8,000

Gains & Losses on Shares & Securities

Valuing Quoted Shares Disposed of by Gift

If shares are gifted, use market value. For quoted (listed) shares, market value = the mid-price between the quoted buying and selling prices on the Stock Exchange on the date of disposal.

Example — Quoted Share Valuation

Shares in Tesco plc are quoted at 210p–220p on the date of gift. Market value = (210 + 220) / 2 = 215p per share.

Share Identification Rules — Individuals

When you buy shares over time, how do you know which shares you're selling? HMRC uses matching rules. Match disposals in this order:

PriorityMatch AgainstWhy It Matters
1st Shares acquired on the same day as the disposal Prevents same-day bed-and-breakfasting
2nd Shares acquired within the next 30 days after disposal Stops the "30-day bed & breakfast" avoidance
3rd Share pool (FA1985 pool — all other shares) Averaging of costs for longer-term holdings
📝 Exam Focus — "Bed & Breakfast" Anti-Avoidance
Without these rules, a person could sell shares to crystallise a loss, then buy them back the next day. The 30-day rule means: if you sell shares and buy the same company shares within 30 days, the repurchase is matched against the sale FIRST (not the pool). This cancels out the tax-planning strategy.

The Share Pool (FA1985 Pool)

The share pool is a running record of all shares held (other than same-day/30-day matches). It tracks both the number of shares and their cumulative cost.

  • Purchases: Add number and cost to the pool
  • Bonus issues: Add shares — no cost (free shares)
  • Rights issues: Add shares at the price paid
  • Disposal: Remove a proportion of shares and apportion cost
Cost of shares sold = Pool Cost × (Number sold / Total shares in pool)
Example — Share Pool with Bonus Issue

Mick owns shares in Stone Ltd:

June 2016: Bought 500,000 sharesCost £960,000
Dec 2021: Bonus issue 3:2 (500,000 × 3/2 = 750,000)Cost £NIL
Pool after bonus: 1,250,000 sharesTotal cost £960,000
Sept 2025: Sells 700,000 shares
Cost = £960,000 × 700,000/1,250,000£537,600
Balance c/f: 550,000 shares @ £422,400

Rights Issues

A rights issue is when the company offers existing shareholders the chance to buy more shares (often at a discount). Unlike a bonus issue, you actually pay for rights issue shares.

Example — Rights Issue in Share Pool

April 2014: Bought 40,000 shares @ £6.50 = £260,000. May 2017: Rights issue 1:4 @ £4 per share (buys 10,000 shares = £40,000).

April 2014 purchase40,000 shares | £260,000
May 2017 rights issue (1:4 × 40,000 = 10,000 @ £4)+10,000 shares | +£40,000
May 2021: Bonus issue 1:5 (50,000/5 = 10,000)+10,000 shares | £NIL
Pool total60,000 shares | £300,000
March 2026: Sells 12,500 shares → Cost = £300,000 × 12,500/60,000(12,500 shares) | (£62,500)

Takeovers and Reorganisations

When company A takes over company B, shareholders in B receive shares (and sometimes cash) in company A. The key rule is: no immediate disposal if only shares received. The original cost is carried forward into the new shares.

Takeover with Cash AND Shares

If consideration includes cash, a part disposal occurs on the cash element. The original cost is split in proportion to market values received.

Example — Takeover (Cash + Shares)

Hunter held shares in Moon Ltd (original cost £90,000). Riley plc takes over and offers: £3 cash per share + 2 Riley plc shares per Moon share. Hunter held 50,000 Moon shares.

Cash received (50,000 × £3)£150,000
Shares received (50,000 × 2 × £1.20)£120,000
Total consideration£270,000
Cost allocated to cash = £90,000 × £150,000/£270,000(£50,000)
Chargeable gain on cash element£100,000
Cost allocated to new shares = £90,000 × £120,000/£270,000 = £40,000 (carried forward)

Exempt Securities

  • Gilt-edged securities (UK government bonds) — always exempt
  • Qualifying Corporate Bonds (QCBs) — always exempt
💡 Exam Note
You only need to know that gilts and QCBs are exempt — you will NOT be asked to calculate gains on them or explain the rules in detail.

The Computation of Capital Gains Tax

CGT Rates — FA2025

The rates below are from the official FA2025 Tax Tables (applicable for June 2026 exam):

Type of GainLower RateHigher Rate
Most assets (shares, business assets, etc.)18% (within BRB)24% (above BRB)
Residential property (investment)18%24%
BADR / Investors' Relief gains14% (flat rate)

What determines 18% vs 24%?

The rate depends on whether the individual has any remaining basic rate band. The basic rate band for 2025/26 is £37,700.

⚠️ How to Find Remaining Basic Rate Band
Remaining BRB = £37,700 − Taxable income (after personal allowance)

Extended by: Gross pension contributions (divide net payment by 80%) or Gift Aid (divide net by 80%)

The Full CGT Computation Proforma

Chargeable gains in the year£ X
Less: Current year capital losses(X)
Net gainsX
Less: Annual Exempt Amount (AEA) — £3,000 (2025/26)(3,000)
Less: Brought forward capital losses (restricted)(X)
Taxable gainsX
Tax gains falling within remaining BRB × 18%X
Gains above BRB × 24%X
CGT Payable£ X
Example — Full CGT Computation (Standard)

Naomi had a chargeable gain of £20,700 in 2025/26 from selling a residential investment property. Taxable income = £29,700. She paid £2,000 net pension contributions.

Chargeable gain£20,700
Less: AEA(£3,000)
Taxable gains£17,700
Working: Remaining BRB
£37,700 + gross pension (£2,000 × 100/80 = £2,500) = £40,200 − taxable income £29,700 = £10,500 remaining BRB
First £10,500 × 18% (within BRB)£1,890
Remaining £7,200 × 24% (above BRB)£1,728
CGT Payable£3,618
Example — Brian (No BRB remaining)

Brian has employment income of £43,000 and a chargeable gain of £8,700 from selling an antique vase. 2025/26.

Chargeable gain£8,700
Less: AEA(£3,000)
Taxable gains£5,700
Remaining BRB = £37,700 − (£43,000 − £12,570 PA) = £37,700 − £30,430 = £7,270
All £5,700 taxable gains fall within remaining BRB
CGT = £5,700 × 18%£1,026

Tax Planning with Spouses

Splitting an asset between spouses before disposal can save CGT in two ways:

  • Use both AEAs: Each spouse gets £3,000 AEA — saving = £3,000 × CGT rate
  • Use lower rate bands: If spouse has unused basic rate band, their portion is taxed at 18% instead of 24%
Example — Spouse Planning

Albert (higher rate taxpayer) wants to sell a property. His wife Victoria has unused BRB of £16,260. If he puts 50% into Victoria's name first (no gain/no loss), the CGT saving is:

Gains falling into Victoria's BRB (£16,260)Tax saved at (24% − 18%) = 6%
CGT saving = £16,260 × 6%£976

Reliefs & Exemptions on Disposal of Capital Assets

Overview of Available Reliefs

Deferral Reliefs
  • Rollover Relief — business asset replaced
  • Gift Holdover Relief — asset gifted
  • EIS Deferral Relief (out of scope)
Reduction Reliefs
  • BADR — 14% rate on qualifying gains
  • Investors' Relief — 14% rate on qualifying gains
  • PRR — full or partial exemption on main home

Business Asset Disposal Relief (BADR)

BADR reduces the tax rate on qualifying gains to a flat 14% — much lower than the normal 18%/24%. There is a lifetime limit of £1,000,000 of qualifying gains.

Qualifying Conditions for BADR — Sole Traders / Partnerships

  • Disposal of the whole or part of a business (not individual business assets)
  • Must have owned the business for at least 2 years before disposal

Qualifying Conditions for BADR — Shares in a Company

  • The company must be the individual's "personal company" — they must hold ≥ 5% of ordinary shares AND voting rights
  • The individual must be an employee or officer of the company (full-time or part-time)
  • Both the 5% holding and employment conditions must have been met for at least 2 years before disposal
  • The company must be a trading company (not an investment company)
Example — BADR Qualifying vs Not Qualifying
Innocent — managing director, holds 20% (>5%), owned 3+ yearsQUALIFIES ✓ — CGT at 14%
Nigel — NOT an employee, holds only 3% (<5%)DOES NOT QUALIFY ✗ — CGT at 24%

Innocent CGT = £63,000 × 14% = £8,820. Nigel's CGT = £38,200 × 24% = £9,168. Saving = £348.

📝 Exam Tips — BADR
  • Lifetime limit = £1,000,000. If some has been used, check how much remains
  • BADR gains are taxed at 14% regardless of whether the person has remaining BRB or not
  • When setting off the AEA: it's more beneficial to set it against non-BADR gains first (saves 24% vs 14%)
  • BADR does NOT apply to associated disposals (excluded from syllabus)

Investors' Relief (IR)

Investors' Relief also gives a flat 14% rate, with a lifetime limit of £1,000,000. It is designed for external investors who put money into unlisted trading companies but are NOT employees.

Qualifying Conditions for Investors' Relief

  • Shares must be unlisted ordinary shares (including AIM shares)
  • The shares must have been newly issued (subscribed for) directly from the company — NOT purchased on the secondary market
  • Held for a minimum of 3 years
  • The individual must NOT be an employee of the company (or an officer)
  • The company must be a trading company
Example — Investors' Relief

Siobhan subscribed for (bought new) shares in an unquoted trading company in 2018. She is NOT an employee. She sold the shares in 2025 (held 7 years). Gain = £900,000. She has not previously used IR.

Qualifies for IR? YES — unquoted, subscribed for, held 3+ years, not an employee
Gain within £1m lifetime limit: £900,000 × 14%£126,000
Remaining lifetime limit c/f: £1,000,000 − £900,000£100,000

Rollover Relief

When a business sells a qualifying asset and reinvests the proceeds in a replacement qualifying asset, the gain can be deferred (rolled over) into the cost of the new asset.

Qualifying Assets for Rollover Relief

  • Land and buildings used in the trade
  • Fixed (immovable) plant and machinery
  • Goodwill
  • NOT: shares, moveable plant and machinery

Timing Rule

  • Replacement must be acquired between 1 year before and 3 years after the disposal
  • The replacement asset must also be a qualifying asset used in the trade

How Rollover Works

If ALL proceeds reinvested → Gain deferred in FULL
If PARTIAL reinvestment → Gain chargeable = Proceeds NOT reinvested
Gain deferred = Total gain − Gain immediately chargeable
Base cost of new asset = Purchase price − Deferred gain
Example — Rollover Relief

Mick's business sells a warehouse for £522,000 (cost £258,000 → gain = £264,000). He buys a new warehouse for £480,000 within 12 months.

Proceeds not reinvested (£522,000 − £480,000)£42,000
Gain immediately chargeable£42,000
Gain deferred (£264,000 − £42,000)£222,000
Base cost of new warehouse (£480,000 − £222,000)£258,000

Gift Holdover Relief

When you give away a business asset (or shares in an unquoted trading company), you can hold over the gain — meaning the gain is deferred until the recipient eventually sells the asset.

How it works:

  • Donor's gain is reduced to nil (or reduced if partial consideration)
  • Recipient's acquisition cost = Market value − Gain held over
  • The gain is "stored" inside the lower cost — crystallising when the recipient disposes
  • Joint election by both donor and recipient — must be made by 4 years after end of the tax year of the gift

What assets qualify?

  • Assets used in a trade (business assets)
  • Shares in unquoted trading companies
  • Shares in the individual's personal company (≥ 5%) — but the relief may be restricted if the company has investment assets
  • No minimum ownership period required
Example — Gift Holdover with Restriction

Bill (5%+ shareholder, employee) gifts shares in High Rise Ltd to his daughter Belle. MV = £260,000. Bill's original cost = £112,000. Gain = £148,000. High Rise Ltd has: chargeable business assets £150,000 / total chargeable assets £180,000.

Restriction: Because the company has investment assets, holdover is restricted:

Gain eligible for holdover = £148,000 × £150,000/£180,000£123,333
Bill's chargeable gain = £148,000 − £123,333£24,667
AEA(£3,000)
Taxable gain£21,667
CGT (BADR applies as Bill qualifies): £21,667 × 14%£3,033
Belle's acquisition cost = £260,000 − £123,333£136,667
📝 Exam Focus — BADR + Gift Holdover Interaction
If BADR is available to the donor, holding over the gain forfeits BADR on the held-over element for the donor — but the recipient might not qualify for BADR. So there's a decision to make: does the donor claim holdover (delaying all CGT) or pay now at 14% with BADR? The exam often asks you to compare both scenarios.

Exam Practice Tips

  1. Always use the standard proforma: Proceeds → Cost → Gain → AEA → Losses → Tax
  2. Check asset type first — exempt assets need NO calculation (car, main home, wasting chattel under £6k)
  3. For chattels — apply the £6,000 rules before computing gain
  4. For shares — apply matching rules in order: same day → 30 day → pool
  5. For CGT rate — calculate remaining basic rate band including pension/gift aid extensions
  6. For BADR — check: 5%? Employee? 2 years? Trading company? Lifetime limit available?
  7. Always compare BADR vs no-BADR in a question involving a choice of who makes the disposal
