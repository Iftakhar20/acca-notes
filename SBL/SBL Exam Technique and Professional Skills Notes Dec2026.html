1

What the SBL Exam Is

Most ACCA exams ask you to show what you know. SBL asks you to act like a senior advisor. You are given a company (a hospital, a manufacturer, a charity, a government agency), shown its problems through documents, and asked to advise the people who run it.

📖 Key term: "Integrated case study"

Integrated means the topics are mixed together, the way they are in real life. One problem, such as a hospital outsourcing its billing, touches strategy, stakeholders, risk, IT security, finance and ethics all at once. You never get a question that just says "explain COSO". You are expected to see which topics apply to the situation.

Exam format at a glance

FeatureWhat it means for you
Length3 hours 15 minutes in total. This includes Reading, Planning and Reflection Time (RPRT), which you can use whenever you like during the exam. There is no separate reading period.
Marks100 marks: 80 technical marks + 20 professional skills marks. Pass mark = 50.
QuestionsAll compulsory. The syllabus guide says each exam contains tasks with varying marks, and a task may be split into parts. Each task can put you in a different role, such as consultant, internal auditor or board advisor.
Pre-seenReleased about 2 weeks before the exam. It gives background on a fictional organisation and its industry. You study it in advance, and it is also available inside the exam.
Unseen exhibitsIn the exam you get new information about the same organisation, such as emails, board minutes, reports and spreadsheets. The tasks are based on these.
Closed bookNo notes allowed. You must remember the models.
Computer-basedYou type answers into a word processor, spreadsheet or presentation tool inside the exam software.
No outside researchACCA says you do not need to research the real industry. Everything you need is in the pre-seen and the exam exhibits.
🏥 Example — what an SBL task feels like

Pre-seen: "Northbridge Health is a public hospital group with 4 hospitals…" (12 pages of background, financial data and an organisation chart).

In the exam, Exhibit 3 is an email from the Finance Director: "The board wants to outsource all laboratory testing to a private company. I am worried about patient data."

Task: "As a consultant, write a briefing paper for the board evaluating the risks of the outsourcing proposal and recommending how they should be managed. (14 marks) Professional skills marks are available for demonstrating scepticism (2 marks)."

You need to know risk management (TARA), outsourcing and IT security. More importantly, you need to use the facts in Exhibit 3, not write general theory.

The three intellectual levels

Each syllabus outcome has a level from 1 to 3. SBL is mostly Level 3, the highest:

LevelMeaningSimple test
1 – Knowledge & comprehensionKnow and explain it"What is a NED?"
2 – Application & analysisUse it on a situation and break the situation down"Which NEDs at this hospital are not independent, and why?"
3 – Synthesis & evaluationCombine information, weigh it, judge it and recommend"Should this board replace its NEDs? Weigh the costs and benefits and recommend."
✅ The one idea to remember

SBL rewards judgement, not memory. Knowing a model is the entry ticket. The marks come from using it on the case and saying what should be done.

2

How Marks Are Awarded

Technical marks (80)

Markers use a marking guide that lists points they are likely to see. As a rule of thumb:

  • Roughly 1 mark per relevant point that is applied to the case and explained. A well-developed point can sometimes earn up to 2.
  • A point that is only theory ("Mendelow's matrix has four quadrants") usually earns 0 or at most ½.
  • Repeating the same idea in different words earns nothing extra.
  • Each task part has a maximum. Writing beyond it wastes time that you need for other tasks.
  • Markers also credit valid points that are not in the guide, as long as they are sensible and linked to the case.

❌ Scores 0–½

"Outsourcing has many risks, such as loss of control and data security problems. Companies should manage these risks carefully."

Generic. It could be about any company. No case facts and no explanation of why it matters.

✅ Scores 2

"The lab contractor would hold test results for around 400,000 patients (Exhibit 3). A breach would expose the hospital to regulatory penalties and loss of public trust, which matters for a government-funded body that answers to the Ministry. The contract should therefore require encryption, annual security audits, and data stored in-country."

Case fact → consequence → action.

Professional skills marks (20)

Up to 20% of the marks are for how you answer, not just what you say. Most tasks carry some. The requirement names the skill, for example "Professional skills marks are available for demonstrating commercial acumen…"

  • Each named skill usually carries a few marks, often 2–4, within a task.
  • The marker judges how well you demonstrated that skill across the whole answer, roughly from "not shown" through "weak" and "good" to "very good". These are not marks you can tick off individually.
  • There are five skills: Communication, Commercial acumen, Analysis, Scepticism, Evaluation. See section 4.
⚠️ Why professional skills marks decide the pass

Suppose you score 45 technical marks out of 80 but only 4 out of 20 for professional skills: you have 49 and fail. Raising professional skills to 10 out of 20 gives 55 and a pass. That is the cheapest way to gain marks in SBL, because it means writing in the right way, not learning more facts.

🧠 Memory hook

"No case, no marks." Before you finish any paragraph, ask yourself whether it could be pasted into an answer about a different company. If it could, it isn't earning marks.

3

Requirement Verbs — What the Examiner Is Really Asking

The verb tells you how deep to go. If you answer the wrong verb, you lose marks even when your content is good.

VerbWhat it meansWhat your answer must contain
ExplainMake it clear why or howThe point + the reason, linked to the case
DiscussLook at it from different sidesArguments for and against, then a conclusion
AnalyseBreak it into parts and examine eachStructured breakdown (often by a model), with causes and effects
AssessJudge importance or extentEach factor + how significant it is ("this is the most serious because…")
EvaluateWeigh up and reach a judgementPros, cons, risks, costs, evidence quality, then a clear verdict
RecommendSay what should be doneSpecific actions: what, who, when, and why it fits this organisation
AdviseGuide the reader to a decisionAnalysis + recommendation, written to the named person
JustifyGive reasons that support a positionStrong case-based reasons why the decision is right
Critically evaluate / critically assessEvaluate with challengeQuestion the assumptions, the evidence and any bias, then judge
🏭 Example — same topic, different verbs

Case: FreshCo, a food manufacturer, plans to build a factory in a neighbouring country.

  • Explain the risks → "The new market's currency is volatile, so FreshCo's profits converted back would vary, because costs are in the local currency but reporting is in dollars."
  • Assess the risks → the above, plus "This is a high-impact risk because the factory represents 30% of group assets, but moderate probability given the central bank's track record."
  • Evaluate the proposal → benefits vs risks vs cost, how reliable the forecast is, then "On balance FreshCo should proceed only if…"
✅ Exam tip

Underline the verb, the number of marks, the role you are playing and the audience. These four things decide your whole answer.

The Five Professional Skills (Syllabus Section I)

Each skill is explained below in plain language: what the syllabus asks for, what the marker is looking for, and a weak versus strong example.

4.1 Communication

📖 In simple words

Write so that the specific reader (a board, a CEO, staff, the public) can quickly understand and act on your answer, in the right format and tone.

The syllabus asks you to:

  • Inform concisely, objectively and without ambiguity, using suitable media and being sensitive to culture.
  • Persuade with logical, compelling arguments, and counter-argue where appropriate.
  • Clarify and simplify complex issues for the intended audience, in an appropriate tone.

The marker looks for:

  • The correct format: a report looks like a report, slides look like slides, an email looks like an email.
  • A clear structure: headings that follow the requirement, short paragraphs, one idea per paragraph.
  • A tone that fits the audience: formal for a board, reassuring for staff, factual and calm for the press.
  • Plain language: jargon is explained if the reader isn't a specialist.

❌ Weak (to a board)

"So basically the IT guys messed up the upgrade and now everything's a mess lol, we need to sort it ASAP."

✅ Strong (to a board)

"The system upgrade on 3 March caused a two-day outage in patient registration. This briefing sets out the cause, the impact and three actions for the board's approval."

❌ Weak (press release)

"Due to inadequate governance and ineffective internal controls, a material data incident occurred."

✅ Strong (press release)

"On 3 March, some patient appointment details were accessed without permission. No medical records or payment details were affected. We have contacted every affected patient and strengthened our systems."

4.2 Commercial Acumen

📖 In simple words

Show business common sense: you understand how this organisation makes money (or delivers its public service), what matters to it, what is realistic, and what is happening around it.

The syllabus asks you to:

  • Show awareness of organisational and external factors such as the market, competitors, regulation and the economy.
  • Use judgement to spot the key issues and propose solutions that can be implemented.
  • Show insight and perception, including how to handle conflict.

The marker looks for:

  • Realistic recommendations that the organisation can afford and has the people to deliver. "Buy the competitor" is not realistic for a company with no cash.
  • Awareness of knock-on effects: cutting nurses saves cost but may increase waiting times and complaints.
  • Links to the mission and objectives stated in the pre-seen.
  • Use of the external context given in the case: a new regulation, a competitor's price cut.

❌ Weak

"The hospital should invest in the latest AI technology to improve efficiency."

Ignores cost, budget and readiness.

✅ Strong

"With capital funding frozen for two years (Exhibit 2), a full AI rollout is not affordable. A 12-month pilot in the outpatient department, funded from the existing IT budget, would test the benefits before the next funding round."

4.3 Analysis

📖 In simple words

Dig into the information (text and numbers) to find causes, patterns and opportunities, and to check whether what people believe is actually true.

The syllabus asks you to:

  • Investigate information from many sources using a range of techniques, to find causes of problems or opportunities.
  • Use evidence to support or dispute existing beliefs.
  • Think carefully about what the findings mean for the organisation.

The marker looks for:

  • Using the numbers: calculating growth, margins and per-unit figures rather than copying them.
  • Linking exhibits: Exhibit 2 says costs are under control, but Exhibit 4's figures show overtime up 40%.
  • Finding the root cause, not just the symptom.
📊 Example — symptom vs root cause

Exhibit: "Patient complaints rose from 120 to 310 this year." The CEO blames "rude staff".

Weak analysis: "Complaints have risen significantly, which is bad for reputation."

Strong analysis: "Complaints rose by 158% (310 vs 120). Exhibit 5 shows 68% of them concern waiting times, not staff behaviour. Over the same period, nursing vacancies rose from 5% to 18%. The evidence points to understaffing as the root cause, not rudeness, so staff training alone is unlikely to fix the problem."

4.4 Scepticism

📖 In simple words

Don't accept what you're told at face value. Question claims, forecasts and opinions, especially from people who benefit from them, and say so politely.

The syllabus asks you to:

  • Probe deeply into the underlying reasons for issues, beyond what is obvious.
  • Question facts, opinions and assertions, and seek justification and evidence.
  • Challenge information or decisions where justified, professionally and courteously, in the public or organisational interest.

Common things to be sceptical about in SBL cases:

  • Optimistic forecasts: "sales will grow 30% every year".
  • Who wrote it: a manager whose bonus depends on the project, or a supplier praising its own product.
  • Missing information: no risk assessment, no cost of implementation, no customer research.
  • Rushed timelines: "board approval needed next week".
  • Contradictions between exhibits.

❌ Weak

"The CFO says the new system will save $2m a year, which will improve profits."

✅ Strong

"The $2m saving comes from the vendor's own sales proposal and has not been independently verified. It also assumes all 40 clerks leave in year one, which conflicts with the government's no-redundancy policy (Exhibit 1). The board should ask for an independent review of the savings before approving."

⚠️ Scepticism ≠ rudeness

Challenge the evidence, never the person. Write "The assumption of 85% occupancy appears optimistic compared with the current 62%", not "The CEO is clearly lying."

4.5 Evaluation

📖 In simple words

Weigh up and decide. Balance costs, benefits, risks and opportunities, think about who is affected and what happens next, then reach a clear conclusion.

The syllabus asks you to:

  • Use professional judgement on issues and decisions, considering their effect on the organisation and the people affected.
  • Estimate trends and make reasoned forecasts of the outcomes.
  • Appraise facts and opinions objectively, balancing costs, risks, benefits and opportunities before recommending.

The marker looks for:

  • Both sides of the argument.
  • A sense of which points matter most. Not every risk is equal.
  • A clear final judgement: "Approve", "Reject" or "Approve subject to…". Never sit on the fence.

❌ Weak ending

"There are advantages and disadvantages, so the board should consider carefully."

✅ Strong ending

"On balance, the benefits (faster cash collection, lower error rates) outweigh the risks, but only if data security is contractually guaranteed. I recommend approval subject to three conditions: …"

Summary — the 5 skills in one table

SkillQuestion to ask yourselfTrigger words / phrases to use
CommunicationWould the reader understand this in one read, in the right format?Headings, "This report…", "I recommend…"
Commercial acumenIs this realistic, affordable and aligned with the mission?"Given the budget freeze…", "This would also affect…"
AnalysisHave I used the numbers and found the real cause?"This represents a 158% increase…", "The root cause appears to be…"
ScepticismWho said this, is there evidence, and what's missing?"This assumption appears optimistic…", "has not been verified…"
EvaluationHave I weighed both sides and given a clear verdict?"On balance…", "The most significant…", "subject to…"
5

The PAIR Paragraph Method

The simplest way to make every paragraph earn marks is to build it in four parts:

  1. P — Point. State the issue in one short sentence. "Patient data security is the most significant risk."
  2. A — Apply. Prove it with a fact from the case, citing the exhibit. "The contractor would access 400,000 patient records (Exhibit 3)."
  3. I — Implication ("so what?"). Say why it matters to this organisation. "A breach could lead to regulatory fines and loss of public trust in a government-funded hospital."
  4. R — Response. Say what should be done. "The contract should require encryption, in-country data storage and annual independent audits."
🛒 Full PAIR example — FMCG company

Case: SnackWave, a crisps manufacturer, gets 45% of its revenue from one supermarket chain, MegaMart (Exhibit 2). MegaMart has just announced its own-brand crisps.

Customer concentration risk

[P] SnackWave is heavily dependent on a single customer. [A] MegaMart provides 45% of revenue (Exhibit 2) and has just launched an own-brand range that competes directly with SnackWave's core product. [I] If MegaMart reduces shelf space or demands lower prices, SnackWave could lose a large share of its revenue very quickly, and because its factory costs are largely fixed, profits would fall even faster than sales. [R] SnackWave should start diversifying its customer base, for example by approaching the two other national chains and growing online sales, with a target of no single customer above 30% of revenue within three years.

This paragraph would typically earn about 2 technical marks, and it also shows commercial acumen (fixed costs, own-brand threat) and evaluation (a measurable target).

🧠 Memory hook

PAIR = "Point, Apply, Implication, Response." If a paragraph is missing A it is generic, and if it is missing I it is shallow. Say "so what?" out loud after each point.

6

Using Models Correctly

Models (PESTEL, Five Forces, Mendelow, TARA, the cultural web…) are tools for organising your thinking. They are not essays to reproduce.

SituationWhat to do
The requirement names a model ("using Mendelow's matrix…")You must use it, with its categories as your headings.
The requirement doesn't name a modelYou may use one if it helps structure your answer, but marks come from the case-based points, not the model's name.
A model doesn't fit the factsDon't force it. Use clear headings based on the issues instead.

❌ Describing the model

"Mendelow's matrix classifies stakeholders by power and interest. High power and high interest stakeholders are key players and must be managed closely. Low power, low interest stakeholders require minimal effort…"

≈ 0 marks: pure textbook.

✅ Applying the model

"Ministry of Health – Key player. It provides 74% of funding and appoints four of the seven board members, so it has high power. Its recent statement on data protection and workforce participation (Exhibit 5) shows high interest in this proposal. The board must consult the Ministry before any decision, or risk the proposal being reversed."

≈ 2 marks: power and interest are both proven with case facts, plus an action.

✅ Rule of thumb

Spend no more than one sentence explaining a model, and only if the reader needs it (for example, a board that doesn't know the model). Use every other sentence on the case.

7

Making Recommendations That Score

Almost every SBL task ends with "recommend" or "advise". A good recommendation answers five questions:

QuestionExample
What exactly?Introduce a monthly reconciliation of insurance claims to the patient register
Who does it?The Revenue Cycle Manager, reviewed by the Finance Director
When?From next quarter, with the first review by the audit committee in 6 months
Why does it fit here?38% of rejections are caused by missing documents (Exhibit 3), and reconciliation catches them before claims are submitted
Cost / risk?About 1 extra FTE, small compared with the rejected claims of roughly $40m a year

❌ Vague

"The company should improve its controls and train its staff."

✅ Specific

"The IT Director should enforce unique logins with two-factor authentication for all billing staff within 30 days, because credential sharing was found in the last audit (Exhibit 3). This costs little and directly reduces the risk of unauthorised claim changes."

🧠 Memory hook

"W-W-W-W-C": What, Who, When, Why, Cost. If two or more are missing, your recommendation is vague.

Answer Formats & Templates

The requirement tells you what to produce. Getting the format right is the easiest way to earn communication marks. Below is a template for each common format.

8.1 Report / Briefing paper (most common)

REPORT
To: Board of Directors, Northbridge Health
From: Strategy Consultant
Date: [today]
Subject: Evaluation of the laboratory outsourcing proposal

1. Introduction
This report evaluates … and recommends …

2. [Heading taken from requirement part (a)]
PAIR paragraphs…

3. [Heading taken from requirement part (b)]
PAIR paragraphs…

4. Conclusion and recommendations
On balance… I recommend…

A briefing paper is similar but shorter and more direct. Keep the To / From / Date / Subject block and the headings.

8.2 Memo

The same header block as a report, with a shorter body. Used for internal communication to a manager or department.

8.3 Email

To: Chief Executive
Subject: Data breach – immediate actions required

Dear [Name/Title],

Purpose sentence (why you are writing).

Short sections with headings or bold lead-ins…

Next steps / what you need from them.

Kind regards,
[Your role]

8.4 Presentation slides

Each slide has a title and 3–5 short bullet points. The detailed explanation goes in the speaker notes beneath the slide. Markers read the notes, and that is where the PAIR paragraphs belong.

Slide 2: Key risks of outsourcing

  • Patient data security
  • Loss of in-house expertise
  • Dependence on a single supplier

Speaker notes: "The most significant risk is data security. The contractor would hold 400,000 patient records… (full PAIR paragraph)."

⚠️ Slide trap

Bullets alone earn very few marks because there is no explanation. Always write speaker notes when the task allows them, and don't cram paragraphs onto the slide itself.

8.5 Press release / public statement

  • A headline, date and place.
  • Plain language: no jargon, no blame, no confidential information.
  • Structure: what happened → what it means for the public → what the organisation is doing → where to get more information.
  • A tone that is honest, calm, takes responsibility and reassures.

8.6 Letter

Sender and recipient addresses (simplified), date, "Dear…", a subject line, paragraphs, then "Yours sincerely" (when you know their name) or "Yours faithfully" (when you don't).

8.7 Notes for a meeting / briefing notes

Headings and numbered points, but each point still needs a full sentence of explanation. "Notes" doesn't mean single words.

Tone guide by audience

AudienceToneAvoid
Board / CEOFormal, concise, decision-focusedLong theory, casual language
StaffClear, respectful, reassuring, honestThreats, technical jargon
Public / pressPlain, calm, accountableBlame, internal details, legal admissions
Regulator / MinistryFormal, factual, compliantSpeculation, defensiveness
Shareholders / investorsProfessional, focused on value and riskOver-promising

Time Management

195 minutes ÷ 100 marks = about 1.95 minutes per mark, so roughly 2 minutes per mark, including reading and planning.

Marks for the taskTotal timeRead & plan (≈ ⅓)Write (≈ ⅔)
1020 min6–7 min13–14 min
1529 min9–10 min19–20 min
2039 min12–13 min26–27 min
2549 min15–16 min33–34 min
3058 min18–19 min39–40 min
⚠️ The golden rule of time

Never go over time on a task. The first marks on any requirement are the easiest to earn, and the last marks are the hardest. Leaving a task half-finished to start the next one gains more marks than polishing one answer to perfection.

⏱️ Example — why moving on matters

You've spent 30 minutes on a 15-mark task and earned about 9 marks. Another 10 minutes there might add 1 more mark. The same 10 minutes on the next task could earn 4–5 marks. Always move on.

✅ Practical tips
  • At the start, note the clock time each task must finish (e.g. "Task 1 → 10:39").
  • Answer tasks in the order you are most confident, if you prefer. Just keep each answer under its correct heading.
  • Keep about 5 minutes at the end to check headings and add any missing recommendations.

The 6-Step Task Method

Use the same process for every task until it becomes automatic.

  1. Read the requirement FIRST, before the exhibits. Identify:
    • the verb;
    • the marks for each part;
    • your role (consultant, internal auditor…);
    • the audience and format;
    • which professional skill is being tested.
  2. Read the relevant exhibits actively. Read with the requirement in mind, as if hunting for evidence. Note every problem, number, person, deadline and contradiction.
  3. Plan quickly. Use the answer screen or the scratchpad to set out headings (taken from the requirement) and the bullet points under each. See the planning grid below.
  4. Write in PAIR paragraphs under clear headings. Aim for about one paragraph per mark-earning point.
  5. Conclude and recommend. Give a clear judgement and specific actions.
  6. Quick review (1–2 minutes): Have I answered every part? Is the format right? Have I shown the named professional skill?

Planning grid example

Requirement: "Evaluate the proposal to outsource billing and recommend whether to approve it. (12 marks + 2 scepticism)"

HeadingPoints (with exhibit reference)
BenefitsLower rejection rate guaranteed <6% (Ex 2) · expertise · faster cash · frees management time
Drawbacks / risksRoot cause is clinical documentation, so outsourcing won't fix 38% of rejections (Ex 3) · patient data access (Ex 4, 5) · 85 staff affected and workforce policy (Ex 5) · 5-year lock-in · fee of 4% of revenue
ScepticismGuarantee untested · rush "this year's results" (Ex 2) · CFO motive? · no option appraisal
RecommendationDon't approve next month · fix documentation and system first · tender with data clauses · revisit in 6 months
✅ Exam tip

Type your plan directly onto the answer page as headings and bullet points, then turn each bullet into a PAIR paragraph. This saves the time of rewriting and makes sure your structure matches your plan.

Handling Numbers & Data

SBL is not a calculation exam, but exhibits often contain numbers. Using them well is analysis, and questioning them is scepticism.

The 5 quick calculations that impress markers

CalculationFormulaExample
% change(New − Old) ÷ Old × 100Revenue 50 → 58: (58−50)/50 = +16%
MarginProfit ÷ Revenue × 100Profit 6 on revenue 60 = 10%
Share / proportionPart ÷ Total × 100Grant 900 of total income 1,210 = 74%
Per unitTotal ÷ UnitsCost $30m ÷ 600 beds = $50k per bed
PaybackInvestment ÷ Annual cash inflow$4.5m ÷ $1.5m = 3 years
📊 Example — turning numbers into insight

Exhibit: "Revenue grew from $80m to $92m. Operating profit fell from $12m to $9.2m."

Weak: "Revenue increased but profit decreased."

Strong: "Revenue grew 15%, but operating margin fell from 15% to 10% ($9.2m ÷ $92m). Growth is being bought at the expense of profitability, probably through discounting or rising costs, and should be investigated before further expansion."

Questions to ask about any forecast (scepticism)

  • Who prepared it, and do they benefit if it is approved?
  • Is the key assumption realistic compared with current performance?
  • What happens if that assumption is wrong? (A quick sensitivity check.)
  • Is anything missing, such as implementation costs, tax, inflation or staff costs?

Preparing the Pre-seen

The pre-seen arrives about two weeks before the exam. Thorough preparation means you won't waste exam time reading background, and your answers will be full of relevant detail.

The 2-week pre-seen plan

WhenActivityOutput
Day 1Read it once, quickly, for the overall storyA one-paragraph summary: "Who are they and what's going on?"
Day 2–3Read again slowly and look up any unfamiliar termsGlossary of industry terms
Day 4–5Analyse the organisation: mission, objectives, structure, key people, products and servicesOne-page company profile and the names of key people
Day 6–7Work through the numbers: growth, margins, ratios, trendsA small table of key ratios with comments
Day 8–9Apply models: PESTEL, Five Forces, SWOT, stakeholders (Mendelow), value chainOne page per model
Day 10–11List the likely issues: problems hinted at, upcoming decisions, risks, governance weaknesses"Top 10 issues" list
Day 12–13Practise past-exam tasks with the pre-seen in mindPractice answers
Day 14Light review and restConfidence
⚠️ Pre-seen traps
  • Don't try to predict the exact questions. The exam brings new information that changes the situation. Prepare broadly.
  • Don't research the real industry online. ACCA says it isn't needed, and outside facts can contradict the case.
  • Don't copy large chunks of the pre-seen into answers. Refer to it briefly ("as stated in the company's mission…").

Digital & Employability Skills (Syllabus Section J)

Section J tests whether you can use the exam software the way you would use office tools at work. The syllabus lists four skills:

Syllabus skillWhat it means in practice
J1 – Access and manipulate informationMove quickly between exhibits, find what you need, copy figures accurately
J2 – Work on response optionsChoose and use the right tool (word processor, spreadsheet, slides) for each task
J3 – Navigate windows and screensResize and arrange windows so you can see an exhibit and your answer side by side
J4 – Present data effectivelyClear headings, tables where useful, tidy spreadsheets with labelled workings
✅ Practical software tips
  • Practise on ACCA's official practice platform so the software feels familiar on exam day.
  • Use bold headings and numbered sections, which make marking easy.
  • In spreadsheets, use formulas (e.g. =B4/B2) and add a short label to every calculation so the marker can follow it.
  • Copy-paste small amounts of data from exhibits if helpful, but never paste whole exhibits. That earns nothing.
  • Learn the basic shortcuts: Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V, Ctrl+B (bold), Ctrl+Z (undo).
  • Typing speed matters. If you type slowly, practise with timed writing sessions.

Worked Mini-Case — Weak vs Distinction Answer

📁 Case: CityCare Clinics

Exhibit 1 (pre-seen extract): CityCare runs 12 private outpatient clinics. Its mission is "affordable, reliable family healthcare". Average clinic utilisation (booked appointment slots ÷ available slots) is 62%. Cash reserves are $6m.

Exhibit 2 (email from the CEO): "I want to open a flagship clinic in Northtown. It costs $4.5m. Our consultants forecast annual revenue of $3.4m at 85% utilisation, with annual running costs of $1.9m, almost all fixed. Payback in 3 years! Northtown is booming. Let's approve this at Friday's board."

Exhibit 3 (HR report): "We currently have 6 unfilled doctor posts across the group. Recruitment takes 5–7 months."

Exhibit 4 (news extract): "A national clinic chain opened a 20-room clinic in Northtown last month."

Requirement: As advisor to the board, prepare a briefing note evaluating the Northtown proposal and recommending whether the board should approve it on Friday. (10 marks) Professional skills marks are available for demonstrating scepticism (2 marks).

Time allowed ≈ 24 minutes (12 marks × 1.95).

❌ Weak answer (typical first attempt)

Opening a new clinic is a market development strategy under Ansoff's matrix. Ansoff has four strategies: market penetration, product development, market development and diversification. 0 – theory

Advantages: the company will grow and earn more revenue. Northtown is booming so there will be many customers. Payback is 3 years which is good. 1 – some case use

Disadvantages: there are risks such as competition and the cost is high. ½ – vague

Conclusion: the board should consider all factors carefully before deciding. 0 – no recommendation

Estimated score: 1½ / 10 technical + 0 / 2 scepticism = 1½ / 12. It is not in briefing-note format, describes theory, accepts the CEO's figures without question and gives no recommendation.

✅ Distinction answer

BRIEFING NOTE
To: Board of Directors, CityCare Clinics
From: Board Advisor
Subject: Evaluation of the proposed Northtown clinic

1. Purpose
This note evaluates the proposal to invest $4.5m in a Northtown clinic and recommends whether it should be approved at Friday's meeting.

2. Potential benefits
Northtown is described as a growing area (Exhibit 2), so a clinic there would extend CityCare into a new geographical market. This would spread the group's revenue beyond its existing 12 locations and support long-term growth in line with its family healthcare mission. 1

If the forecast is achieved, annual net cash inflow would be $1.5m ($3.4m − $1.9m), giving a payback of 3 years on $4.5m. That would be an attractive return. 1

3. Concerns about the forecast
The forecast assumes 85% utilisation, but CityCare's existing clinics average only 62% (Exhibit 1). There is no evidence that a brand-new clinic would outperform established ones, especially in its first years. 1 + scepticism

Because almost all costs are fixed, results are very sensitive to utilisation. At 62%, revenue would be about $2.48m ($3.4m × 62/85), net inflow only $0.58m, and payback around 7.8 years rather than 3. Even at 70%, payback would be about 5 years. 2 – analysis

The forecast was prepared by consultants engaged for the project, and it has not been independently reviewed. The board should ask for the underlying assumptions before relying on it. scepticism

4. Other risks
Staffing: The group already has 6 unfilled doctor posts and recruitment takes 5–7 months (Exhibit 3). A new clinic would increase the shortage, could delay the opening, and might draw doctors away from existing clinics, harming their service. 1

Competition: A national chain opened a 20-room clinic in Northtown last month (Exhibit 4). This makes high utilisation even less likely, and a price-led response could undermine CityCare's affordability positioning. 1

Financial exposure: The $4.5m cost equals 75% of CityCare's $6m cash reserves (Exhibit 1). If the clinic underperforms, the group would have little buffer for other needs. 1

Governance: The CEO wants approval at Friday's meeting. That leaves too little time for due diligence on a decision of this size, and the board should not be pressured into approving it. scepticism

5. Recommendation
I recommend the board does not approve the proposal on Friday. Instead, it should:
(i) ask the Finance Director to prepare a revised forecast using 60–70% utilisation, with a sensitivity analysis, within one month;
(ii) ask HR to confirm whether doctors can realistically be recruited for a new site;
(iii) commission a short market study of demand in Northtown following the competitor's entry.
The board can reconsider within two to three months with reliable information. A smaller or leased site could also be considered to reduce the $4.5m commitment. 2

Estimated score: 10 / 10 technical (capped at the maximum) + 2 / 2 scepticism = 12 / 12.

✅ What made the difference?
  • Format: a briefing note header and headings → communication.
  • Numbers used: recalculated payback at realistic utilisation → analysis.
  • Assumptions challenged: 85% vs 62%, who prepared the forecast, rushed timing → scepticism.
  • Every point tied to an exhibit → application.
  • A clear, practical recommendation with steps and timing → evaluation and commercial acumen.

Memory Tips & One-Page Cheat Sheet

🧠 The 5 skills: "CCASE" ("see case")

Communication · Commercial acumen · Analysis · Scepticism · Evaluation. Because SBL is about the case, "CCASE" reminds you to see the case.

🧠 Every paragraph: PAIR

Point → Apply (exhibit fact) → Implication ("so what?") → Response (action).

🧠 Every requirement: "V-M-R-A-F-S"

Verb · Marks · Role · Audience · Format · Skill. Underline all six before reading the exhibits.

🧠 Every recommendation: W-W-W-W-C

What · Who · When · Why · Cost.

🧠 Time: "2 minutes a mark"

⅓ reading and planning, ⅔ writing. Never go over time.

Scepticism phrase bank

  • "This assumption appears optimistic when compared with…"
  • "The figures were prepared by [person], who may benefit from approval, so independent verification is needed."
  • "No evidence has been provided to support the claim that…"
  • "The proposal does not consider [cost / risk / stakeholder]…"
  • "Exhibit X appears to contradict Exhibit Y, because…"

Evaluation phrase bank

  • "The most significant issue is… because…"
  • "On balance, the benefits outweigh the risks, provided that…"
  • "While X is an advantage, it is outweighed by…"
  • "I recommend approval subject to the following conditions…"

Commercial acumen phrase bank

  • "Given the organisation's [budget / cash / staffing] position…"
  • "This would also affect [another department / stakeholder]…"
  • "This is consistent with the mission of…"
  • "A phased / pilot approach would reduce the risk while…"

Self-Test Quiz

Try to answer each question before opening it.

Q1. How many marks are professional skills worth in SBL, and what are the five skills?

20 marks out of 100. The skills are Communication, Commercial acumen, Analysis, Scepticism and Evaluation ("CCASE").

Q2. How long is the exam and roughly how much time should you allow per mark?

3 hours 15 minutes (195 minutes) including reading, planning and reflection time, so about 1.95, or roughly 2, minutes per mark.

Q3. What does PAIR stand for?

Point, Apply (case fact), Implication (so what?), Response (action).

Q4. A requirement says "Evaluate". What must your answer end with?

A clear judgement or conclusion, such as approve, reject, or approve subject to conditions, after weighing both sides.

Q5. A sales director forecasts 40% growth for a product whose market grew 4% last year. Which professional skill should you show, and how?

Scepticism. Point out that the forecast is far above market growth, that the sales director may benefit from approval, and that it needs independent verification and a sensitivity analysis.

Q6. You're asked for presentation slides. Where do most of your marks come from?

The speaker notes, where the full explanations are written. The slides themselves should have a title and 3–5 short bullets.

Q7. You have spent your full time allowance on Task 2 but aren't finished. What should you do?

Move on to the next task. The early marks on a new task are easier to earn than the last marks on a nearly finished one. If time remains at the end, come back.

Q8. Rewrite this weak recommendation: "The company should improve cyber security."

Example: "The IT Director should introduce two-factor authentication for all remote logins within 30 days, because three phishing incidents occurred last quarter (Exhibit 4). The cost is low compared with the potential loss of customer data and regulatory fines."

Q9. Should you research the real industry before the exam?

No. ACCA states it isn't expected. Everything needed is in the pre-seen and exam exhibits, and outside facts may contradict the case.

Q10. Revenue rises from $40m to $46m while operating profit falls from $6m to $4.6m. Write one analytical sentence.

"Revenue grew 15%, but operating margin fell from 15% to 10% ($4.6m ÷ $46m), suggesting growth is being achieved through discounting or rising costs. This needs investigating before further expansion."
