1. The NPV Proforma D1

Every investment appraisal question — however dressed up — reduces to building this table. Get the layout right first, then worry about the tricky adjustments.

NPV decision rule NPV = ΣPV of inflows − ΣPV of outflows  →  Accept if NPV ≥ 0 (increases shareholder wealth)
Memory hook Think of the table as "Cash In, Cash Out, Never Depreciation." Only cash flows go in the table — depreciation is not a cash flow, it only matters via the tax saved on TAD line.

What NEVER belongs in the NPV table

EXCLUDE
  • Depreciation charge itself
  • Sunk costs (already spent, irrelevant)
  • Apportioned / general head office overheads
  • Interest and finance charges (reflected in the discount rate, not the cash flows)
INCLUDE
  • Opportunity costs (lost contribution elsewhere)
  • Incremental / avoidable fixed costs only
  • Working capital — invested at start of each year's trading, recovered in full at the end of the project
  • Tax effects (in arrears, unless stated otherwise)

2. Real vs Money (Nominal) Cash Flows D2

This is one of the highest-value traps in the exam. The rule is simple to state but easy to break under time pressure:

The golden rule Real cash flows → discount at the real rate.   Money (nominal) cash flows → discount at the money rate. Never cross the streams.
Fisher equation (1 + money rate) = (1 + real rate) × (1 + inflation rate)
Situation given in questionMethod to use
One single general rate of inflation applies to all cash flowsEither method gives the same NPV — real cash flows @ real rate, OR money cash flows @ money rate
Different (specific) inflation rates for different cost/revenue itemsMust use the money method — inflate each cash flow at its own specific rate, discount at the money rate
Common trap Do not use the shortcut "money rate − inflation rate" to approximate the real rate — this loses marks. Always apply the full Fisher formula: real = [(1+money)/(1+inflation)] − 1.
Worked mini-example (AED) Money discount rate 12.5%, general inflation 5%. Real rate = (1.125/1.05) − 1 = 7.14%. A Dubai Health equipment project with cash flows already stated in today's (real) AED terms should be discounted at 7.14% — do NOT inflate the cash flows again.

3. Taxation & Tax-Allowable Depreciation (TAD) D2

Tax on operating cash flow Taxable profit = Sales − Costs (excl. depreciation)  →  Tax payable = Taxable profit × Tax rate  (usually paid/saved one year in arrears unless told otherwise)

TAD (Writing Down Allowance) — the standard pattern

YearWorkingsWDA (tax saving base)
Year 1Cost × WDA%WDV c/f = Cost − WDA
Year 2 onwardWDV b/f × WDA%reducing balance
Final year of ownershipWDV b/f − Scrap proceedsBalancing allowance / charge — not a normal WDA%
Balancing Allowance

Arises when scrap proceeds < WDV at disposal. Company hasn't claimed enough depreciation yet → extra tax relief given. Adds to the tax saving.

Balancing Charge

Arises when scrap proceeds > WDV. Company has over-claimed depreciation → some tax relief clawed back. Reduces the tax saving.

Sign-check hook "Proceeds below the books → benefit flows. Proceeds above the books → benefit's clawed."
Before/after tax discount rate After-tax cost of debt = Pre-tax cost of debt × (1 − Tax rate)

4. Adjusting for Risk & Uncertainty D3

TechniqueWhat it measures / doesFormula / approach
Sensitivity analysis How much one variable can change before NPV = 0 (identifies the key risk driver) Sensitivity % = NPV ÷ PV of the variable being flexed
Probability / Expected NPV Weighted-average NPV across possible outcomes ENPV = Σ (NPV of outcome × probability)
Simulation Models many variables changing simultaneously using random sampling — gives a distribution of possible NPVs, not one number —
Adjusted payback Shortens the required payback period as a crude risk buffer —
Risk-adjusted discount rate Adds a risk premium to the discount rate for riskier projects Risk-adjusted rate = Risk-free rate + Risk premium
Risk vs uncertainty — don't blur these Risk = outcomes and probabilities are known/estimable (e.g. from historical data). Uncertainty = outcomes cannot be predicted with any probability, often because the project life is long or the environment is unfamiliar. Uncertainty tends to increase the further into the project life you look.
Exam tip Sensitivity only ever flexes one variable at a time — it does not tell you the probability that variable will actually change. That's the standard "usefulness/limitation" discussion point examiners look for.

5. Lease vs Buy D4

This is a financing decision, not an investment decision — the operating cash flows of the underlying project are identical either way and are ignored. You are comparing two financing cash outflows and picking the option with the lower present cost (least negative NPV).

Buy (borrow to buy)Lease
Who gets TAD?User (owner) — full TAD tax reliefLessor — user gets none
Relevant cash flowsPurchase cost, TAD tax savings, residual/scrap valueLease rentals, tax relief on the full rental
Interest / loan repayments?Excluded — captured via discount rate, not as a cash flowN/A
Discount ratePost-tax cost of borrowing — same rate used for both options
Discount rate to use Post-tax cost of borrowing = Pre-tax cost of borrowing × (1 − Tax rate)
Timing trap Lease payments are usually paid in advance (so first payment at T0), but the tax relief on that payment still follows the normal one-year-in-arrears rule — so tax effects often start a year later than the cash payments themselves. Check the question's specific timing wording carefully.
Qualitative reasons to lease (max 2 needed in exam) Flexibility / shorter commitment than asset's useful life · Risk of obsolescence and disposal value shifted to lessor · May be the only option if cash-poor or loan finance is unavailable.

6. Asset Replacement — Equivalent Annual Cost (EAC) D4

Used when an asset must be replaced repeatedly over a long/infinite horizon and the competing replacement cycles are of different lengths — comparing raw NPVs would be unfair because they cover different time periods.

EAC formula EAC = PV of costs over one replacement cycle ÷ Annuity factor for that cycle length
1. NPV of costs, per cycle length
→
2. Divide by relevant annuity factor
→
3. Pick the LOWEST EAC
Memory hook EAC spreads a lumpy cost evenly over the years — like converting a one-off school fee into "cost per year" so you can compare a 3-year plan fairly against a 5-year plan.
Common mistakes (examiner-reported)
  • Only calculate the residual values / cash flows for the replacement intervals actually asked for — don't waste time on cycles not requested.
  • Never include depreciation in the cash flows.
  • Match the timing convention of maintenance/running costs exactly as stated (paid in advance vs in arrears) — this changes which cycle wins.
  • State the decision with justification — "choose the 4-year cycle" alone will not earn the marks.
Why not just use NPV? For continual (infinite) replacement, comparing raw NPVs of a 3-year and a 4-year cycle is meaningless — they cover different time spans. EAC puts them on the same "per year" footing.

7. Single-Period Capital Rationing D4

Arises when funds available are less than funds needed for all positive-NPV projects — you must ration a scarce resource (capital) to maximise shareholder wealth.

Profitability Index (PI) PI = PV of future cash inflows ÷ Initial investment   (or NPV ÷ Investment, per question convention — check which the question wants)
Project typeMethod
Divisible, independent projectsRank by PI (highest first), invest available funds proportionally down the ranking until capital is exhausted
Non-divisible (indivisible) projectsPI ranking does not work — must test every feasible combination of whole projects within the budget and pick the combination with the highest total NPV
Mutually exclusive projectsCannot simply rank by PI either — compare combinations/branches directly on total NPV, same logic as indivisible case
Exam trap PI ranking is only valid for divisible, independent projects. The moment a question says projects are indivisible or mutually exclusive, switch to testing combinations of whole projects by total NPV — do not rank by PI.
Reasons for capital rationing (discussion marks) Soft rationing — internal management choice to limit capital (e.g. maintain conservative gearing, avoid diluting control). Hard rationing — external constraints (e.g. banks unwilling to lend more, depressed capital markets).

8. IRR — Quick Reference D1

Linear interpolation IRR = L + [ N_L ÷ (N_L − N_H) ] × (H − L)
L = lower discount rate tested, H = higher rate, N_L / N_H = NPVs at those rates
Picking your two test rates Choose one rate giving a small positive NPV and one giving a small negative NPV — the closer both are to zero, the more accurate the interpolation estimate.

NPV vs IRR — why NPV wins in exam discussions

IssueNPVIRR
MeasuresAbsolute increase in shareholder wealth (AED value)Percentage rate of return only
Comparing different-sized projectsWorks directlyIgnores project scale — can mislead
Multiple/unconventional cash flowsNo issueCan produce multiple IRRs
Mutually exclusive project rankingRanks correctly (reinvestment @ cost of capital)Can rank incorrectly (assumes reinvestment @ IRR — unrealistic)
Changing cost of capital over timeCan incorporate different rates per yearCannot handle a single changing rate

9. Exam-Day Common Mistakes Checklist

Am I mixing real cash flows with the money discount rate (or vice versa)?
Check whether inflation is a single general rate (either method fine) or multiple specific rates (money method only, compulsory).
Have I included depreciation as a cash flow?
Never — only the tax saving arising from TAD is a cash flow.
Is the balancing allowance/charge sign correct?
Proceeds below WDV → balancing allowance (more tax relief). Proceeds above WDV → balancing charge (less tax relief / clawback).
Have I used PI ranking on indivisible or mutually exclusive projects?
Don't — test whole-project combinations by total NPV instead.
Am I comparing EAC cycles using the correct annuity factor and matching cost timing?
Double-check whether running/maintenance costs are in advance or arrears — it changes which cycle is optimal.
In a lease vs buy question, have I included loan interest as a separate cash flow?
No — interest is already reflected in the post-tax cost of borrowing used as the discount rate.
Have I stated my final recommendation with justification, not just a number?
Constructed-response marks are often specifically allocated to the advice/conclusion sentence — always write one.
