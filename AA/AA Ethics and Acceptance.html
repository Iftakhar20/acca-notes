Overview: Why Ethics Matters in Audit
The conceptual framework approach · ACCA Code of Ethics
Two Approaches to Ethics

The ACCA Code uses a conceptual framework approach, not a rulebook. Understanding the difference is an exam question in itself.

Aspect Rulebook Approach Conceptual Framework Approach (ACCA)
Nature Clearly defined laws; black and white rules Principles-based guidelines; judgement applied
Flexibility Rigid — fixed for specific situations Flexible — useful in a dynamic environment
Ease of use Easy to know what is or is not allowed Requires professional judgement to apply
Coverage Only covers situations specifically listed Can handle new or unusual situations
📌
Exam Tip The conceptual framework is useful in a dynamic environment — that phrase is an exam answer. Rulebook is easy to follow but inflexible. ACCA's approach is conceptual framework.
The Conceptual Framework Process

When an ethical issue arises, the auditor works through this thought process:

  1. Identify the threat to the fundamental principles (e.g. familiarity, self-interest)
  2. Evaluate the significance of the threat — is it trivial or significant?
  3. Apply safeguards to eliminate or reduce the threat to an acceptable level
  4. If no safeguards can reduce the threat — resign from the engagement
⚠️
Key principle Resignation is always the last resort — only when all other safeguards have been considered and none can reduce the threat to an acceptable level.

The Five Fundamental Principles
ACCA Code of Ethics and Conduct — every auditor must uphold all five
Mnemonic to remember all five
IPCCO
Integrity Professional competence & due care Confidentiality Conduct (professional behaviour) Objectivity
I
Integrity
Be straightforward and honest in all professional and business relationships. Do not associate with misleading information or false statements.
O
Objectivity
Do not allow bias, conflicts of interest, or undue influence to override professional judgements. This is the core of auditor independence.
P
Professional Competence & Due Care
Maintain professional knowledge and skill at the level required. Act diligently and in accordance with applicable standards.
C
Confidentiality
Do not disclose client information to third parties without proper authority, unless there is a legal or professional right or duty to do so.
C
Professional Behaviour
Comply with laws and regulations. Avoid any action that discredits the profession. Do not make disparaging remarks about other professionals.
When is Confidentiality Overridden?

Confidentiality is a fundamental principle, but it is not absolute. There are three situations when disclosure is permitted or required:

  • Required by law — e.g. reporting money laundering (POCA 2002), or court order. The legal duty overrides confidentiality.
  • Permitted by law and authorised by the client — e.g. responding to a bank reference with client consent.
  • Public interest — e.g. the client is breaching laws and regulations and the breach is a public interest matter (environmental, safety). If the client refuses to self-report, the auditor may report to the appropriate authority.
❌
Common Exam Trap Management sometimes claims that reporting a breach of laws is a "breach of confidentiality." This is false. When non-compliance is a public interest matter and management refuses to report, the auditor must report to the appropriate authority.

The Five Ethical Threats
Situations that create a risk to the fundamental principles
💡
Memory Aid — SAFFI Self-interest · Advocacy · Familiarity · Fear (Intimidation) · Inspection (Self-review)
🔶 Self-Interest
The auditor has a financial or other personal interest in the outcome of the audit. The auditor might be tempted to make favourable judgements to protect that interest.
Common Exam Examples
  • Holding shares in an audit client
  • Overdue fees owed by client
  • Fee dependency (fees >15% of income)
  • Contingent fee arrangement
  • Close family member has financial interest in client
  • Partner's son receives shares in client as remuneration
🔷 Self-Review
The auditor is asked to review their own work. They may not be objective about detecting errors or be reluctant to admit mistakes.
Common Exam Examples
  • Auditor also prepares the financial statements
  • Auditor provides payroll / bookkeeping services, then audits payroll figures
  • Audit senior seconded to client, then assigned to audit of that client
  • Previous audit team member joins client (if high seniority)
  • Tax advice (not preparation of the return itself)
  • Internal audit outsourced to external auditor
🟢 Advocacy
The auditor promotes or represents the client's position to the point where objectivity is compromised. The auditor appears to be acting as an agent for the client rather than independently.
Common Exam Examples
  • Auditor liaises with tax authority on client's behalf
  • Acting as solicitor in litigation on behalf of client
  • Promoting the client's shares to investors
  • Auditor acting as an advocate in a legal dispute
🟣 Familiarity
The auditor becomes too close to the client and too trusting of their work. Long relationships, friendships, or family ties reduce professional scepticism.
Common Exam Examples
  • Partner on audit for 7+ years (listed) / long association
  • Partner socialises / goes on holiday with finance director
  • Ex-audit team member joins as financial controller
  • Close friendship between audit manager and new finance director
  • Family member of audit team works at client
🔴 Intimidation
The auditor is pressured or threatened by the client, creating fear that affects professional judgement. The auditor may back down from a correct audit opinion to avoid conflict.
Common Exam Examples
  • Client threatens to remove auditor unless they change opinion
  • Client threatens legal action against auditor
  • Dominant personality of client management
  • Client threatens to withhold future business
Tricky Distinctions the Exam Tests
Situation Correct Threat Why — Not the Other?
Overdue fees still owed by client Self-Interest Auditor has a financial interest in keeping client happy to recover money. Also can be intimidation — client uses debt as leverage. Both can apply.
Partner/FD go on holiday together Familiarity Long personal relationship creates over-trust. Not intimidation — no threat of consequences; not self-interest — no financial angle.
Audit senior moves to client as FD Familiarity Old audit team colleagues may be too trusting of ex-colleague. Also self-review if senior did audit work that FD now oversees. Significance depends on seniority.
Preparing tax return for audit client ❌ No self-review threat Mechanical process — does not involve judgements affecting the financial statements. But tax advice does create a self-review threat.
Tax adviser liaises with HMRC on client's behalf Advocacy Auditor is promoting/representing the client's interests to a third party.
Payroll services provided to audit client Self-Review Auditor will then audit the payroll figures in the financial statements — reviewing their own work.
Assistance selecting a new NED Self-Interest Auditor is involved in appointing someone who has significant influence over the financial statements and audit.

Threat-Specific Safeguards
Threat Most Common Safeguards When Must You Resign?
Self-Interest (shares) Dispose of the shares. Remove person from team. If the person with the financial interest cannot be removed and shares cannot be disposed of quickly.
Self-Interest (overdue fees) Request payment. Do not sign auditor's report until fees are paid. Independent review of audit. If fees remain outstanding and significant — threat cannot be managed.
Self-Review (non-audit services) Use separate teams. Ensure client management makes all decisions (not the auditor). EQR. If the auditor would effectively be reviewing their own significant judgements (e.g., if auditor prepared the financial statements themselves and is also auditing).
Familiarity (long association) Partner rotation. Remove individual from team. Assign different audit manager. Change team composition. If a partner joins the client — e.g. as NED or director — resignation usually required.
Familiarity (social relationship) Remove the individual (e.g. partner). Restructure responsibilities. If threat is so significant and no suitable replacement partner available.
Intimidation Discuss with management. Escalate to those charged with governance. Involve ethics partner. If threat is unresolvable — auditor's opinion is compromised.
Fee Dependency (>15% of income, listed) Disclose to those charged with governance. Pre-issuance review. EQR. Only if safeguards cannot reduce the threat.
Conflict of interest (two competing clients) Inform both clients. Obtain consent. Separate teams. Confidentiality agreements. Information barriers. EQR. If consent cannot be obtained from both clients.

Non-Audit Services — What's Allowed?

Providing non-audit services to an audit client creates threats (mainly self-review). The key principle is: management must make all decisions — the auditor must not assume management responsibility.

Service Threat Created Rules / Notes
Preparing tax returns ❌ Generally no self-review threat Mechanical — auditor is not making judgements on the figures, just completing the form. Allowed.
Tax advice Self-Review Auditor's advice could affect figures in financial statements. If advice is wrong, auditor may be reluctant to admit it. Significant threat — use separate teams.
Liaising with tax authority on client's behalf Advocacy Auditor is representing / promoting client's interests to a third party.
Payroll services (routine) Self-Review Payroll is audited in the financial statements. Allowed for non-listed clients if mechanical/routine and client management makes all decisions.
Bookkeeping services Self-Review Allowed if routine and mechanical for non-listed. Prohibited for listed if significant judgement involved.
Advice on accounting policies Self-Review Allowed if client makes all decisions on which policies to use. Auditor can advise, not decide.
Assistance recruiting a NED Self-Interest Reviewing qualifications/competence is OK — no management decision made. If auditor selects or appoints, it becomes a management responsibility = self-review threat.
Internal audit services (outsourced) Self-Review If the same firm provides both internal and external audit, external auditor will rely on their own work = self-review. Auditor must not assume management responsibility over IA.
Contingent fee for non-assurance work Self-Interest Fee tied to outcome (e.g. tax saving, profit). Not allowed if fee is material/significant to the firm. Never allowed for assurance work.
🚫
Management Responsibility Rule If the external auditor is making decisions that management should make, they are assuming management responsibility. This creates a self-review threat that cannot be safeguarded. Examples: designing internal controls, deciding which internal audit recommendations to implement, determining the scope of internal audit work, selecting which accounting policies to use.
Gifts and Hospitality

Gifts and hospitality from audit clients create a self-interest threat and potentially a familiarity threat.

Scenario Action Reasoning
Gift is trivial and inconsequential, offered innocently May accept Threat is at an acceptable level
Gift is offered as an inducement to influence behaviour (even if trivial) Must decline Intent to influence makes even small gifts unacceptable. The code is clear on this.
Gift is not trivial (e.g. 40% discount on luxury goods) Must decline Not trivial — creates significant self-interest threat
⚠️
Exam Trick A 10% discount on phone accessories was described as "trivial" by the partner — but the finance director said it was "to keep the audit team on side." Answer: must decline because it is an inducement, regardless of the trivial value.
Staff Secondments & Ex-Employees Joining Clients
Situation Threat(s) Action Required
Audit senior seconded to client, then assigned to audit of that client Self-Review + Familiarity Audit senior must not be assigned to the audit of LV Fones Co. Self-review (may have caused the problems they now audit) + familiarity (friendships formed during secondment).
Audit senior joins client as financial controller (junior level) Familiarity (low significance) Review and change audit team composition where needed. Significant threat if more senior role. Firm cannot prevent the individual from taking the job.
Audit manager (or higher) joins client as FD/controller Familiarity (significant) Change audit team — appoint a different audit manager. Make audit approach unpredictable (not predictable to the ex-employee).
Audit staff member joins client: what should they have done first? ACCA Code requirement Must notify the firm immediately so they can be removed from the audit team. They should not have applied without notifying the firm.
Conflict of Interest — Two Competing Clients

When the audit firm has two clients who are competitors (e.g. Winnie Co and Piglet Co), a conflict of interest exists. Both clients may be concerned that commercially sensitive information will be disclosed to their competitor.

Identify the conflict of interest
→
Inform both clients
→
Obtain consent from both
→
Implement safeguards
→
Engagement quality review
✅
Correct Order (Exam Question) 4 → 2 → 3 → 1: Inform both clients first, then obtain consent, then implement safeguards, then perform engagement quality review.

Key safeguards for conflict of interest:

  • Separate engagement teams and separate engagement partners for each client
  • Confidentiality agreements signed by each engagement team
  • Information barriers / secure data filing to prevent disclosure
  • Monitoring by engagement quality reviewer
  • Not: requiring every employee of the firm to sign a confidentiality agreement — that is disproportionate
⚠️
Important The firm does not have to resign from the existing client to take on the new one. They may choose to resign from either engagement, but neither is compulsory if safeguards can manage the threat. However, if consent is not given by both clients, the firm must choose.

Engagement Acceptance
What the firm must consider before accepting a new audit engagement
Pre-Acceptance Procedures

Before accepting a new audit engagement, the firm must consider the following:

  1. Client integrity — Does management appear to lack integrity? (Red flag: previous auditor resigned without notice; other firms declined the engagement)
  2. Professional competence — Does the firm have the skills and expertise to perform the audit? (Specialist industry, tight deadline, resource availability)
  3. Independence and ethical threats — Can all threats be reduced to an acceptable level? (e.g. existing client who is a competitor creates conflict of interest)
  4. Communication with predecessor auditor — Contact the previous auditor (with client permission) to ask if there are any professional reasons not to accept
  5. Resources — Does the firm have enough staff and time to do the work properly?
  6. Fee — Is the proposed fee adequate for the level of work required?
Red Flags that Increase Risk of Accepting
Red Flag What it Signals
Previous auditor resigned without notice Management may lack integrity; something serious may have happened
Several other audit firms have declined Strong signal that management lacks integrity or there are unresolvable issues
Very tight deadline for completing the audit Insufficient time to gather appropriate evidence — risk to audit quality
Client is a competitor of an existing client Conflict of interest — threats to objectivity and confidentiality
Highly specialised or unfamiliar industry Professional competence concern — firm may lack knowledge
🎯
Exam Scenario — Mickey Co Mickey Co: previous auditor resigned without notice + several firms declined = management integrity risk (reason E). Tight deadline (must file by 30 Sept) = resource/time risk (reason D). Mickey is a competitor of Goofy Co (existing client) = conflict of interest / objectivity and confidentiality risk (reason A).
Engagement Letters

Once a firm accepts an audit engagement, it must issue an engagement letter (ISA 210). This is a contract between the auditor and the client. Key contents:

  • The objective and scope of the audit
  • The responsibilities of the auditor
  • The responsibilities of management (for the financial statements and for providing access)
  • The basis for fee calculation
  • The form of any reports to be issued
  • Identification of the applicable financial reporting framework
📌
When to Reissue A new engagement letter should be issued when there is a significant change in circumstances (e.g. change in scope, change in management, change in financial reporting framework, or after a gap in audit appointments).

Question 1 · Bark & Co / Foliage Co — Rulebook vs Conceptual Framework 2 marks
Select whether the following statements describe a rulebook approach or a conceptual framework approach to ethics.
Statement Answer
Clearly defined laws for the auditor to follow Rulebook
Useful in a dynamic environment Conceptual Framework
A set of guidelines with which the auditor uses judgement to apply to specific circumstances Conceptual Framework
Easy to know what is allowed and not allowed Rulebook
✅ Explanation
The conceptual framework provides guidelines with the objective that the auditor chooses the most appropriate course of action in the circumstances. This allows flexibility to deal with all possible situations — useful in a dynamic environment. The guidelines are professional guidance but are not law. Laws (rulebook) clearly outline what is acceptable and unacceptable in specific circumstances — hence easy to follow but inflexible.
Question 2 · Bark & Co / Foliage Co — Jane Leaf as EQR 2 marks
Jane Leaf has been audit engagement partner for Foliage Co (a listed company) for eight years. As she has completed seven years, she has recently been rotated off the engagement. The new partner Chang Petiole suggests Jane should undertake the Engagement Quality Review this year to maintain a close relationship.
What is the most appropriate response to the suggestion that Jane Leaf takes on the role of Engagement Quality Reviewer?
  • A Jane Leaf could take the review role immediately
  • B Jane Leaf could take the review role immediately but additional safeguards will be required
  • C Jane Leaf should not serve as the EQR for a period of at least five years
  • D Bark & Co will need to consider resigning as auditor of Foliage Co
✅ Answer: C — 5-year cooling-off period
As Foliage Co is a listed company, Jane Leaf must not serve as the Engagement Quality Reviewer until a cooling-off period of five years has passed since she was engagement partner. She cannot return to the engagement in any capacity (partner, EQR, etc.) during this period. Resignation would not be required — this is a manageable threat.
Question 3 · Milberry Co — Threats from Friendship and Outstanding Fees 2 marks
The audit engagement partner and the finance director of Milberry Co have known each other socially for many years and went on holiday together last summer. As a result, the partner has not spoken to the client about the fee for last year's audit, 20% of which is still outstanding.
Select which threat to objectivity is created by each piece of information.
Situation Threat
The partner and FD have known each other socially for many years Familiarity
40% of the fee for last year's audit is still outstanding Self-Interest
✅ Explanation
The social relationship gives rise to a familiarity threat — the partner may be too trusting of someone they know personally. Outstanding fees create a self-interest threat (and can also create intimidation) — the auditor has a financial interest in maintaining the relationship to recover the money, which may bias judgements. Note: the question says 20% still outstanding — this IS significant and should be addressed.
Question 4 · Milberry Co — Outstanding Fees: Most Appropriate Response 2 marks
Which is the MOST appropriate response to the outstanding fees from Milberry Co?
  • A The auditor should resign from the client
  • B The auditor should report the client to the ACCA
  • C Continue working but ensure the credit control department is informed
  • D The auditor's report for this year should not be issued until the fees have been paid
✅ Answer: D
The audit firm must request payment of the outstanding fees. The auditor's report must not be issued until the fees have been paid. This is the correct and proportionate safeguard. Resignation is a last resort. Reporting to ACCA is not appropriate — this is an internal commercial issue. Simply informing credit control (Option C) is insufficient — the report should not be signed off while fees are outstanding.
Question 5 · Milberry Co — Tax Return vs Tax Advice (Self-Review?) 2 marks
Milberry Co's finance director has asked if the firm will prepare the company's tax return and provide tax advice to minimise the amount of tax payable.
Select the option which correctly identifies whether a self-review threat is likely to arise in relation to the tax services requested.
Tax Return Tax Advice
Option A Yes Yes
Option B No No
Option C Yes No
Option D ✓ No Yes
✅ Answer: D
Tax return preparation: Generally does not create a self-review threat. It is mechanical in nature — the auditor is completing the form, not making judgements on figures that affect the financial statements.

Tax advice: Creates a significant self-review threat. The advice could affect the figures in the financial statements, and if the advice later turns out to be incorrect, the auditor may be reluctant to admit or highlight this. This could compromise the audit of the related figures.
Question 6 · Winnie Co / Piglet Co — Conflict of Interest 2 marks
Tigger & Co is the auditor of Winnie Co. Piglet Co — Winnie Co's main competitor — has approached Tigger & Co to act as auditor due to the firm's expertise in the car manufacturing industry.
Which TWO of the following statements are TRUE in respect of the conflict of interest?
  • A Tigger & Co must decline the audit engagement of Piglet Co
  • B Winnie Co and Piglet Co may be concerned that commercially sensitive information may be disclosed by Tigger & Co to their competitor
  • C Tigger & Co must ask permission of ACCA before accepting the audit of Piglet Co
  • D Tigger & Co must obtain consent of both clients before continuing with the engagements
✅ Answers: B and D
The firm does not have to decline Piglet Co's engagement outright (A is false) — it can act for both with safeguards. There is no requirement to consult ACCA (C is false). The correct actions are: both clients may worry about confidential information being leaked (B is true), and the firm must obtain consent from both clients before continuing (D is true). If either refuses, the firm must choose.
Question 7 · Sycamore & Co / Fir Co — Non-Audit Service Threats 2 marks
Fir Co has asked Sycamore & Co to provide: (1) Routine maintenance of payroll records, (2) Assistance with the selection of a new non-executive director, (3) Tax services whereby Sycamore & Co would liaise with the tax authority on Fir Co's behalf.
Identify the threats to independence from providing the non-audit services.
Service Threat
Routine maintenance of payroll records Self-Review
Assistance with selection of a new NED Self-Interest
Tax services — liaise with tax authority on client's behalf Advocacy
✅ Explanation
Payroll → Self-Review: The auditor prepares the payroll figures, then audits those same figures in the financial statements.

NED recruitment → Self-Interest: The auditor would be involved in selecting an officer of the company who has significant influence over the financial statements and the audit. Note: merely reviewing qualifications (not selecting) is acceptable — no threat.

Tax liaison → Advocacy: The auditor is promoting and representing Fir Co's interests when liaising with the tax authority — this is an advocacy threat.
Question 8 · Mickey Co — Acceptance Considerations 2 marks
Mickey Co's previous auditor resigned without prior notice. Mickey Co has approached several other firms who all declined. The audit must be completed by 30 September 20X5 (approx. 3 months). Mickey Co is a main competitor of Goofy Co, NAB & Co's existing client.
Match the acceptance considerations with the MOST appropriate reason for why they should be considered.
Consideration Reason
1. Previous auditor resigned; several firms declined E — Management may lack integrity
2. Audit must be completed before 30 Sept 20X5 D — Firm may not have enough resources
3. Mickey Co is a competitor of Goofy Co (existing client) A — Threats to objectivity and confidentiality
✅ Answers: 1E, 2D, 3A
1E: Difficulties retaining/obtaining an audit firm typically indicates that management lacks integrity and firms are not willing to take on the engagement. This is a strong warning sign.

2D: Three months to complete the full acceptance process, planning, fieldwork and sign-off is tight. The firm needs sufficient resources and must assess whether this is achievable.

3A: As Mickey Co is a main competitor of Goofy Co, a conflict of interest arises that creates threats to objectivity and confidentiality — both clients may worry about their sensitive information being disclosed.
Question 9 · Eeyore Co — Laws & Regulations, Public Interest Reporting 2 marks
During the audit of Eeyore Co, the audit team discovered that the company had developed a device giving false readings during government emissions tests. The engagement partner advised management to report this to the regulator. Management refused, stating that the auditor has a duty of confidentiality.
Select whether the following statements are TRUE or FALSE.
Statement Answer
Senior management is correct that reporting would constitute a breach of confidentiality FALSE
Tigger & Co must report to the appropriate authority if the client refuses TRUE
Tigger & Co must report as it is a public interest matter TRUE
Tigger & Co must report to the parent company and the parent's auditors TRUE
✅ Explanation — ISA 250 (Revised)
Management's claim about confidentiality is wrong. Under ISA 250 (Revised), when the auditor identifies non-compliance that is likely to be a public interest matter, and management refuses to report it themselves, the auditor has a duty to report to the appropriate authority.

False environmental emissions readings affecting government tests = clear public interest issue. As Eeyore Co is a subsidiary of a global company, the matter must also be reported to the parent company and the parent's auditors.
Question 10 · Weed Co — Fee Dependency: Listed Client 2 marks
Weed Co is a listed company. Last year fees were $1.2m (16.6% of Horti & Co's total income). This year fees are expected to be $1.3m (18.1% of total income). Both years exceed 15% of total fee income.
Which of the following actions should Horti & Co take to maintain its objectivity?
  • A Disclose to those charged with governance AND request a pre-issuance review
  • B Request payment of the current year's audit fee in advance
  • C Use separate teams for audit and non-audit services
  • D All of the above
✅ Answer: A (Disclosure + Pre-issuance review)
Weed Co is listed and fees have exceeded 15% for two consecutive years. The required actions are: (1) Disclose to those charged with governance at Weed Co, and (2) carry out a pre-issuance review (before the audit opinion is signed) by an external accountant. Using separate teams (Option C) addresses self-review from non-audit services but does not reduce the fee dependency threat. Requesting payment in advance (B) does not address fee dependency.

Golden Rules for Exam Answers
  1. Resignation is ALWAYS the last resort — only when no safeguards can work. Never say "must resign" unless the threat is truly irresolvable.
  2. Preparing a tax return ≠ self-review threat (mechanical). Tax advice = self-review threat.
  3. Reviewing qualifications for NED recruitment = OK. Actually selecting/appointing = management responsibility = self-review threat.
  4. Gift as inducement must be declined regardless of size. Trivial gift offered genuinely may be accepted.
  5. Confidentiality is not absolute — overridden by law (e.g. money laundering, public interest matters).
  6. Both clients must consent before the firm acts for two competing clients. The firm does not have to choose its existing client.
  7. Familiarity from ex-employee depends on seniority — low seniority (audit senior) = manageable; high seniority (audit manager, partner) = significant.
  8. Fee dependency safeguard for listed clients = disclose to those charged with governance + pre-issuance review. Not resignation.
