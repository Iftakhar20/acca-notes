A · Audit Framework, Regulation & Ethics

What audit actually is

External audit gives reasonable (not absolute) assurance that the financial statements give a true and fair view and are free from material misstatement. It is a positive opinion. A review engagement gives only limited assurance — negative ("nothing came to our attention…").

Story: Audit is a health check-up, not a guarantee you'll never get sick. The doctor (auditor) examines samples, not every cell, so it's reasonable not absolute assurance. "Reasonable = high but not 100%."
Element of assuranceMemory
5 elements: Three-party relationship, Subject matter, Criteria, Evidence, Report"3 SCER" — 3 parties (practitioner, responsible party, intended user) + Subject matter, Criteria, Evidence, Report

Fundamental ethical principles

PISCO

  • Professional behaviour — obey laws, no disrepute
  • Integrity — straightforward & honest
  • S = professional competence & due Skill/care — keep knowledge current
  • Confidentiality — don't disclose/use client info
  • Objectivity — no bias, no conflict, no undue influence

Threats to independence

SAFI-I — Self-interest, Self-review, Advocacy, Familiarity, Intimidation

Story — picture one audit client, "RiskyCo":
  • Self-interest = you own shares / huge fee → you profit if they look good.
  • Self-review = you checked your own homework (audited the system you built).
  • Advocacy = you became their lawyer / cheerleader (promoted their shares).
  • Familiarity = the FD is your best friend / spouse (too cosy).
  • Intimidation = they threaten to sack you unless you agree.

Confidentiality can be broken only when: obligatory (law/court, e.g. money laundering, terrorism) or voluntary with proper justification (public duty, to protect the member). Mnemonic: "Court order or money-laundering = mouth opens."

Corporate governance (UK Code principles)

  • Split Chair and CEO (no over-mighty individual).
  • At least half the board = independent non-execs (NEDs).
  • Audit committee = all independent NEDs (≥1 with recent financial expertise) — oversees auditor, internal controls, risk, internal audit.
  • Remuneration & Nomination committees = NEDs decide pay & appointments.
Story: Audit committee = the "referees" in striped shirts who don't play but keep the directors honest and shield the external auditor from management pressure.

B · Planning & Risk Assessment

The Audit Risk model

Audit Risk = Inherent Risk × Control Risk × Detection Risk

  • Inherent — susceptibility to error before controls (complex estimates, cash business).
  • Control — risk controls won't catch it.
  • Detection — risk the auditor misses it. The only one the auditor controls (by doing more/better work).
Story: A leaky boat. Inherent = how leaky the hull is. Control = quality of the bilge pump. Detection = how hard you bail with a bucket. If hull & pump are bad (high IR & CR), you must bail harder → lower detection risk → more substantive testing.

Writing an audit-risk answer (exam technique)

1️⃣ Identify the risk from the scenario (a fact + why it's risky).
2️⃣ Say which balance/area is affected and the direction — over- or under-stated.
3️⃣ Give the auditor's response (a procedure to address it).
⚠ Your two repeat slips: (1) always state the direction of misstatement (e.g. "revenue overstated"); (2) audit risk ≠ business risk. Audit risk = FS could be misstated. Business risk = threat to the company's objectives/survival. AA wants audit risk.

Materiality (rules of thumb)

BenchmarkRange
Profit before tax5% – 10%
Revenue½% – 1%
Total assets1% – 2%

Performance materiality = a figure set lower than materiality to leave room for the aggregation of small errors. By nature = some items are material regardless of size (director transactions, fraud, items that turn profit→loss).

Analytical procedures — used 3 times

StagePurpose
Planning (mandatory)Risk assessment — spot unusual trends/areas of risk
SubstantiveAs evidence on relationships (optional)
Final review (mandatory)Do the FS make sense overall? Consistent with auditor's understanding
⚠ Repeat slip: don't confuse planning-stage (find risks) with completion-stage (sense-check final FS) purposes.

Planning essentials

  • Engagement letter = the contract; sets out scope, responsibilities, fees. Sent before work; re-issue if scope/management changes.
  • Interim audit (controls testing, early work) vs Final audit (year-end balances, opinion).
  • Professional scepticism — a questioning mind; assume nothing.
  • Fraud: management is responsible for prevention; auditor only gives reasonable assurance and stays sceptical.
  • Documentation test: would an experienced auditor with no prior connection understand the work from the file?

C · Internal Control

Components of internal control

CRIME — Control environment, Risk assessment process, Information system, Monitoring of controls, Control activities (the E rounds it out — Entity-level "Environment").

Types of control activity

SOAP-SPAM light version → key ones: Authorisation, Performance reviews, Information processing, Physical controls, Segregation of duties.

Story — "SoD = the 3 keys rule": never let one person Authorise, Record, and Custody (ARC) the same transaction. The cashier who counts the money should not also write the ledger and sign the cheques — else they can steal and cover it up.

The deficiency answer format

1️⃣ Deficiency — what's missing/weak.
2️⃣ Implication / consequence — what could go wrong (fraud, error, loss).
3️⃣ Recommendation — a specific control to fix it.

Tests of controls confirm a control operated (e.g. inspect for signature). They differ from substantive tests, which check the £ figure is right.

Cycle quick-fire (deficiency → control)

CycleClassic control to recommend
Sales / ReceivablesCredit checks before sale; sequentially numbered GDNs & invoices; authorised price list; monthly statements/reconciliations
Purchases / PayablesApproved supplier list; orders authorised; 3-way match (PO–GRN–invoice); supplier statement recs
PayrollAuthorise new starters & rate changes; clock-in/biometric; payroll reconciliation; segregate preparer from payer
CashDaily banking; restricted till access; surprise counts; bank recs by someone independent
InventoryRegular counts; restricted warehouse access; reorder levels; obsolescence reviews

Internal Audit (IA)

  • IA = internal, reports to audit committee; helps governance & risk. External audit = statutory opinion to shareholders.
  • IA assignments: value-for-money (3 E's: Economy, Efficiency, Effectiveness), operational, IT/systems, fraud, financial.
  • External auditor may use IA's work if IA is objective, competent & systematic — but the opinion stays the external auditor's responsibility.

D · Audit Evidence

Financial statement assertions

Transactions / events (P&L) — COCOA

  • Completeness
  • Occurrence
  • Cut-off
  • clO... Classification
  • Accuracy
  • (+ Presentation)

Balances (SOFP) — CEAVR

  • Completeness
  • Existence
  • Accuracy & valuation
  • V... (allocation)
  • Rights & obligations
Story to split the two big confusable ones: Existence goes from book → real world ("does this recorded asset truly exist?" — overstatement test). Completeness goes from real world → book ("is everything that should be recorded actually in the books?" — understatement test). Arrow direction = the assertion.

Types of procedure

AEIOU — Analytical procedures, Enquiry & confirmation, Inspection, Observation, U = recalc/reperformance.

⚠ Repeat slip: enquiry alone is weak — always pair it with stronger verbs: inspect, recalculate, agree to, trace, confirm, observe, reperform. Start procedures with an action verb, not "ask."

Reliability of evidence (strongest → weakest)

  • External > internal
  • Written > oral
  • Auditor-obtained > entity-provided
  • Original > photocopy/scan

Mnemonic: "WOE" backwards — the best evidence is Written, Original, External & auditor-generated.

Substantive procedures — the headline ones

AreaKey tests
ReceivablesPositive/negative confirmation circularisation; after-date cash receipts; review aged listing for irrecoverable debts
InventoryAttend the count (existence & condition); test counts both directions; check NRV vs cost (lower); cut-off of last GRN/GDN
PayablesSupplier statement reconciliations (best — external); search for unrecorded liabilities (post year-end invoices) — completeness focus
BankBank confirmation letter; review bank reconciliation, agree outstanding items
Non-current assetsPhysically inspect (existence); agree to invoices (cost/rights); recalc depreciation; review for impairment
RevenueAnalytical review vs PY/budget; cut-off testing around year-end; agree to GDNs & cash
Story: For receivables you ask the customer ("do you owe £X?") — overstatement worry. For payables you suspect the company hides bills, so you hunt for missing invoices — understatement worry. Receivables = exist? Payables = complete?

Other evidence topics

  • Sampling: statistical (random/systematic) vs non-statistical (judgement). Project errors to the population.
  • CAATs: Test data (dummy data through the system to test controls) and Audit software (interrogates real data — recalc, exception reports). Faster, tests 100%, but set-up cost.
  • Written representations: management's confirmation of assertions; supports but never replaces other evidence (e.g. completeness of liabilities, fraud).
  • Using an expert / service org / component auditor: assess their competence, objectivity & capability; opinion remains the auditor's.
  • Estimates & NFP: estimates are inherently risky (review assumptions/data); not-for-profit → controls often weaker, more reliance on substantive work.

E · Review & Reporting high marks

Subsequent events (IAS 10 + ISA 560)

PeriodAuditor's duty
Year-end → auditor's report dateActive duty to search for events (review minutes, post year-end items, enquiries)
Report date → FS issuedNo duty to search; but if becomes aware, discuss & consider amending report
After FS issuedNo duty; if material fact emerges, consider new/amended report
⚠ Repeat slip: the active-search period ends at the auditor's report date, NOT the date the FS are approved/issued.

Going concern

GC basis appropriate, material uncertainty disclosed adequately → Unmodified opinion + a "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section.
Material uncertainty NOT disclosed → Modified opinion (qualified or adverse).
GC basis is wrong (company will fail, but FS prepared on GC) → Adverse opinion.

The Audit Opinion decision tree

Q1 — Is there a problem? Either a misstatement (you disagree) OR a lack of sufficient evidence (a limitation).
Q2 — How big?
• Immaterial → Unmodified, clean opinion.
• Material but NOT pervasive → Qualified — "except for"
• Material AND pervasive →
   – Misstatement → Adverse ("do NOT give a true & fair view")
   – Lack of evidence → Disclaimer ("we do not express an opinion")
Story — the two-knob mixer: Knob 1 = cause (disagree vs can't-see). Knob 2 = size (material vs pervasive). Pervasive means it poisons the whole picture, not just one corner. Disagree + whole picture = Adverse. Can't-see + whole picture = Disclaimer. Anything just "material" = "except for".
Cause ↓ / Effect →Material (not pervasive)Material AND pervasive
Misstatement (disagreement)Qualified "except for"Adverse
Insufficient evidence (limitation)Qualified "except for"Disclaimer

Emphasis of Matter vs Other Matter vs KAM

ParagraphWhat it is
Emphasis of Matter (EoM)Draws attention to a matter correctly disclosed in the FS that is fundamental to understanding (e.g. major litigation, going-concern note). Opinion stays unmodified.
Other Matter (OM)A matter not in the FS but relevant to users' understanding of the audit/report.
Key Audit Matters (KAM)For listed entities — areas of most significance in the audit. Informational, not a modification.
⚠ Repeat slip: An EoM never accompanies a modified opinion, and EoM ≠ qualified opinion. EoM = "look here, but the numbers are right." A modified opinion = "the numbers/evidence have a problem." Keep them apart.

Structure of the unmodified report (order matters)

  • Title & Addressee → Opinion → Basis for Opinion → (Material Uncertainty re GC if any) → KAM (listed) → Other Information → Responsibilities (management & auditor) → Signature, date, address.

In a modified report the order flips slightly: the "Basis for Qualified/Adverse/Disclaimer of Opinion" paragraph explains the problem and comes right after the modified opinion.

★ Golden Rules — your personal banana skins

  • Always state the direction of misstatement (over/understated) in every audit-risk point.
  • Never use "enquiry" alone — pair it with inspect / recalc / agree / confirm / reperform.
  • EoM ≠ qualified; EoM never sits with a modified opinion.
  • Subsequent events active-search ends at the auditor's report date.
  • Audit risk ≠ business risk — AA wants risk the FS are misstated.
  • Planning-stage analytical procedures = find risk; completion-stage = sense-check FS.
  • Opinion grid: cause (disagree / can't-see) × size (material / pervasive) → except-for / Adverse / Disclaimer.
  • Receivables = existence (overstatement); payables = completeness (understatement).
  • Detection risk is the only one the auditor controls — lower it with more testing.
