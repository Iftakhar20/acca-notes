Why Audit Reports Matter — The Big Picture

The auditor's report is the final product of the entire audit. Everything we do — risk assessment, evidence gathering, sampling — feeds into this one document. Users of financial statements (banks, investors, regulators) read it to decide whether they can trust the accounts.

Core Concept
The auditor's job is to express an opinion on whether the financial statements give a true and fair view (or are presented fairly in all material respects) in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework.

The Key ISAs for This Topic

ISA Topic What it covers
ISA 700 Forming and Expressing an Opinion Content and structure of the auditor's report; conditions for unmodified opinion
ISA 705 Modifications to the Opinion Qualified, Adverse, Disclaimer — when and why
ISA 706 Emphasis of Matter & Other Matter Paragraphs Additional communications within unmodified reports
ISA 570 Going Concern Auditor's responsibilities; impact on report when issues arise
ISA 701 Key Audit Matters Communication of KAMs for listed entities
ISA 720 Other Information Auditor's responsibility for information in annual reports outside the FS
02

Structure of the Auditor's Report (ISA 700)

⚠️ Exam Alert
Questions ask you to match elements to their purpose (Q179 in the Kaplan kit) or identify what should/shouldn't be included. Learn all elements and their purpose precisely.

Required Elements — Know All of These

1

Title

Must clearly state "Independent Auditor's Report" — confirms independence from the entity and identifies the document type.

2

Addressee

Identifies who the report is directed to (typically shareholders or members). Defines the intended users and limits liability exposure.

3

Auditor's Opinion

The core conclusion — states whether the financial statements give a true and fair view. Uses specific wording depending on opinion type.

4

Basis for Opinion

Describes the professional standards applied (ISAs), confirms independence, and confirms sufficient appropriate evidence was obtained. This gives users confidence the report can be relied upon.

5

Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern (if applicable)

Required when there is a material uncertainty about going concern that is adequately disclosed in the financial statements. Appears separately from the opinion.

6

Key Audit Matters (listed entities only)

Communicates the most significant matters from the audit. Draws attention to significant matters discussed with those charged with governance. Required by ISA 701 for listed company audits.

7

Other Information

Describes the auditor's responsibilities regarding other information in the annual report (e.g. chair's statement). The auditor reads it but does not audit it.

8

Responsibilities of Management

Explains that management (directors) are responsible for preparing the financial statements and for internal control.

9

Auditor's Responsibilities

Describes what the auditor does — obtain reasonable assurance, apply professional judgement, assess risks, etc.

10

Name of Engagement Partner

Identifies the individual responsible for the audit opinion — allows users to know who to contact with queries and increases accountability.

11

Auditor's Signature, Address & Date

The date is critical — it indicates the point up to which subsequent events procedures were performed. The report cannot be dated before the financial statements are approved.

✅ Exam Tip — Q179 type question
Know these matchings: Title → "Clearly identifies as Independent Auditor's Report" | Addressee → "Identifies intended user" | Basis for Opinion → "Describes professional standards applied; confidence in reliability" | KAMs → "Significant matters discussed with those charged with governance" | Engagement partner name → "Person responsible for the opinion"
03

The Unmodified Opinion — "Clean" Report

An unmodified opinion is the best possible outcome. It means the auditor is satisfied that the financial statements give a true and fair view. This is what most audits conclude with.

✅ Conditions for Unmodified Opinion
  • The financial statements are prepared in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework (e.g. IFRS)
  • No material misstatements exist (or any identified have been corrected by management)
  • The auditor has obtained sufficient appropriate audit evidence

The Exact Wording — Learn This

Unmodified Opinion Wording

"In our opinion, the financial statements give a true and fair view of the financial position of [Entity] as at [Date] and of its financial performance and cash flows for the year then ended in accordance with [IFRS / applicable framework]."

Important: What Doesn't Modify the Opinion

Several additional elements can appear in a report WITHOUT modifying the opinion:

  • Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern section
  • Emphasis of Matter paragraph
  • Other Matter paragraph
  • Key Audit Matters section
  • Other Information section

These are additional communications, not modifications. The opinion paragraph itself still says "true and fair view."

⚠️ Common Exam Trap
Students confuse "unmodified with going concern section" with a "modified opinion." They are different. The opinion paragraph is only modified when the auditor issues a Qualified, Adverse, or Disclaimer opinion.
04

Modified Opinions (ISA 705) — The Three Types

A modification to the opinion is needed in one of two circumstances:

Circumstance A: Material Misstatement

The auditor has obtained sufficient evidence but concludes the financial statements ARE materially misstated (management has refused to correct).

Circumstance B: Inability to Obtain Evidence

The auditor cannot obtain sufficient appropriate evidence — for whatever reason — and therefore cannot conclude whether the statements are free from material misstatement.

The Three Modified Opinions — Key Differences

Opinion Type Cause Nature of Issue Wording Used Report Section Changed
Qualified Material misstatement OR inability to obtain evidence Material but NOT pervasive — affects specific areas only "Except for…" Basis for Qualified Opinion + Opinion
Adverse Material misstatement only Material AND pervasive — affects the statements as a whole "Do not give a true and fair view" Basis for Adverse Opinion + Opinion
Disclaimer Inability to obtain evidence only Material AND pervasive — potential effect is so significant auditor cannot form any opinion "Do not express an opinion" Basis for Disclaimer + Opinion (no opinion)

Understanding "Pervasive" — This is the Key Decision

Pervasive means the effects are not limited to a specific element — they affect many parts of the financial statements, or represent a substantial proportion. Think of it as: would someone reading the financials be fundamentally misled about the company as a whole?

Material but NOT Pervasive → Qualified

  • One specific balance is wrong (e.g. inventory overstated by 6% of profit)
  • One audit area has no evidence (e.g. can't verify opening stock)
  • The rest of the financial statements are fine

Material AND Pervasive → Adverse or Disclaimer

  • Error is enormous relative to the financial statements (e.g. 86% of profit)
  • Affects multiple interconnected areas throughout the statements
  • If unable to evidence — the entire audit basis is undermined

The Wording Matrix — Memorise This

Opinion Exact wording used What it means to a reader
Unmodified "…give a true and fair view" Financial statements are reliable — no material issues
Qualified "Except for [the issue], …give a true and fair view" Mostly reliable, but there is a specific problem you should know about
Adverse "…do NOT give a true and fair view" Statements are fundamentally unreliable as a whole — do not rely on them
Disclaimer "We do NOT express an opinion" We could not obtain enough evidence — we simply cannot say anything
⚠️ Exam Trap — Q180
A common MCQ asks you to match opinion type with wording. The trap is confusing Adverse and Qualified wording. Adverse = "do NOT give a true and fair view." Qualified = "EXCEPT FOR [X], give a true and fair view." Disclaimer = "do NOT express an opinion."

What Changes in a Modified Report?

  • The "Basis for Opinion" section becomes "Basis for Qualified/Adverse/Disclaimer Opinion" and describes the issue in detail
  • The "Opinion" section uses the modified wording
  • For a Disclaimer, the Auditor's Responsibilities section is also amended

Worked Example — Materiality Calculation for Opinion

🧮 Example: Paddington Co (from Kaplan Kit)

Profit before tax: $29m

Issue Amount % of Profit Material? Pervasive? Opinion if uncorrected
Lawsuit (contingent liability not disclosed) $3m 10.3% Yes (material) No Qualified
Warranty provision not recognised $25m 86.2% Yes (highly material) Yes (pervasive) Adverse
Depreciation not charged $1m 3.4% Not material No Unmodified (immaterial)
Intangible assets overstated $12m 41.4% Yes (very material) Borderline Qualified or Adverse

The warranty provision at 86% of profit = material AND pervasive → Adverse opinion

Going Concern and the Auditor's Report (ISA 570)

Responsibilities — Directors vs Auditors

Directors' Responsibilities

  • Assess whether the company can continue to trade for the foreseeable future (at least 12 months from the date of financial statements)
  • Prepare cash flow forecasts and scenarios
  • Disclose any material uncertainties in the financial statements
  • Decide the basis of preparation (going concern vs break-up)

Auditor's Responsibilities

  • Evaluate management's going concern assessment
  • Assess whether the period assessed is adequate (must be ≥12 months from the balance sheet date)
  • Obtain sufficient evidence about the appropriateness of the going concern basis
  • Determine the impact on the auditor's report based on findings
⚠️ Key Exam Point — Q171 type
Only directors disclose going concern in the financial statements. The auditor does not make disclosure in the financial statements — the auditor highlights the disclosure in the auditor's report. Also: directors assess 12 months from the balance sheet date, not from when the statements are issued.

Going Concern Indicators

Financial Indicators

  • Net current liabilities / negative working capital
  • Significant operating losses
  • Overdraft facility due for renewal and uncertain outcome
  • Significant cash outflows forecast
  • Loan repayments falling due that may not be met
  • Inability to pay suppliers on time (extended payables)
  • Suppliers withdrawing credit terms

Operational Indicators

  • Loss of key management / staff with no replacements
  • Loss of major customer or supplier
  • Main supplier ceasing to trade
  • New competitor taking significant market share
  • Major court case likely to succeed against the company

Audit Procedures for Going Concern Assessment

Procedures (ISA 570)
  • Review cash flow forecasts prepared by management and assess reasonableness of assumptions
  • Inspect correspondence with the bank regarding overdraft or loan facility renewal
  • Inspect board minutes for discussions on going concern, financing plans, and management's strategy
  • Enquire of management whether alternative sources of finance have been explored
  • Review the terms of any financing agreements for covenant breaches
  • Perform analytical procedures (ratios) — quick ratio, gearing, interest cover
  • Review post-year-end management accounts for evidence of improvement or deterioration
  • Obtain a written representation from management confirming their going concern assessment and plans
  • Review legal correspondence for claims that could affect liquidity

Impact on the Auditor's Report — The Key Grid

This grid is directly examined in MCQ format (Q173 in the kit). Memorise it exactly.

Situation Going Concern Basis Appropriate? Disclosures Adequate? Opinion Report Impact
Material uncertainty exists — well disclosed Yes Yes Unmodified Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern section added to the report
Material uncertainty exists — NOT adequately disclosed Yes No Qualified or Adverse Financial statements are materially misstated → modified opinion (usually qualified; adverse if pervasive)
Going concern basis is NOT appropriate (should be break-up basis) — management uses it anyway No N/A Adverse Fundamental departure from framework → adverse opinion (material and pervasive)
No material uncertainty exists Yes N/A Unmodified No additional communication needed — standard report
💡 Plain English — The Going Concern Logic

Think of it this way: if the company is struggling but the directors have told us about it properly in the notes, we write an unmodified opinion (they've done their job) but we highlight the uncertainty with a special paragraph. If they've hidden the problem or not disclosed it adequately, the financial statements are misleading → we modify the opinion. If the whole going concern basis is wrong (company is already effectively dead) → we say the statements are completely wrong → adverse.

When Should the Break-Up Basis Be Used?

Break-Up Basis Required When:
  • The company has already ceased to trade
  • A decision has been made to close or wind up the company
  • The company has no realistic alternative but to cease trading (e.g. can't pay debts when they fall due and has no rescue options)

NOT when there are merely uncertainties about going concern — in that case, going concern basis still applies with disclosure.

Emphasis of Matter & Other Matter Paragraphs (ISA 706)

These are additional paragraphs in an otherwise unmodified report. They do not change the opinion — they draw attention to important matters.

Emphasis of Matter (EoM)

Definition & When Used

An EoM paragraph is used when the auditor wishes to draw users' attention to a matter that is already adequately disclosed in the financial statements and which is fundamental to users' understanding of those statements.

Examples of when EoM is appropriate:

  • Uncertainty relating to a significant pending litigation (if adequately disclosed)
  • Early application of a new accounting standard that has a major effect
  • A major catastrophe after the balance sheet date that is disclosed in the notes
  • Significant uncertainty that management has disclosed properly
⚠️ Note
EoM is NOT used for going concern uncertainties — those get their own specific "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section. EoM is for other fundamental matters.

Other Matter Paragraph

Definition & When Used

An Other Matter paragraph refers to a matter that is not presented or disclosed in the financial statements but which the auditor considers necessary to communicate — typically matters relating to the auditor's own responsibilities.

Examples of when Other Matter is appropriate:

  • Restricting the use or liability of the auditor's report to specific parties
  • Explaining that the prior year was audited by a different firm
  • Additional reporting responsibilities required by regulators
  • Matters relevant to users' understanding of the audit or auditor's role

The Critical Distinction — Q169 type MCQ

Paragraph Type Where the matter is disclosed What it relates to Modifies opinion?
Emphasis of Matter Already in the financial statements (adequately disclosed) Matters fundamental to users' understanding of the FS No
Other Matter NOT in the financial statements Auditor's responsibilities or other non-FS matters No
Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern Adequately disclosed in the FS Specifically going concern uncertainties No
💡 Memory Trick
EoM = "I want to emphasise something already in the accounts." Other Matter = "I have something to say that is NOT in the accounts — about my own role/report."

Key Audit Matters (ISA 701) — Listed Entities Only

When Does ISA 701 Apply?
KAMs are required only for listed entities (companies listed on a stock exchange). They are not required for private companies, though auditors may voluntarily include them.

What Is a KAM?

A Key Audit Matter is one of the matters communicated to those charged with governance (the audit committee / board) that, in the auditor's professional judgement, required the most significant attention during the audit.

How the Auditor Determines KAMs — Factors Considered

These factors are directly examined — you need to know them for parts (d)(i) of Section B questions:

ISA 701 Criteria — Factors that make something a KAM
  • Areas of higher assessed risk of material misstatement or significant risks identified in the audit
  • Areas involving significant management judgement or estimation uncertainty (e.g. provisions, fair values, going concern)
  • The effect on the audit of significant events or transactions in the period
  • Areas where significant auditor judgement was required (e.g. evaluating complex accounting treatments)
  • Matters that required significant auditor attention — large or complex balances, significant misstatements found and corrected
  • Whether the matter was communicated to those charged with governance (this is a prerequisite)

Content of a KAM Section in the Auditor's Report — Part (d)(ii)

For each KAM, the report must describe:

  1. Why the matter was determined to be a KAM — what made it require significant attention
  2. How the auditor addressed the matter — the key audit procedures performed, judgements made, and evidence obtained
  3. A reference to the relevant disclosure in the financial statements (if any)
📝 Worked Example — Latte Co / Spinach Co KAM (from Kaplan Kit)

Scenario: A provision was significantly increased (e.g. legal claim provision increased from $0.25m to $0.6m, or inventory written down significantly). The engagement partner determines this is a KAM.


Why it is a KAM:

  • The provision involved significant management judgement in estimating the likely outcome of the legal claim / the net realisable value of inventory
  • It represented a significant risk of material misstatement — the original estimate was materially understated
  • The matter required significant auditor attention — specialist input, legal correspondence review, and judgement about adequacy of the provision
  • The issue was discussed and communicated to those charged with governance

Content of the KAM Section:

  • Description of the KAM: The valuation of the provision for the legal claim (or inventory write-down) as at [year end]
  • Why the matter was considered a KAM: The provision required significant judgement and was a significant risk in the audit
  • How the matter was addressed: Procedures performed — reviewed legal correspondence, discussed with management, evaluated assumptions, compared to external evidence
  • Reference to disclosures: See Note X to the financial statements

What KAMs Are NOT

⚠️ Common Misconceptions
  • KAMs are NOT a modification to the opinion — they appear in an unmodified report
  • KAMs are NOT all matters communicated to those charged with governance — only the most significant ones
  • KAMs are NOT all matters that were material — they require significant auditor attention specifically
  • KAMs are NOT used in place of a modified opinion when misstatements exist — a separate qualified/adverse opinion is needed

Other Information in the Annual Report (ISA 720)

"Other information" means information included in the annual report other than the financial statements and auditor's report — for example, a chairman's statement, director's report, or investor presentation sections.

Auditor's Responsibilities Under ISA 720

What the Auditor Does
  • Read the other information to identify whether there are material inconsistencies between it and:
    • The audited financial statements
    • The auditor's knowledge obtained during the audit
  • Does NOT audit the other information — the auditor expresses no assurance opinion on it
  • The report includes an "Other Information" section describing the auditor's responsibilities

What Happens When There is an Inconsistency?

Scenario Auditor's Action Impact on Report Impact on Opinion
Chair's statement contradicts the financial statements Discuss with management, request correction. If management refuses... Describe the inconsistency in the Other Information section Opinion not modified — the FS themselves are fine
Other information appears to misstate a fact known from the audit Report the misstatement in the Other Information section of the report Description in Other Information section Opinion not modified
Other information causes the auditor to reconsider whether the FS are misstated Revise FS audit work as needed; if FS are now believed misstated, modify opinion May lead to modified opinion May modify opinion
✅ Key Point — Q163/Q164 type

An inconsistency in the chair's statement → handled in the Other Information section of the report, NOT an Emphasis of Matter, NOT a modification. The audit opinion covers the financial statements only, not the chair's statement. Failing to highlight the inconsistency would mislead users and undermine the credibility of the audit.

Why Does the Auditor Read Other Information at All?

  • Users of annual reports read the whole document, not just the FS — they could be misled by contradictory information
  • If inconsistencies are not highlighted, users may believe the auditor approved all the content
  • It protects the credibility of the auditor's report itself

Worked Exam Scenarios — Kaplan Kit Style

Objective Test (MCQ) Type Scenarios

📝 Q1 — Opinion type (based on Kaplan Q180) 2 marks

Match each opinion type to the correct wording:

Opinion Wording
Unmodified A – "Except for…"
Qualified B – "Do not express an opinion"
Adverse C – "Give a true and fair view"
Disclaimer D – "Do not give a true and fair view"
Unmodified → C | Qualified → A | Adverse → D | Disclaimer → B

Unmodified = "give a true and fair view" (clean opinion)
Qualified = "except for [issue], give a true and fair view" (isolated problem)
Adverse = "do NOT give a true and fair view" (fundamentally wrong)
Disclaimer = "do NOT express an opinion" (can't form a view at all)
📝 Q2 — Going Concern Report Impact (based on Kaplan Q155/Q173) 2 marks

MMT Travel Agency has a material uncertainty about going concern due to uncertain overdraft renewal. The directors have agreed to include adequate disclosure in the financial statements. The engagement partner confirms the going concern basis is appropriate.

What is the correct opinion and additional report communication?

  • A — Unmodified opinion with no additional communication
  • B — Unmodified opinion with Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern section
  • C — Modified opinion with Emphasis of Matter paragraph
  • D — Modified opinion with Key Audit Matters
Answer: B

Going concern basis is appropriate (partner confirmed this) + adequate disclosures made = unmodified opinion.
However, because the matter is fundamental to users' understanding, the report must include a separate "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" section. This is NOT an Emphasis of Matter — it's its own specific section under ISA 570.
The opinion paragraph itself is still unmodified.
📝 Q3 — Adverse Opinion Identification (based on Kaplan Q175/Q176) 2 marks

During the audit of Paddington Co (profit before tax $29m), the following issues remain uncorrected:

  • Warranty provision not recognised: $25m (86% of profit)
  • Lawsuit contingent liability not disclosed: $3m (10.3% of profit)
  • Depreciation not charged: $1m (3.4% of profit)

Which issue is most likely to lead to an ADVERSE opinion?

Answer: Warranty provision ($25m, 86% of profit)

At 86% of profit, this is material AND pervasive — it fundamentally misrepresents the company's financial position. A pervasive misstatement means the financial statements are unreliable as a whole → adverse opinion.

The lawsuit (10.3%) is material but not pervasive → qualified.
The depreciation (3.4%) is not material → unmodified (no action needed).
📝 Q4 — Other Information / Chair's Statement (based on Kaplan Q163) 2 marks

During the audit of Mississippi Co, the auditor identifies that the Chair's statement in the annual report contradicts the financial statements (it claims a profit when the FS show a loss). Management refuses to amend the Chair's statement.

How should this inconsistency be addressed in the auditor's report?

  • A — Within the Other Information section
  • B — Within an Emphasis of Matter paragraph
  • C — Within an Other Matter paragraph
  • D — Within the Auditor's Opinion section
Answer: A — Other Information section

The Chair's statement is "other information" — it is not part of the audited financial statements. The auditor does not audit it but must read it.
If there is an inconsistency with the FS, this must be described in the Other Information section of the auditor's report.
The audit opinion covers the financial statements only, so the opinion itself is not modified.
EoM (B) is for matters in the FS themselves. Other Matter (C) is for auditor's responsibilities. Neither fits here.
📝 Q5 — Inability to Obtain Evidence (based on Kaplan Q260 Violet & Co) 2 marks

Daisy Co: Revenue $15.6m, receivables $3.4m, profit before tax $2m. The sales ledger was corrupted by a virus after year end. No backups are available. The audit team cannot test revenue or year-end receivables (combined = $19m = highly material and pervasive).

What is the impact on the auditor's report if this issue remains unresolved?

Answer: Disclaimer of opinion

This is a case of inability to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence.
Revenue ($15.6m) and receivables ($3.4m) combined are extremely material. Revenue is typically the single largest figure in the FS — not being able to audit it is pervasive.
Lack of evidence + pervasive = Disclaimer of opinion (not Qualified — that would be for material but not pervasive).
The "Basis for Disclaimer" section would explain why no opinion could be formed.
📝 Q6 — KAM Section B Type (based on Kaplan Q65d, Q69d) 5 marks — Section B style

You are auditing Spinach Co (listed). During the audit, the team identified five slow-moving product lines where NRV was below cost. A significant inventory write-down was required under IAS 2. The engagement partner has determined this is a KAM.

(i) Describe the factors the engagement partner considered in determining this is a KAM. (2.5 marks)

(ii) Describe the content of the KAM section for Spinach Co. (2.5 marks)

Part (i) — Factors:
• The inventory valuation involved significant management judgement — estimating NRV required assumptions about future selling prices and demand for slow-moving items
• Inventory is a significant risk of material misstatement — valuing at the lower of cost and NRV requires assessment of each product line
• The write-down was a significant event in the period — it had a material impact on profit
• The matter required significant auditor attention — the team needed to challenge management's NRV estimates and inspect supporting evidence
• The issue was communicated to those charged with governance — prerequisite for KAM designation

Part (ii) — Content of KAM section:
• Title/Description: "Valuation of Inventory — Net Realisable Value of slow-moving product lines"
• Why a KAM: Inventory valuation at year end involved significant management judgement in estimating NRV of five slow-moving product lines, representing a significant risk area
• How the auditor addressed it: Procedures performed — reviewed management's NRV estimates, compared estimated selling prices to actual post-year-end sales, assessed reasonableness of assumptions, tested a sample of lines against sales records
• Reference: See Note X — Accounting Policies and Note Y — Inventories in the financial statements
📝 Q7 — Going Concern Procedures (based on Kaplan Q77d) Section B style — 5 marks

Jasmine Co: overdraft of $5.1m (up from $1.2m), facility due for renewal in August. Directors are confident it will be renewed. Post-year-end, the bank has stated they will not decide until after the auditor's report is signed.

Describe audit procedures to assess whether Jasmine Co is a going concern, AND describe the impact on the auditor's report if (a) adequate disclosures are made and (b) disclosures are inadequate.

Procedures:
• Inspect correspondence with the bank about overdraft renewal — assess likelihood of renewal and any conditions
• Review the cash flow forecast and stress-test assumptions — what happens if overdraft is not renewed?
• Enquire whether alternative financing has been explored (e.g. other banks, equity, asset sales)
• Review board minutes for discussions about the overdraft and management's contingency plans
• Obtain written representations from directors about their assessment and plans
• Perform analytical procedures — calculate key ratios (quick ratio, gearing) to identify deteriorating trends
• Review post-year-end management accounts to assess whether the financial position is worsening

Report Impact:
If adequate disclosures made: The auditor is satisfied the going concern basis is appropriate. Issue an unmodified opinion + include a Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern section in the report highlighting the overdraft renewal uncertainty.

If disclosures are inadequate (directors refuse to disclose): The financial statements are materially misstated due to insufficient disclosure. Issue a qualified opinion (or adverse if the omission is so significant it is pervasive). The "Basis for Qualified Opinion" section explains the inadequacy of disclosure.

Quick Reference & Exam Technique

The "5 Big Questions" for Any Audit Report Scenario

  1. Is there an issue at all? If no → unmodified, done.
  2. Is the issue material? Apply materiality benchmarks (5-10% profit, 1-2% assets, ½-1% revenue). If not material → unmodified, done.
  3. Is it a misstatement or a lack of evidence? Misstatement = know the number is wrong. Lack of evidence = can't verify.
  4. Is it pervasive? Does it affect the statements as a whole? If not pervasive → Qualified. If pervasive → Adverse (misstatement) or Disclaimer (no evidence).
  5. Are there any additional communications needed? Going concern? EoM? KAM (listed only)? Other information inconsistency?

Materiality Benchmarks

Benchmark Range When primarily used
Profit before income taxes 5% – 10% Profit-oriented entities
Revenue / Turnover ½% – 1% When profit is low, nil, or negative
Total assets 1% – 2% Asset-heavy businesses, financial institutions
Total expenditure ½% – 1% Not-for-profit entities

Note: A single figure can be material by nature (e.g. directors' remuneration, related party transactions) even if below the size thresholds.

Common Exam Errors to Avoid

🚫 Do NOT Make These Mistakes
  • Confusing "no additional communication" with "EoM" — An immaterial matter needs no additional paragraph. EoM is only for fundamental matters.
  • Using EoM for going concern — Going concern has its own specific section ("Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern"), not EoM.
  • Modifying opinion for other information inconsistency — Chair's statement issues go in the Other Information section; the financial statement opinion is unaffected.
  • Thinking KAMs = modified opinion — KAMs appear in reports with unmodified opinions. They don't change the opinion.
  • Applying KAMs to non-listed entities — ISA 701 KAMs are required only for listed entities.
  • Saying directors assess 12 months from financial statement date — It's 12 months from the balance sheet date, not the date of issue.

Section B Answer Tips — Audit Report Questions

✅ Technique
  • For "Discuss the issue and impact on the auditor's report": (1) Calculate materiality, (2) State whether it is material/pervasive, (3) State the opinion type and why, (4) Name the new section in the report
  • For KAM parts: Part (i) = factors → always include "significant auditor attention", "significant judgement", "significant risk", "communicated to TCWG." Part (ii) = content → description, why KAM, how addressed, reference to FS disclosure
  • For going concern procedures: Focus on forward-looking evidence — cash flow forecasts, bank correspondence, board minutes, post-year-end management accounts. Always include written representations.
  • For going concern report impact: State the opinion AND the additional section clearly. Don't just say "modified" — specify qualified/adverse.
🎯
Final Exam Reminder: The auditor's report topic tests your ability to take a scenario, assess materiality and pervasiveness, and select the correct opinion and communications. Practice the decision framework until it is automatic. In the exam, always show your materiality calculation — even a quick "at 8% of profit this is material" earns marks.
