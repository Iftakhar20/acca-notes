Section E5(a) · ISA 700

Basic Elements of the Independent Auditor's Report

ISA 700 sets out the required elements that must appear in every auditor's report. These are commonly tested in MCQ format — you need to know what each element is and what purpose it serves.

1
Title
Must clearly state Independent Auditor's Report. The word "independent" distinguishes it from reports issued by others (e.g. internal audit). This signals to users the report can be relied upon.
2
Addressee
Identifies the intended users of the report — typically the shareholders of the company (those who appointed the auditor). It is not addressed to management or directors.
3
Auditor's Opinion
The conclusion of the entire audit — whether the financial statements give a true and fair view in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework. This is the most important element.
4
Basis for Opinion
Explains that the audit was conducted in accordance with International Standards on Auditing (ISAs). Confirms auditor independence and that the auditor believes sufficient appropriate evidence was obtained. Provides users confidence the report can be relied upon.
5
Going Concern Section (if applicable)
Required when there is a Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern. Describes the uncertainty and refers to the relevant disclosures in the financial statements. Included in an unmodified report when disclosure is adequate.
6
Key Audit Matters (listed companies only)
Under ISA 701, required for listed companies. Draws attention to matters that required significant auditor attention and were communicated to TCWG. Does not result in a modified opinion — these are just areas of focus, not problems.
7
Responsibilities of Management & TCWG
Describes management's responsibility for preparing the financial statements and for internal control. Confirms it is management, not the auditor, who prepares the financial statements.
8
Auditor's Responsibilities
Explains the auditor's objective: to obtain reasonable assurance (not absolute assurance) that the financial statements are free from material misstatement whether due to fraud or error. Describes key audit procedures performed.
9
Other Reporting Responsibilities / Other Information
Covers the auditor's responsibilities regarding other information (e.g. Chair's statement, directors' report) included alongside the financial statements. The auditor reads this for consistency but does not audit it.
10
Name of Engagement Partner
Identifies the individual responsible for the audit opinion, so that users can make queries. Required for listed company audits.
11
Auditor's Signature, Address and Date
The report is signed in the name of the audit firm. The date is the date the auditor signs the report — this is important as it defines the auditor's responsibilities for subsequent events.
🎯
Exam Trap — Q179 Style (Matching)

Know these pairings: Title → "clearly identifies the report as an IAR" · Addressee → "identifies the intended user" · Basis for opinion → "describes professional standards applied" · KAM → "draws attention to significant matters discussed with TCWG" · Engagement partner name → "identifies person responsible in case of queries". These come up as matching MCQs.

Important Wording to Memorise (ISA 700)

⚠️ Correct ISA 700 Wording — Q195 Style Trap

Three common errors the examiner tests:

  • Correct: "in accordance with International Standards on Auditing" — NOT "applicable to this audit"
  • Correct: "reasonable assurance" — NOT "maximum assurance"
  • Correct: "fraud or error" — NOT "deliberate manipulation or error"
Section E5(b) · ISA 700

Unmodified Opinion

An unmodified (clean) opinion is issued when the auditor concludes that the financial statements give a true and fair view in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework, and no material misstatements exist.

✅ Unmodified Opinion — Key Facts
  • Wording: "In our opinion, the financial statements give a true and fair view..."
  • This is the most common opinion in practice
  • Does NOT mean the financial statements are perfect — the auditor has only obtained reasonable assurance
  • An unmodified opinion can still have additional paragraphs (KAM, Emphasis of Matter, Going Concern section) added to it

When Can You Still Get an Unmodified Opinion?

A clean opinion can be issued even if issues exist — provided they are correctly treated and disclosed:

Situation Opinion Additional Communication
No issues found Unmodified None
Material uncertainty re going concern — adequately disclosed Unmodified Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern section
Contingent liability — correctly disclosed in notes Unmodified Emphasis of Matter paragraph (to highlight)
Listed company — significant audit areas Unmodified Key Audit Matters section
Inconsistency in Chair's statement vs financial statements Unmodified Other Information section (describes inconsistency)
💡
Key Distinction

Additional paragraphs (KAM, Emphasis of Matter, Going Concern section) do not modify the opinion. An unmodified opinion with a KAM section is still an unmodified opinion. These are communications, not modifications.

Section E5(c)(d) · ISA 705

Modified Opinions

A modified opinion is issued when something is wrong with the financial statements or the audit has been limited. ISA 705 governs this area. There are three types of modified opinion, and knowing which applies in which scenario is one of the most heavily tested areas in AA.

The Two Reasons for Modification

🚨 Reason 1 — Material Misstatement

The financial statements contain a misstatement that is material. The auditor has evidence that something is wrong.

  • Wrong accounting treatment
  • Missing provision
  • Incorrect disclosure
  • Going concern basis wrong
⚠️ Reason 2 — Inability to Obtain Evidence

The auditor cannot obtain sufficient appropriate evidence to form a conclusion. This is a limitation of scope.

  • Corrupted records
  • Management refuses access
  • Refusal to provide written representations
  • Auditor appointed after inventory count

🔑
Memory Framework — 2×2 Grid

Think of a 2×2 grid: Rows = Reason (Misstatement vs Can't get evidence). Columns = Severity (Material not pervasive vs Material and pervasive). This gives you 4 cells → Qualified / Adverse / Qualified / Disclaimer. Adverse is only ever for confirmed misstatements. Disclaimer is only ever when you CAN'T form an opinion at all.

What Pervasive Means

Pervasive means the effect is so widespread that the financial statements as a whole are unreliable. A misstatement or limitation is pervasive if it:

  • Is not confined to specific elements — it affects the FS as a whole, OR
  • If confined to specific elements, those elements represent or could represent a substantial proportion of the FS, OR
  • Relates to disclosures fundamental to users' understanding of the FS
📋 Practical Guide to Materiality & Pervasiveness
  • Not material (<5% of profit, <1% of revenue, <1% of assets) → No modification needed but ISA 450 requires auditor to ask management to correct it
  • Material (5–10% of profit or 1–2% of assets), limited to one area → likely Qualified
  • Highly material (>10% of profit) or affecting core FS elements → likely Adverse / Disclaimer
  • These are guidelines — always read the exam scenario for clues about pervasiveness

Impact on the Report When Modified

When the opinion is modified, the structure of the report changes:

Opinion Type Section Name Changes To What It Contains
Qualified "Basis for Qualified Opinion" Explains the specific matter causing the qualification
Adverse "Basis for Adverse Opinion" Explains why the FS are materially and pervasively misstated
Disclaimer "Basis for Disclaimer of Opinion" Explains the limitation on scope that prevented forming an opinion
🚨 Common Exam Errors to Avoid
  • Do NOT say "Adverse" when the issue is inability to obtain evidence — that is a Disclaimer situation
  • Do NOT say "Qualified" when the misstatement is pervasive — that requires Adverse
  • An Adverse opinion still means the FS are presented — the auditor just says they don't give a TFV
  • A Disclaimer means the auditor gives NO opinion at all
Section E5(e) · ISA 701 · ISA 706

Key Audit Matters, Emphasis of Matter & Other Matter Paragraphs

Beyond the opinion itself, the auditor's report can include additional paragraphs to communicate important information to users. These do not modify the opinion but add context and transparency.

1. Key Audit Matters (KAM) — ISA 701

📌 What Are Key Audit Matters?

KAMs are matters that, in the auditor's professional judgement, were of most significance in the audit of the current period's financial statements. They are selected from matters communicated to TCWG.

When Is a KAM Section Required?

Only for listed companies — required by ISA 701. Not required for non-listed (private) companies, though auditors may include them voluntarily.

How Are KAMs Selected?

The auditor selects KAMs from matters communicated to TCWG, choosing those that required significant auditor attention due to:

  • Areas of higher assessed risk of material misstatement
  • Significant auditor judgement relating to management's judgements
  • Effect on the audit of significant events or transactions during the period

What Does a KAM Section Contain?

  • A description of why the matter was considered a KAM
  • How the auditor responded to the matter
  • A reference to relevant disclosures in the FS (if any)
🎯
Exam Trap — Q159 Style

KAMs are matters that "required significant auditor attention" — NOT all matters communicated to TCWG, NOT all material matters, NOT matters that resulted in a modification. That answer (Option 1 in Q159) is key.

2. Emphasis of Matter Paragraph — ISA 706

📋 Emphasis of Matter — Definition and Use

A paragraph added to draw users' attention to a matter that is already disclosed in the financial statements and which the auditor considers is fundamental to users' understanding. The FS treat the matter correctly — the auditor simply wants to highlight it.

When Is an Emphasis of Matter Paragraph Used?

  • A material uncertainty related to a contingent liability that has been properly disclosed
  • A significant subsequent event that has been properly reflected in the FS
  • Early application of a new accounting standard that has a significant effect
  • Any matter that is fundamental to user understanding and adequately disclosed
⚠️ Critical Rule — Emphasis of Matter
  • The matter must already be disclosed in the FS — the EOM does not replace disclosure
  • The opinion is still unmodified — EOM is not a modification
  • Placed after the Basis for Opinion paragraph
  • EOM paragraph title: "Emphasis of Matter"

3. Other Matter Paragraph — ISA 706

📋 Other Matter — Definition and Use

A paragraph that refers to a matter not presented or disclosed in the financial statements that the auditor considers relevant to users' understanding of the audit, the auditor's responsibilities, or the auditor's report.

When Is an Other Matter Paragraph Used?

  • To explain auditor's responsibilities further (e.g. restriction on liability)
  • When reporting under local regulations in addition to ISAs
  • To communicate something relevant to users that is not about the FS themselves

Comparing the Three Paragraphs

Type Matter Relates To Disclosed in FS? Modifies Opinion? Listed Only?
Key Audit Matters Most significant audit areas Usually yes No Yes
Emphasis of Matter Fundamental matters in the FS Yes — must be No No
Other Matter Auditor's responsibilities / other info No — not in FS No No

The Other Information Section

Separate from the above — the auditor's report must include an Other Information section that describes what the auditor does with other information (e.g. the Chair's statement, directors' report) issued alongside the FS. Key rules:

  • The auditor reads other information for consistency — does not audit it
  • No assurance conclusion is expressed on other information
  • If inconsistency is found between other information and the FS, it is described in the Other Information section — not an EOM or Other Matter paragraph
  • The auditor's opinion on the FS is not modified due to an inconsistency in other information
💡
Scenario Trick — Chair's Statement

Chair's statement contradicts the FS → Other Information section of the report describes the inconsistency. Opinion stays unmodified. This is NOT an Emphasis of Matter and NOT an Other Matter paragraph. Q163 in the Exam Kit tests exactly this.

ISA 260 · Syllabus Area C4

Communication with Those Charged with Governance (TCWG)

Those Charged with Governance (TCWG) are those responsible for overseeing the strategic direction of the entity and its accountability obligations — typically the board of directors and/or the audit committee. Communication with TCWG is governed by ISA 260.

Why Does the Auditor Communicate with TCWG?

📌 Purpose of Communication (ISA 260)
  • To assist TCWG in fulfilling their oversight responsibilities
  • To give the auditor information relevant to the audit
  • To promote effective two-way communication throughout the audit
  • To inform TCWG of significant audit findings and any issues encountered

What Must the Auditor Communicate to TCWG?

ISA 260 requires the auditor to communicate a wide range of matters:

1

Auditor's Responsibilities

The overall audit strategy and planned scope — communicating the nature, timing and extent of planned audit work.

2

Significant Findings from the Audit

Qualitative aspects of accounting practices including accounting policies and estimates; disagreements with management; significant difficulties encountered; uncorrected misstatements; any expected modifications to the auditor's report.

3

Auditor Independence

Where ISAs require it, the auditor must communicate about independence — particularly any threats to independence and the safeguards applied.

4

Significant Deficiencies in Internal Control

Under ISA 265, any significant deficiencies in internal control identified during the audit must be communicated to TCWG in writing.

5

Other Required Communications

Fraud (ISA 240), non-compliance with laws and regulations (ISA 250), going concern issues (ISA 570), and any expected modifications to the auditor's report.

Form and Timing of Communication

📅 Timing
  • Must be timely — enables TCWG to take action
  • May be communicated during or after the audit
  • Significant matters communicated as they are identified — don't wait until the end
📝 Form
  • Can be oral or written
  • Significant matters should be in writing
  • Where communicated orally, it should be documented in the audit file

Link to Key Audit Matters (ISA 701)

Key Audit Matters in the auditor's report are selected from matters communicated to TCWG. The chain is:

  • Auditor identifies significant audit areas during the audit
  • These are communicated to TCWG under ISA 260
  • From those communicated matters, the auditor selects those that required most significant attention
  • Those are reported publicly as KAMs in the auditor's report (listed companies)
🔗
Connection to the Wider Syllabus

TCWG communication also links to the internal control chapter (C4) — significant deficiencies in internal controls must be reported to TCWG in writing (ISA 265). The management letter / report to management covers control deficiencies found during the audit. TCWG communication is therefore a theme across the whole audit, not just at the reporting stage.

Who Are TCWG in Practice?

Entity Type TCWG typically are...
Listed company Audit committee (preferred) — or full board if no audit committee
Private company with governance structure Board of directors
Small/owner-managed business The owner-manager themselves (though management = TCWG overlap)
