All of this topic is governed by ISA 580 — Written Representations. Know this ISA number — it may be tested directly.

Section E3(a)

What Is a Written Representation?

A written statement by management provided to the auditor to confirm certain matters or to support other audit evidence.

— ISA 580, Definition

In plain terms: it is a letter from management to the auditor confirming important facts about the financial statements. It is also called a Management Representation Letter or Letter of Representation.

✅ What it IS
  • A formal written letter
  • Signed by management (directors)
  • Addressed to the auditor
  • Dated on or near the audit report date
  • A piece of audit evidence
❌ What it is NOT
  • A substitute for other audit evidence
  • Highly reliable on its own
  • Optional where ISA requires it
  • A guarantee of accuracy
💡
Quick Memory

Think of it as the directors "signing off" in writing on key facts they told the auditor. It's management acknowledging responsibility — not the auditor verifying facts.

Section E3(a) — Purpose

Why Do Auditors Obtain Written Representations?

ISA 580 sets out three key purposes:

1

Confirm Management Responsibilities

Management must acknowledge they are responsible for the preparation of the financial statements in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework, and that they believe the financial statements are fairly presented.

2

Support Other Audit Evidence

For certain areas — particularly those involving management's judgement, intentions, or knowledge — written representations are the only practical source of evidence. They corroborate what management said verbally during the audit.

3

Reduce the Risk of Misunderstanding

By asking management to put things in writing, the auditor reduces the risk that management later claims they never confirmed a particular fact or intention.

⚠️ Important Limitation

Written representations do not replace sufficient appropriate audit evidence. If evidence exists elsewhere, the auditor must gather it — they cannot simply rely on a representation.

Section E3(a) — Procedure

How Are Written Representations Obtained?

1

Identify Required Representations

Throughout the audit, the auditor identifies areas where a written representation is required (mandatory under ISA) or where it is appropriate (to support audit evidence).

2

Draft the Letter

The auditor typically drafts the representation letter for management to review and sign. This ensures all required matters are included and clearly worded.

3

Who Signs It?

The letter is signed by those with appropriate responsibilities — usually the directors (those charged with governance and management). In practice this is often the CEO and/or Finance Director.

4

Timing — Date of the Letter

The written representation letter is dated as near as practicable to, but not after, the date of the auditor's report. It should cover the period up to the date the auditor signs the report.

5

File in Audit Documentation

The signed letter is retained as part of the audit file — it is formal audit evidence.

📅 Timing — Exam Trick

The letter is dated on or just before the auditor's report date — not the period end date, and not after the report is signed. This is a common exam trap.

Section E3(c) — Content

What Does the Letter Contain?

ISA 580 distinguishes between mandatory representations (required in every audit) and subject-specific representations (required when certain ISAs apply or when specific circumstances exist).

🔴 Mandatory Representations — Required in EVERY Audit

ISA 580 requires two representations to be obtained in all audits:

Must Always Include
  1. Management's responsibility for the financial statements — that they have fulfilled their responsibility to prepare the financial statements in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework.
  2. Completeness of information provided — that they have provided the auditor with all relevant information and access to records, and have disclosed all matters relevant to the financial statements and the audit.

🟡 Subject-Specific Mandatory Representations

Other ISAs also require specific written representations. These come up when the relevant ISA applies:

ISA Topic What Is Confirmed
ISA 240 Fraud & Error Management has disclosed all known or suspected fraud; they have designed controls to prevent/detect fraud
ISA 250 Laws & Regulations Management has disclosed all known actual/possible non-compliance with laws and regulations
ISA 450 Misstatements Management believes the effect of uncorrected misstatements is immaterial (individually and in aggregate)
ISA 501 Inventory/Litigation All litigation and claims have been disclosed; inventory quantities/values are complete and accurate
ISA 540 Accounting Estimates Assumptions used for estimates are reasonable; estimates are properly measured and disclosed
ISA 550 Related Parties All related party relationships/transactions have been disclosed; related party transactions are conducted on arm's length terms (if claimed)
ISA 560 Subsequent Events All events occurring after the reporting date have been disclosed to the auditor
ISA 570 Going Concern Management has disclosed all events/conditions relevant to the going concern assessment

🟢 Additional Representations (As Appropriate)

Beyond the mandatory items, representations may be obtained on any matter where the auditor considers it necessary, particularly involving:

  • Management's plans or intentions that affect asset carrying values (e.g. intention to hold to maturity, intention to sell)
  • Management's knowledge of facts that cannot be independently verified (e.g. title to assets, absence of undisclosed side agreements)
  • Completeness of specific classes of transactions or disclosures
🎯
Exam Trap — Q189 Style

Analytical procedures do NOT require a written representation. Fraud & error ✓, Laws & regulations ✓, Subsequent events ✓ — but not analytical procedures. This is a classic MCQ trap in the Exam Kit.

Section E3(b)

Quality & Reliability of Written Representations

This is a critical exam area. Written representations are not highly reliable as audit evidence — you must understand why, and how auditors respond.

Why Reliability Is LIMITED

  • Internal source: They come from management, who prepared the financial statements being audited — there is a self-serving bias risk.
  • Not independently verifiable: The auditor cannot always check whether what management said is true.
  • Written ≠ accurate: The fact that management put something in writing does not make it correct.
  • Potential for management bias: Management may have incentives (bonuses, debt covenants, share price) to misrepresent.
📊 Evidence Hierarchy Context

Written representations sit towards the lower end of the audit evidence reliability hierarchy. External confirmations, physical inspection, and third-party documents are all more reliable. Representations from management are internal & oral (turned written) — less persuasive.

How Auditors Address Limited Reliability

  • Treat representations as corroborative evidence, not standalone evidence.
  • Where other evidence is available, obtain it — do not substitute with a representation.
  • Consider whether representations are consistent with other evidence gathered.
  • If a representation conflicts with other evidence, investigate further.

Impact on Reliability of Other Evidence

⚠️ Management Integrity Knock-On

If management's written representations appear unreliable or inconsistent with other evidence, the auditor should be concerned about management's integrity. This calls into question the reliability of all evidence provided by management — not just the representation itself. The auditor must reconsider the audit risk assessment and the sufficiency of evidence obtained throughout the entire audit.

Section E3(c)

Circumstances Where Representations Are Necessary

Written representations are particularly important in the following circumstances:

1

Management Judgement

Where amounts depend on management's estimates, assumptions, or judgements (e.g. provisions, impairment, fair values).

2

Management Intentions

Where the accounting treatment depends on what management intends to do (e.g. hold-to-maturity investment, plans to restructure).

3

Knowledge of Management

Information known only to management (e.g. undisclosed side agreements, knowledge of fraud, related party dealings).

4

No Other Evidence Available

Where sufficient appropriate evidence cannot be obtained from other sources — the representation is the only practical option.

Common Matters Covered in Practice

  • Confirmation that all transactions are recorded and reflected in the financial statements
  • Completeness of disclosures of related party transactions
  • No undisclosed side agreements or commitments
  • Warranty provision amount is management's best estimate
  • Going concern — no events/conditions beyond those disclosed
  • Subsequent events — no material events after year-end beyond those disclosed
  • Inventory is stated at the lower of cost and NRV
  • All litigation and contingent liabilities have been disclosed
  • Title to assets is held by the company
📝
Exam Scenario Technique

In a scenario question, if you see management judgement, management intention, or something only management knows — these signal that a written representation would be appropriate. Warranty provisions and impairment write-downs are classic examples.

Section E3 — Reporting Implications

What If Management Refuses to Sign?

This is heavily tested. If management refuses to provide written representations, the consequences are serious:

🚨 ISA 580 — Effect of Refusal

A refusal to provide written representations is a limitation on the scope of the audit. The auditor cannot obtain sufficient appropriate evidence. This means:

Situation Audit Opinion Reason
Management refuses to provide the two mandatory representations required by ISA 580 Disclaimer of Opinion The auditor cannot express an opinion at all — the limitation is fundamental. There is insufficient appropriate evidence to form any opinion.
Management refuses to provide a specific/other representation Qualified Opinion (inability to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence) The limitation may be material but not pervasive, so a "except for" qualification is appropriate.
⚠️ Management Integrity Concern

Refusal to sign also raises serious doubts about management's integrity. If management won't confirm basic facts in writing, the auditor must question whether management can be trusted at all — potentially affecting the entire audit approach and risk assessment.

What the Opinion is NOT

❌
Adverse opinion — this is NOT correct. An adverse opinion means the financial statements are materially misstated. Refusal is a limitation of scope, not a confirmed misstatement.
❌
Unmodified opinion — clearly wrong. The auditor cannot simply ignore the refusal.
❌
Emphasis of Matter paragraph — this is added to an unmodified opinion and does not apply here.
🔑
Memory Key

Refusal = Limitation of Scope. Mandatory reps refused = Disclaimer. Specific rep refused = Qualified (unable to obtain evidence). Never Adverse, never Unmodified.
