1. What Are Substantive Procedures?

Core Definition
Substantive procedures are audit procedures designed to detect material misstatements at the assertion level in financial statement balances, transactions, and disclosures. They provide direct evidence that the numbers in the financial statements are correct (or not).

Think of it this way: once you understand the company and its controls, substantive procedures are how you actually prove the numbers are right. They answer the question: "Is this figure in the accounts free from material error?"

The word "substantive" literally means dealing with the substance (the facts). You are testing whether amounts, transactions and disclosures are materially correct – not whether controls are working.

Why They Always Exist
No matter how strong a company's controls are, ISA 330 requires auditors to perform some substantive procedures on every material balance and class of transactions. You can never audit purely through tests of controls – you must always get some direct evidence about the numbers themselves.

2. Tests of Controls vs Substantive Procedures

This distinction comes up frequently in Section A (objective test) questions. Know it cold.

Feature Tests of Controls Substantive Procedures
Purpose Evaluate whether a control is operating effectively Detect material misstatements in the financial statements
What they test The process / system The number / transaction itself
Example Inspect bank transfer list for finance director's signature confirming approval Recalculate the total of the bank transfer list
When performed When auditor plans to rely on controls (interim stage) Always – required on all material items
Effect of strong controls Auditor performs tests of controls Allows reduced substantive testing (smaller samples, analytical procedures instead of detailed tests)
Common Exam Trap
Reviewing board minutes for approval of useful lives = Test of control. Comparing depreciation charge to prior year using a "proof in total" = Substantive analytical procedure. The exam loves mixing these up. Ask yourself: "Am I checking a process worked, or checking the number is right?"
Quick Test – Section A Style

Which of the following is a SUBSTANTIVE procedure for payroll?

A. Inspect payslips for evidence of line manager authorisation
B. Review procedures to ensure payroll files are kept secure
C. For a sample of employees, agree salary details in standing data to the payslip calculation
D. Review exception reports produced by the payroll system

Show answer & explanation

C is correct.

  • C tests whether the salary amount in the financial statements is correct — that is a substantive test of detail.
  • A tests whether an authorisation control operated — test of control.
  • B tests whether a physical/logical security control is in place — test of control.
  • D tests whether the exception reporting control is being used — test of control.

3. The Two Types of Substantive Procedures

Type 1: Tests of Detail
Direct examination of individual transactions, balances or disclosures. Examples: agreeing a receivable balance to an invoice, confirming a bank balance with the bank, physically counting inventory, recalculating depreciation.

Best for: existence, rights, accuracy of specific items.
Type 2: Substantive Analytical Procedures
Using relationships between data to identify misstatements. Examples: comparing revenue to prior year, using a "proof in total" for depreciation, comparing gross margin % to industry or prior year.

Best for: completeness, overall reasonableness, plausibility.
Exam Keyword Alert
When a question asks for "SUBSTANTIVE ANALYTICAL procedures" (capitals in Kaplan questions), you must only write ratio/comparison-based procedures — not tests of detail. If it asks for "substantive procedures" generally, you can use both types.

Key Analytical Procedure Techniques

TechniqueWhat it involvesBest assertion
Proof in totalCalculate expected figure independently and compare to recorded figure. E.g. average salary × headcount = expected payroll cost.Accuracy / Completeness
Prior year comparisonCompare current year balance/ratio to prior year. Investigate significant unexplained movements.Completeness / Occurrence
Trend analysisCompare monthly or quarterly data to identify unusual spikes or drops.Occurrence / Completeness

Reasonableness testUse non-financial data to test a financial figure. E.g. number of hotel rooms × average occupancy × average rate = expected revenue.Completeness / Accuracy

4. Financial Statement Assertions – The Core Framework

Every substantive procedure you design should be linked to a specific assertion. Assertions are the claims management makes about items in the financial statements. The examiner loves asking you to match procedures to assertions.

Assertions for Classes of Transactions & Events (Income Statement / P&L items)

Occurrence
Transactions actually happened and relate to the entity. (Anti-fraud assertion — no fictional revenue.)
Completeness
All transactions that should have been recorded have been. (No missing purchases / expenses.)
Accuracy
Transactions have been recorded at the correct amounts.
Cut-off
Transactions are recorded in the correct accounting period.
Classification
Transactions are recorded in the correct account (e.g., capex not expensed as repairs).

Assertions for Account Balances (Balance Sheet items)

Existence
Assets and liabilities actually exist at the period end. (Prevents phantom assets.)
Completeness
All assets and liabilities that should be recorded have been. (Prevents understated liabilities.)
Rights & Obligations
Entity owns or controls assets; liabilities are the entity's obligations.
Accuracy, Valuation & Allocation
Balances are recorded at appropriate amounts, including adjustments for impairment, depreciation, NRV, etc.
Golden Rules for the Exam
  • Existence = test from records → physical world (e.g., trace invoice to goods receipt)
  • Completeness = test from physical world → records (e.g., trace goods received note to invoice recorded)
  • Completeness and occurrence/existence point in opposite directions
  • Occurrence = relevant to transactions (did this sale happen?)
  • Existence = relevant to balances (does this asset actually exist?)

5. Receivables (Trade Debtors)

AR

Trade Receivables & Revenue – Key Audit Concerns

Receivables carry the risk of overstatement (management wants to show high debtors / high revenue). Main assertions are existence, valuation, rights, and completeness of revenue.

5a. Receivables Circularisation (Direct Confirmation)

A circularisation sends letters directly to customers asking them to confirm their outstanding balance. This is the most reliable evidence for existence (it comes from an independent third party).

FeaturePositive CircularisationNegative Circularisation
RequestPlease confirm your balance or tell us if it differsOnly reply if the balance is wrong
Evidence strengthStronger – response required regardlessWeaker – silence assumed to mean agreement
When usedHigher risk situations, larger balancesLarge number of small, low-risk balances
Non-responsesMust follow up with alternative proceduresNo follow-up required (but risky)

Steps in a Positive Circularisation (Exam Favourite)

  • 1Obtain the year-end receivables ledger and agree the total to the trial balance / financial statements.
  • 2Select a sample of balances to circularise (usually including large and overdue balances).
  • 3Prepare confirmation letters on the audit firm's letterhead, stating the balance per the client's records and asking the customer to confirm or disagree.
  • 4Ensure the letters are sent directly by the audit team (not through the client) and that replies come back directly to the auditor.
  • 5For non-responses, send a second letter and, if still no response, perform alternative procedures (see below).
  • 6For differences (disputes), investigate to determine whether there is a misstatement in the financial statements.
  • 7Conclude on the overall evidence obtained from the circularisation.

5b. Alternative Procedures (When No Circularisation)

Scenario Alert
The client may refuse a circularisation (customers complained, low response rate historically). The examiner will ask you for "substantive procedures other than a receivables circularisation." Learn these well!
AssertionProcedure (without circularisation)
Existence
  • Perform a receivables circularisation on a sample (if possible)
  • Review post-year-end cash receipts from customers – if cash received after year end, the balance existed
  • Agree balances to sales invoices, delivery notes and orders
Valuation (recoverability)
  • Review post-year-end cash receipts – largest test of recoverability
  • Review aged receivables listing and identify balances overdue
  • Inspect correspondence with customers for evidence of disputes or financial difficulty
  • Review industry news / press for customers experiencing financial problems (e.g. Albatross Co)
  • Recalculate the allowance for credit losses and assess reasonableness
  • Compare allowance % to prior year and investigate significant changes
Completeness
  • Agree the receivables ledger total to the control account and trial balance
  • Review credit notes issued after year end (may indicate unrecorded returns reducing receivables)
  • Test sales cut-off around year end
Rights & Obligations
  • Review sales agreements for any factoring or assignment of receivables (entity may no longer have rights)
  • Confirm no receivables have been pledged as security

5c. Revenue – Completeness and Occurrence

  • 1Occurrence: Select a sample of recorded revenue transactions. Agree each to despatch records, delivery notes and customer orders. Confirm the sale relates to the entity.
  • 2Completeness: Test cut-off – select a sample of goods despatched notes around the year end and confirm revenue is recognised in the correct period.
  • 3Analytical: Compare revenue by month / product line to prior year. Investigate unexplained variances exceeding a set threshold. Calculate gross margin % and compare to prior year and budget.
  • 4Reasonableness: If relevant, calculate expected revenue using non-financial data (e.g. hotel rooms × occupancy rate × room rate).
Exam Question – Section B Style (Kaplan: Hawthorn Enterprises)

Required: Describe substantive procedures to verify EXISTENCE and VALUATION of receivables (no circularisation allowed).

Show model answer

Existence:

  • Obtain the aged receivables listing and agree the total to the trial balance to confirm completeness of the ledger.
  • For a sample of balances, agree to the supporting sales invoices and delivery notes to confirm the sale occurred and relates to the entity.
  • Review post-year-end cash receipts – if the customer has paid after the year end, the balance existed at the year end.

Valuation (recoverability):

  • Review the aged receivables listing for balances significantly overdue and discuss recoverability with management.
  • Inspect correspondence files for evidence of disputes, customer complaints or intimation of inability to pay.
  • Review post-year-end cash receipts to confirm amounts have been received.
  • Recalculate the allowance for credit losses and assess whether it is sufficient given the aged profile.
  • Compare the allowance as a % of gross receivables to prior year and investigate significant increases or decreases.

Note: The receivables balance increased from $1.4m to $2.3m — this is a specific risk factor the examiner flagged. Your answer should address this by noting you'd focus on the new/increased balances.

6. Inventory

INV

Inventory – Key Audit Concerns

Inventory carries risks of both overstatement (inflated to boost profits) and understatement. Key assertions: existence, completeness, valuation (lower of cost and NRV).

6a. Attendance at Inventory Count (Critical Procedure)

The auditor's attendance at the year-end count is the primary way to gain evidence over existence and completeness. ISA 501 deals with this.

Before the Count

  • 1Obtain and review the client's count instructions to assess adequacy.
  • 2Confirm the count includes all inventory locations, including third-party warehouses.
  • 3Assess whether the client's procedures address: slow-moving items, damaged goods, items held for third parties.

During the Count

  • 1Observe the client's count procedures to assess whether they are being followed.
  • 2Perform test counts in both directions: records → floor (existence) and floor → records (completeness).
  • 3Note the last goods received number and last despatch note number for cut-off testing.
  • 4Identify any damaged, obsolete or slow-moving items and note details for valuation purposes.
  • 5Confirm that items held for third parties are segregated and excluded from the count.

6b. Cut-Off Testing

  • 1Record the last GRN and despatch note numbers at the count.
  • 2Confirm that goods received before year end are included in inventory and the corresponding purchase is accrued.
  • 3Confirm that goods despatched before year end are excluded from inventory and revenue has been recognised.

6c. Valuation (Lower of Cost and NRV)

IAS 2 Principle
Inventory must be valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value (NRV). NRV = estimated selling price less estimated costs of completion and costs to sell. If NRV is below cost, a write-down is required.
  • 1Obtain the inventory valuation report and agree totals to the trial balance.
  • 2For a sample of inventory lines, agree the cost figure to purchase invoices or standard costing records.
  • 3Review the aged inventory report — items with no movement for 6+ months may be slow-moving or obsolete.
  • 4For potentially obsolete items (e.g. Remy Gusteau products in Kaplan's Cookit Co question), obtain post-year-end sales prices and compare to cost — if NRV is below cost, a write-down is needed.
  • 5Review post-year-end sales of inventory items to obtain evidence of NRV.
  • 6Compare overall inventory days ratio to prior year; a significant increase may suggest obsolete inventory.
  • 7For damaged goods noted at count, obtain evidence of their NRV (scrap values, repairs cost).

6d. Inventory Held by Third Parties

  • 1Obtain direct written confirmation from the third party of the quantity and condition of inventory held on behalf of the client.
  • 2Review the contractual agreement to confirm the entity's rights to the inventory.
  • 3If practicable, attend the third-party count.
Practice Question (Kaplan: Cookit Co – Inventory Valuation)

Cookit Co includes Remy Gusteau-branded products at cost $1.7m in total inventory of $4.25m. Demand has collapsed following cancellation of the TV show. Describe substantive procedures for the VALUATION of Cookit Co's inventory.

Show model answer
  • Obtain the inventory valuation report from Cookit Co and agree the total to the trial balance.
  • Agree the cost of Remy Gusteau products ($1.7m) to the purchase invoices or standard costing records to confirm the cost figure is accurate.
  • Obtain post-year-end selling prices for Remy Gusteau products by reviewing sales after the year end. Compare selling price to cost — if NRV is below $1.7m, a write-down is required.
  • Review the aged inventory report to identify how long Remy Gusteau products have been held; if there have been no sales since March 20X5, the NRV may be negligible.
  • Enquire of management about any planned promotional activities, price reductions or disposal routes for the Remy Gusteau stock.
  • Calculate the expected write-down and assess whether management's provision (if any) is reasonable.
  • Review board minutes for any discussion of the Remy Gusteau stock write-down policy.
  • For the remainder of inventory, agree a sample of cost figures to purchase invoices and compare NRV to post-year-end selling prices to confirm lower of cost and NRV.

7. Payables & Accruals

PAY

Trade Payables – Key Audit Concern: Completeness (Understatement)

The main risk for payables is understatement — management may want to show lower liabilities. Always think "completeness" first for payables.

7a. Supplier Statement Reconciliations

  • 1Obtain supplier statements for a sample of major suppliers and reconcile each statement to the balance on the payables ledger.
  • 2Investigate differences — these may indicate timing differences (invoices in transit) or unrecorded liabilities.
  • 3For suppliers where no statement is available, request one from the supplier directly.
  • 4Agree the total payables ledger to the control account and to the financial statements.

7b. Completeness of Payables

  • 1Review invoices received after the year end (unrecorded liabilities search) — identify invoices dated before the year end that have not been recorded and assess whether an accrual is needed.
  • 2Review GRN records for goods received before year end with no matching invoice — confirm an accrual has been raised (GRNI).
  • 3Compare the payables payment period (payables days) to prior year — a significant decrease (e.g. 72 days to 53 days in Cookit Co) may indicate understatement.
  • 4Compare the payables balance as a % of cost of sales to prior year.
  • 5Review board minutes and management accounts for references to significant creditors not reflected in the accounts.

7c. Payables Circularisation (Less Common)

Direct confirmation with suppliers is used less often than receivables circularisation because supplier statements are usually available. However, if statements are unavailable, the auditor may send confirmation requests.

Exam Question – Completeness (Kaplan: Cookit Co – Trade Payables)

Payables days have fallen from 72 to 53 days; no supplier statement reconciliations since December 20X4. Describe substantive procedures for COMPLETENESS of payables.

Show model answer
  • Obtain supplier statements from major suppliers and reconcile to balances on the payables ledger. Investigate any differences as these may represent unrecorded liabilities.
  • Perform an unrecorded liabilities search: review invoices received in the weeks after year end and determine whether any relate to goods received before the year end — if so, an accrual should have been raised.
  • Review GRN records for the period just before year end to identify goods received without a corresponding purchase invoice — confirm that an accrual (GRNI) has been raised for each.
  • Calculate the payables payment period for the current year (53 days) and compare to the prior year (72 days). Investigate the reasons for the significant decrease — this may indicate omitted liabilities.
  • Compare total payables as a percentage of cost of sales to the prior year figure and investigate significant decreases.
  • Review minutes of board meetings for references to significant suppliers or liabilities not reflected in the accounts.

8. Bank & Cash

BNK

Bank – Existence, Valuation, Rights, Completeness

8a. Bank Confirmation Report (Bank Letter)

  • 1Obtain written confirmation directly from the bank of all balances held (current accounts, savings accounts, overdrafts, loans) as at the year end.
  • 2Agree confirmed balances to the bank reconciliation and the trial balance.
  • 3Confirm whether any assets have been pledged as security for borrowings.
  • 4Request confirmation of any contingent liabilities, guarantees or off-balance sheet arrangements.

8b. Auditing the Bank Reconciliation

What a Bank Reconciliation Does
It reconciles the balance per the bank statement to the balance per the cashbook (general ledger). Main differences: timing (unpresented cheques, outstanding lodgements). Any other items may indicate fraud or error.
  • 1Obtain the bank reconciliation prepared by management and check its arithmetic.
  • 2Agree the balance per the bank statement to the bank confirmation letter (direct external evidence).
  • 3Agree the balance per the cashbook to the trial balance and financial statements.
  • 4Agree outstanding lodgements (deposits in transit) to the subsequent bank statement — confirm they have cleared promptly after the year end.
  • 5Agree unpresented cheques to the subsequent bank statement — confirm they have cleared after the year end. Investigate any long-outstanding unpresented cheques (may need to be reversed).
  • 6Review for any unusual reconciling items — if post-year-end cheques are included, this may indicate cut-off manipulation (as in the Hawthorn Enterprises scenario where cheques were processed early to reduce payables).
  • 7Confirm there are no large reconciling items not explained by timing differences.
Section B Style – Bank Reconciliation (Kaplan: Hawthorn Enterprises)

Last year, significant cut-off errors were found (post-year-end cheques processed early). Describe substantive procedures for the bank reconciliation.

Show model answer
  • Obtain the year-end bank reconciliation and verify its arithmetical accuracy.
  • Agree the bank balance per the statement to the bank confirmation letter obtained directly from the bank.
  • Agree the cashbook balance to the trial balance / financial statements.
  • Trace all outstanding lodgements (deposits in transit) to the post-year-end bank statement to confirm they cleared promptly — lodgements not clearing within a few days may be fictitious.
  • Trace all unpresented cheques to the post-year-end bank statement to confirm clearance. Investigate any cheques that remain unpresented for an unreasonable period (may need to be reversed).
  • Given the prior year issue, specifically inspect the bank statement in the days surrounding the year end — identify any cheques dated after the year end that were presented to the bank before the year end (window dressing).
  • Review correspondence with the bank for any additional accounts, overdrafts or contingent liabilities not included in the reconciliation.

9. Non-Current Assets (Tangible & Intangible)

NCA

Property, Plant & Equipment / Intangibles – Existence, Valuation, Completeness

9a. Additions to Property, Plant & Equipment

  • 1Obtain the non-current asset register (NCAR) and agree total additions to the trial balance.
  • 2For a sample of additions, agree the cost to purchase invoices, contracts and bank payments.
  • 3Physically inspect major new assets to confirm existence.
  • 4Capitalisation: Review the nature of costs capitalised — confirm only costs directly attributable to bringing the asset to its intended use are included. Training costs, refundable taxes, and administration costs should be expensed (common exam issue — Heron Co question).
  • 5Confirm assets are recognised from the date they are available for use (depreciation starts correctly).

9b. Depreciation

  • 1Review management's depreciation policy and assess reasonableness against useful lives.
  • 2Perform a "proof in total" — recalculate expected depreciation (cost × rate) and compare to the recorded charge. Investigate differences exceeding a materiality threshold.
  • 3Compare depreciation as a % of cost to prior year and investigate significant changes.
  • 4Confirm useful lives assigned to new assets are reasonable based on industry norms or management assessment.

9c. Disposals

  • 1Obtain the disposal schedule and agree proceeds to bank receipts or legal documentation.
  • 2Agree cost and accumulated depreciation of disposed assets to the NCAR.
  • 3Recalculate the profit or loss on disposal and agree to the income statement.
  • 4Confirm the asset has been removed from the NCAR.

9d. Revaluations

When assets are revalued (as in the Elounda Co question – a related valuer), the auditor must:

  • 1Assess the competence, capability and objectivity of the valuer used. If the valuer is related to management (e.g. the finance director's sibling), objectivity is called into question.
  • 2Agree the revalued amount to the valuation report.
  • 3Review the methodology used by the valuer and assess reasonableness.
  • 4Confirm the revaluation surplus has been correctly credited to the revaluation reserve (OCI) and any deferred tax liability recognised.
  • 5Confirm the date of the valuation is appropriate (close to year end).

9e. Intangible Assets

Common scenario: research & development expenditure (IAS 38). Only development costs meeting strict criteria can be capitalised; research costs must be expensed.

  • 1Review the classification of expenditure between research and development and assess whether the entity's categorisation is appropriate.
  • 2For development costs capitalised, confirm all six IAS 38 capitalisation criteria are met (technical feasibility, intention, ability, adequate resources, probable future economic benefits, ability to reliably measure).
  • 3Agree amounts to invoices and payroll records.
  • 4Assess amortisation policy and recalculate the amortisation charge.

10. Non-Current Liabilities, Provisions & Contingencies

PROV

Provisions & Contingencies – Valuation is the Key Risk

IAS 37 Reminder
A provision is recognised when: (1) a present obligation exists from a past event, (2) it is probable the obligation will require an outflow, and (3) a reliable estimate can be made. A contingent liability is disclosed only (not recognised) when the outflow is possible but not probable.

10a. Provisions (e.g. Legal Claims, Redundancy)

  • 1Obtain a schedule of all provisions and agree totals to the trial balance.
  • 2Review correspondence with the company's legal advisors — obtain a letter from lawyers confirming the status of claims, the likelihood of an outflow, and a best estimate of the amount.
  • 3Assess whether a present obligation exists at the year end by reviewing documents confirming the triggering event occurred before year end (e.g. announcement of shop closure before year end for redundancy provision).
  • 4For redundancy provisions: agree the number of employees affected to HR records; agree salary details to payroll records; recalculate the provision based on statutory redundancy pay entitlements.
  • 5Review board minutes for evidence of management's intention and supporting estimates.
  • 6Review post-year-end settlements of claims to assess the adequacy of the provision.
  • 7Assess management's judgement and compare the provision estimate to the auditor's independent calculation.

10b. Bank Loans

  • 1Obtain the bank confirmation letter — confirm loan balance, interest rate, repayment terms and any covenants.
  • 2Agree balance to the loan agreement / trust deed.
  • 3Confirm correct split between current and non-current portions (amounts due within and beyond 12 months).
  • 4Recalculate interest charge for the year and agree accrued interest at year end.
  • 5Review loan covenants and assess whether they have been complied with (non-compliance could be a going concern indicator).
Exam Question (Kaplan: Dashing Co – Redundancy Provision)

Describe substantive procedures for the redundancy provision at the year end.

Show model answer
  • Confirm that the provision meets IAS 37 criteria: review board minutes and staff communications to confirm the formal plan was approved and announced before the year end, giving rise to a constructive obligation.
  • Obtain a schedule of employees included in the provision — agree the number of employees to HR records and redundancy notices.
  • Agree salary details of employees to payroll records to confirm the input data to the provision calculation is accurate.
  • Recalculate the provision independently using statutory redundancy pay entitlements (or contractual terms if more generous) and compare to management's figure.
  • Review post-year-end events — have any redundancy payments been made? If so, compare to the provision amount.
  • Review any correspondence with employees' legal representatives regarding the terms of redundancy.
  • Review management accounts produced after year end to assess whether the actual redundancy costs are tracking to the provision.

11. Share Capital, Reserves & Directors' Remuneration

EQT

Share Capital – Rights, Valuation, Completeness

11a. Issue of Share Capital

  • 1Obtain a copy of the board resolution authorising the share issue and confirm it was properly approved.
  • 2Agree proceeds received to bank statements and cash receipts.
  • 3Confirm the correct split between nominal value (share capital) and premium (share premium account).
  • 4Agree details of the new shares to the statutory register of members and the company's Articles of Association.
  • 5Confirm disclosures in the financial statements are complete and correct (number of shares issued, authorised share capital, share premium).

11b. Directors' Remuneration

  • 1Agree total directors' remuneration to the payroll records for the year.
  • 2Confirm individual emoluments to service contracts and board resolutions authorising pay.
  • 3Confirm disclosures meet legal requirements (highest paid director, aggregate emoluments).
  • 4Agree pension contributions to pension scheme documentation.

12. Revenue & Substantive Analytical Procedures

REV

Revenue – Occurrence, Completeness, Accuracy, Cut-Off

Know the Difference
Substantive analytical procedures = ratios, comparisons, proof in total. They cannot test classification. Tests of detail = tracing individual transactions. The examiner tests whether you know which is which.

Substantive Analytical Procedures for Revenue

ProcedureAssertion TestedWhat to Look For
Compare monthly/quarterly revenue to prior year and budget; investigate variances > materiality threshold Occurrence, Completeness Unexplained spikes (fictitious sales?) or drops (missing revenue?)
Calculate gross profit margin % and compare to prior year and industry benchmarks Accuracy, Occurrence Margin increase may signal revenue overstatement; margin fall may indicate cost mis-allocation
Calculate receivables days and compare to prior year Occurrence, Valuation Large increase may indicate fictitious revenue or uncollectible debts
Perform a "proof in total" using non-financial data (rooms × occupancy × rate; units × price) Completeness, Accuracy Large difference between expected and recorded revenue requires investigation
Compare revenue per business segment / product line to prior year Completeness, Occurrence Unexplained decline in a segment could indicate omitted transactions
Exam Technique

When the question says "SUBSTANTIVE ANALYTICAL procedures only"

  • Only write ratio/comparison/proof-in-total procedures.
  • Do NOT write "inspect sales invoices", "trace to despatch notes" — those are tests of detail.
  • A good analytical procedure answer states: (1) what to calculate, (2) what to compare it to, (3) what the result would indicate.
  • Example: "Calculate gross margin % for the current year and compare to the prior year figure. A significant increase may indicate revenue is overstated or cost of sales is understated."

13. Accounting Estimates

Accounting estimates are inherently uncertain. ISA 540 deals with the audit of estimates. Common examples: bad debt allowance, depreciation, warranty provisions, inventory NRV write-downs, fair value of investments.

Problems with Estimates

  • 1Management bias — estimates may be biased to achieve a profit target or debt covenant compliance. Auditors must look for indicators of management bias in the assumptions used.
  • 2Uncertainty range — a wide range of acceptable outcomes makes it hard to determine if the recorded estimate is reasonable.
  • 3Complexity — complex valuation models are hard to audit without specialist expertise.

Three Approaches for Auditing Estimates

ApproachHow
Review subsequent events Look at what actually happened after the year end — if a provision was for $500k and the case settled for $800k, the provision is understated.
Develop an independent estimate Auditor makes their own estimate using the same inputs and compares to management's figure. Significant differences require investigation.
Test management's assumptions Audit the underlying data and assumptions used by management — challenge key inputs, assess reasonableness of assumptions.

14. Exam Technique Master Guide

The Golden Formula for Every Procedure Answer

A well-structured substantive procedure contains:

  • Verb – Obtain / Agree / Inspect / Recalculate / Confirm / Compare / Review
  • Document/source – aged receivables listing, bank confirmation, NCAR, legal correspondence
  • To what – the trial balance, the supporting invoice, prior year figure
  • Purpose / what to look for – to confirm existence, to identify unrecorded liabilities, to confirm misstatement if different

Example: "Agree the balance confirmed in the bank letter [verb + document] to the balance per the bank reconciliation [to what] to confirm the accuracy of the reconciliation opening balance [purpose]."

Key Verbs and What They Mean

VerbMeaning in an Audit Context
ObtainGet hold of a document (obtain the bank reconciliation, obtain the aged receivables listing)
Agree / TraceMatch one document to another (agree invoice amount to the bank payment)
InspectRead and examine a document closely (inspect the loan agreement for repayment terms)
RecalculateRedo the maths independently (recalculate depreciation charge)
ConfirmGet written external confirmation (confirm bank balance with the bank; confirm legal claim with lawyers)
ReviewCritically examine for reasonableness (review aged receivables report for overdue balances)
CompareAnalytical — set two figures or ratios side by side and investigate differences
EnquireAsk management or staff a question (enquire of management as to reasons for the fall in payables days)
ObserveWatch a process happen (observe the inventory count)

Common Ways Marks Are Lost – and How to Avoid Them

❌ Too vague
"Review the receivables balance." This scores zero. You must say what document, what to compare it to, and what you're looking for.
❌ Repeating the same point
"Inspect invoices" then "check invoices" then "look at invoices" = 1 mark not 3. Each bullet must be a genuinely different procedure.
❌ Wrong type of procedure
Answering a "substantive analytical" question with tests of detail. Read the requirement word for word.
❌ Ignoring the scenario
The scenario contains specific risk flags (e.g., a related valuer, a known fraud risk, a collapsing product line). Your procedures should address the specific risk, not just generic ones.
✓ Scenario-specific procedures
Use the facts given. If Cookit Co's Remy Gusteau products have no demand, explicitly test NRV by reviewing post-year-end sales (or lack of) for those specific items.
✓ Explain what you expect to find
End each procedure with what a problem looks like: "…a difference would indicate a possible unrecorded liability." This shows you understand the audit objective.

Quick-Reference Procedure Bank

The Six Most Reliable Audit Procedures (by source)
  1. External confirmation – bank letter, solicitor's letter, trade receivable circularisation
  2. Physical inspection – inventory count attendance, inspecting tangible assets
  3. Recalculation – depreciation proof in total, payroll calculation, redundancy provision
  4. Subsequent events review – post-year-end receipts, settlements, bank clearances
  5. Third-party documents – supplier invoices, title deeds, loan agreements
  6. Analytical procedures – ratio analysis, trend analysis, proof in total
Time Allocation Tip

Section B: Managing your time on procedure questions

  • Each procedure question typically earns 1 mark per valid, different point. A 5-mark question requires 5–6 well-developed points.
  • Aim for one sentence per bullet: verb + document + comparison + what it shows.
  • Spend ~1.8 minutes per mark. A 5-mark question = ~9 minutes max.
  • If stuck: think through OEVRCA (Occurrence, Existence, Valuation, Rights, Completeness, Accuracy) — what could go wrong with this balance?
